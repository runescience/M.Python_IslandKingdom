Kingdom Compendium: The Known World

Every kingdom in the campaign world, drawn from real-world cultural analogs and filtered through 300 years of isolation and the Great Lie.

The Alliance Territories

Earth 2 / Akamara Alliance

Analog: The AmericasGrid: 03,03Pop: 80mOwner: All kingdoms (shared capital)

The grand capital of the Alliance, owned collectively by all ten allied kingdoms. Draws from the broad spirit of the Americas: a melting pot of ambition, frontier energy, and democratic idealism, tempered by the weight of being the world's administrative heart.

  • Culture: Cosmopolitan and transactional. Every language, guild, and political faction has a foothold here. Akamara is where deals are made and broken.
  • Governance: A council of representatives from all ten Allied kingdoms — perpetually gridlocked and perpetually negotiated.
  • Military: Home to the Alliance's largest naval fleet, including warships like the Nashua. The most heavily fortified harbor in the known world.
  • Atmosphere: Grand, loud, and crawling with royal inquisitors, spies, and merchants. Opportunity and surveillance in equal measure.

Blake World / Liege Alliance

Analog: United KingdomGrid: 03,04Pop: 300mMoons: 7

The most powerful sovereign duchy in the Alliance — technically a member, but operating with immense political independence. Constitutional monarchy, legendary navy, global trading empire, and an unshakeable belief in its own exceptionalism. Destination of the players' diplomatic pouch.

  • Culture: Formal, hierarchical, deeply proud of tradition. Values order, contract law, and institutional rule. They invented half the Alliance's legal framework.
  • Naval Power: Second only to Akamara in fleet size, arguably superior in seamanship. Liege captains are considered the finest navigators in the known world.
  • Trade: Merchant houses operate across every Allied port. Their currency is the most stable in the Alliance.
  • Atmosphere: Stately and fog-shrouded. Fortified harbors, stone architecture, a bureaucracy that runs like clockwork — or claims to.

Manchu Empire Alliance

Analog: China (Manchurian/Qing dynasty)Grid: 04,03Pop: 600mMoons: 1

The most populous kingdom in the Alliance by a vast margin. A sleeping giant — enormous in population and resource, slow to mobilize, deeply conservative in its institutions. Drawing from the Qing dynasty's legacy of centralized imperial administration and sophisticated bureaucracy.

  • Culture: Confucian in spirit — hierarchical, scholarly, invested in social harmony and filial duty. Art, philosophy, and engineering are prized above military glory.
  • Governance: An imperial court with a vast civil service selected by examination. Decisions move slowly but are implemented with total thoroughness.
  • Economy: The largest internal market in the Alliance. They need little from others, which gives them enormous leverage.
  • Atmosphere: Ancient, layered, self-sufficient. Outsiders are tolerated as guests, never equals.

Sinochia Alliance

Analog: China (southern coastal / trading diaspora)Grid: 03,05Pop: 600mMoons: 4

Where Manchu represents imperial inland China, Sinochia is its coastal mercantile counterpart — the China of Guangzhou and Fujian. Its waters are permanently shrouded in supernatural fog and treacherous reef lines, making it the best hiding place in the known world for ships fleeing pursuit.

  • Culture: Entrepreneurial, clan-based, intensely practical. Traders, shipbuilders, and navigators. Family loyalty and commercial networks are the twin pillars of society.
  • Resources: Rich in undiscovered raw material caches and ancient processing infrastructure from the old world.
  • Atmosphere: Fog-wrapped and labyrinthine. Locals know every current; outsiders get lost and die.
  • Strategic Value: The fog makes Sinochia's waters the best place to hide from a pursuing fleet.

New Portigua Alliance

Analog: Portugal / Spain (PortoSpania)Grid: 04,04Pop: 30mMoons: 9

Small in population but enormous in reach. The great Age-of-Exploration kingdom of the Alliance — nine planets and moons, more than almost any other kingdom. Punches far above its weight through sheer navigational daring and a sprawling network of colonial outposts.

  • Culture: Adventurous, devout, intensely proud of their exploratory heritage. New Portiguan sailors are the most widely traveled people in the Alliance. They have charts no one else has seen.
  • Governance: Constitutional monarchy with a powerful merchant-noble class. The crown funds exploration; the merchants fund the crown.
  • Economy: Trade routes, colonial extraction, and the sale of navigational knowledge. Their cartographers are worth their weight in gold.
  • Atmosphere: Sun-bleached harbor towns, tiled plazas, salt and spice. Warm, proud, and quietly aware they know more about the world than they let on.

NewFoundland / Akira Alliance

Analog: Canada / Japan (hybrid frontier)Grid: 06,05Pop: 200mMoons: 3

A cultural hybrid of Canada's vast resource-rich wilderness and Japan's disciplined precision engineering. Combines rugged self-reliance with extraordinary technical sophistication. They build the best ships in the Alliance and harvest resources from environments no one else can survive in.

  • Culture: Stoic, industrious, deeply communal. Individual glory is subordinated to collective excellence. Craftsmanship is a spiritual practice.
  • Industry: Shipbuilding, precision engineering, and resource extraction from extreme environments.
  • Governance: Hybrid of parliamentary democracy and clan-council tradition. Decisions are slow but rarely reversed.
  • Atmosphere: Cold, vast, quietly magnificent. Enormous forests, deep harbors, workshops where master craftspeople work in near-silence.

Tokyoshima Alliance

Analog: Japan (metropolitan core)Grid: 06,07Pop: 80mMoons: 0

The urban, cultural, and commercial heart of Japan's presence in the Alliance. Where NewFoundland/Akira represents Japan's frontier engineering spirit, Tokyoshima is its aesthetic and mercantile soul: art, ceremony, financial markets, and razor-sharp social hierarchy.

  • Culture: Intensely refined and protocol-driven. Every interaction has a correct form. Outsiders who learn the forms are welcomed; those who ignore them are frozen out permanently.
  • Economy: A financial and trading hub. Their merchant banks hold debt from half the Alliance.
  • Governance: Constitutional monarchy with a powerful shogunate-style administrative class beneath the imperial surface.
  • Atmosphere: Immaculate, layered, beautiful. Lantern-lit harbor districts and the constant low hum of commerce conducted with perfect composure.

Dark Sun / Dark World Alliance

Analog: Japan (shadow / outcast culture)Grid: 06,06Pop: 20mMoons: 0

The shadow twin of Tokyoshima — a small, officially unacknowledged territory that handles everything the gleaming city-state cannot be seen to touch. Drawing from Japan's historical tradition of shadow governance and outcast communities. Tokyoshima pretends it doesn't exist. Everyone uses it.

  • Culture: Pragmatic, secretive, governed by a strict code of its own — older and more binding than Alliance law. Oaths made here are kept absolutely.
  • Economy: Black market goods, intelligence brokering, contract work, and financial transactions that cannot appear on any official ledger.
  • Governance: Nominally none. In practice, run by a council of guild masters whose identities are never publicly confirmed.
  • Atmosphere: Perpetually dim, fog-wrapped, and surprisingly orderly. Violence is rare because everyone here has too much to lose.

Ruskaya Alliance

Analog: RussiaGrid: 07,06Pop: 100mMoons: 3

The great northern giant of the Alliance — vast, cold, and possessed of a cultural depth and resilience that outsiders perpetually underestimate. An ally you want and an enemy you never want to make.

  • Culture: Communal, fatalistic, fiercely proud. Ruskayans believe suffering builds character and that their kingdom has been tested by history in ways no other has. They are probably right.
  • Military: Enormous standing army and a navy built for endurance over speed. Ruskayan ships survive conditions that sink everyone else.
  • Economy: Timber, furs, grain, and mineral wealth from territories so vast they have never been fully mapped. Self-sufficient enough to be diplomatically independent.
  • Atmosphere: Vast, cold, beautiful in a way that feels indifferent to human comfort. Fortified port cities, enormous feasting halls, and a hospitality that is absolute once you are inside.

EuroPublic: M/Mont Blanc & M/Ebon Towers Alliance

Analog: European Union (continental Europe)Grid: 05,05Pop: 60m eachMoons: 0

The Alliance's two great administrative moon-colonies — small in population but enormous in institutional influence. Drawing from the EU tradition of multilateral governance and civil law, these are where the Alliance's legal framework, trade agreements, and diplomatic protocols are actually written and maintained.

  • Culture: Technocratic, multilingual, obsessed with process. The EuroPublic believes the right committee, given enough time, can solve any problem.
  • Governance: A rotating council of appointed commissioners representing all Allied kingdoms. Produces an extraordinary volume of binding regulation.
  • Economy: No independent resource base — entirely dependent on Alliance trade and administrative fees. Politically neutral, financially vulnerable.
  • Atmosphere: Clean, orderly, slightly sterile. Grand meeting halls and the quiet hum of bureaucrats who genuinely believe paperwork saves lives.

The Independent Reaches (Neutral Kingdoms)

Bartog Neutral

Grid: 05,04Pop: 5mMoons: 1Stations: 4Mining: 6

A hardscrabble buffer-zone kingdom wedged between the Alliance and Axis frontlines. Survives by selling neutrality to everyone and loyalty to no one. Draws from the tradition of small, fiercely independent trading republics — the Hanseatic League, the Swiss Confederation — that profit from the wars of larger powers without being consumed by them.

  • Culture: Pragmatic, mercantile, deeply suspicious of idealism. Bartog has watched too many great causes burn through their territory.
  • Economy: Transit fees, mercenary contracts, black-market arbitrage, and six active mining stations that sell to whoever pays.
  • Atmosphere: Rough, functional, and surprisingly well-armed. Every merchant has a weapon. Every tavern has a back door. The streets are safe because everyone is too busy making money to start trouble.

Sender Neutral

Grid: 05,06Pop: 14mMoons: 5Stations: 4Mining: 3

The most volatile of the neutral kingdoms — sitting directly between the Axis slave-fleet lanes and the Alliance patrol routes. Has the resources to be a real power but lacks the political will to commit to either side. Draws from the tradition of contested border states that survive by being useful to everyone.

  • Culture: Tense, adaptive, deeply fatalistic. Sender's people read the political weather better than anyone. They change allegiances the way others change clothes — not from cowardice, but from survival instinct honed over generations.
  • Economy: Privateering licenses, refugee processing fees, intelligence sales, and five planetary holdings that maintain genuine independence.
  • Atmosphere: Volatile buffer-zone energy. The best information in the known world is available here, for a price. So is the worst trouble.

Dagnarius Neutral

Grid: 06,03Pop: 4mMoons: 4Stations: 12Mining: 2

Tiny in population but possessing twelve orbital stations — the most of any kingdom in the known world. Draws from the tradition of great free-trading city-states like Genoa or Amsterdam. Where ships refuel, cargoes change hands, and no one asks too many questions.

  • Culture: Libertarian, cosmopolitan, obsessively focused on the freedom of commerce. No state religion, no mandatory military service, no extradition treaties.
  • Economy: Transit, storage, refueling, and financial services requiring absolute discretion. Acme Corporation almost certainly has agents here.
  • Atmosphere: Busy, anonymous, and surprisingly pleasant. No one cares who you are. Everyone cares whether you can pay.

Xosha Declarative Neutral

Analog: Xhosa / South AfricaGrid: 06,04Pop: 25mMoons: 2Stations: 1Mining: 1

The most principled of the neutral kingdoms — a state that chose independence not from pragmatism but from a deep philosophical commitment to self-determination. Drawing from the Xhosa tradition of oral law, communal governance, and the fierce dignity of a people who have resisted absorption by larger powers for centuries. Neutral because it refuses to be owned.

  • Culture: Deeply communal, orally sophisticated, governed by consensus decision-making. Elders hold real authority. Storytelling is a political act.
  • Governance: A council of clan elders. The Declarative's name comes from its founding document — a formal declaration of permanent non-alignment signed by every clan chief.
  • Atmosphere: Warm, proud, and quietly watchful. Visitors are welcomed with genuine hospitality and observed with careful intelligence. The Xosha know more about the neutral zone than anyone else, and share it selectively.

The Axis Slave Reaches

The three Axis kingdoms take no prisoners — only slaves. Populations are listed as unreported because they do not distinguish between citizens and captives in their census records. All three maintain massive armadas and attack any ship flying Alliance colors on sight.

Cush Axis

Analog: Ancient Nubia / Kingdom of KushGrid: 03,07Pop: UnreportedMoons: 2Stations: 7Mining: 3

Draws from the ancient Kingdom of Kush — the great Nubian civilization that rivaled and at times conquered Egypt, known for its warrior culture, iron-smelting, and fierce independence. The oldest of the three Axis powers and the most militarily sophisticated. Their iron-hulled warships are the most feared vessels on the Axis frontline.

  • Culture: Martial, hierarchical, built around a warrior-priest caste that controls both military and religious authority. Strength is the only currency that matters.
  • Military: The most disciplined Axis fleet. Heavily armored, slow, nearly impossible to sink. Their boarding parties are legendary for their ferocity.
  • Slave Economy: Uses enslaved labor for mining and construction on a scale that dwarfs the other Axis kingdoms. Their seven stations are built and maintained entirely by captive labor.
  • Atmosphere: Monumental and terrifying. Enormous iron fortresses, war-drums that carry across the water, a military culture that has been at war so long it no longer remembers peace.

Pote Axis

Analog: Ancient Carthage / PhoeniciaGrid: 04,06Pop: UnreportedMoons: 1Stations: 7Mining: 3

Draws from the Carthaginian and Phoenician tradition — a maritime mercantile empire built on trade, naval supremacy, and a ruthless willingness to do what other civilizations considered unthinkable. Has taken that energy and weaponized it entirely toward slave-trading and resource extraction.

  • Culture: Mercantile at its core, but the commodity is people. Views every interaction as a transaction and every person as a potential asset or liability.
  • Naval Power: Fast, maneuverable raiding fleets designed for interception and capture rather than destruction. Dead slaves have no value.
  • Economy: The Axis slave trade's primary broker. Pote processes, categorizes, and distributes captives to the other Axis kingdoms and black-market buyers in the neutral zone.
  • Atmosphere: Coldly efficient and deeply unsettling. Their ports are immaculate. Their records are meticulous. The horror is in the normalcy of it all.

Qenit Axis

Analog: Ancient EgyptGrid: 03,08Pop: UnreportedMoons: 1Stations: 7Mining: 3

Draws from ancient Egypt — the god-king pharaonic tradition, monumental building culture, and a belief in divine right so absolute that the ruler is literally considered a living deity. Qenit's pharaoh-class rulers have used that theological framework to justify a slave economy of staggering scale. Every monument, every station, every warship is built by captive hands.

  • Culture: Theocratic and monumental. The ruling class genuinely believes they are divine intermediaries between the mortal world and cosmic order. This is sincere, which makes it more dangerous than propaganda.
  • Architecture: Builds on a scale that defies comprehension. Their orbital stations are the largest structures in the known world, built to last ten thousand years.
  • Military: Enormous but slow-moving. Relies on overwhelming force rather than tactical sophistication. They do not need to be clever when they can simply bring more ships.
  • Atmosphere: Awe-inspiring and suffocating. Gold, stone, and the absolute silence of a society where questioning the divine order is not merely illegal — it is literally inconceivable to most of its inhabitants.
