================================================================================
PART 2 — PLAYER SCENE: THE EVENING BEFORE
Three Stops. The Mersling. What the City Knows.
================================================================================

You have until first light.

You use the afternoon.

Not to rest. To move. To see who moves with you.

You know this city well enough to have three places you go when you are
in Liege — not the harbor district, not the places a sailor goes, but
the places you have been going since before you were old enough to know
why they mattered. You are going to visit all three tonight. You are
going to take the long way between each one. You are going to double
back twice, stop once for no reason, and see who is still behind you
when you arrive.

If someone is following you, they will have to work for it.

If no one is following you, you will know that too.

You tell your crew to stay with the vessel. You tell Bramble specifically
to watch the lines — not the dock, the lines. He nods. He understands
what you mean.

Then you go.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STOP ONE: FARNSWORTH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Farnsworth lives in the same house he has always lived in, three streets
back from the harbor district in a neighborhood that is neither wealthy
nor poor — the kind of neighborhood that has been exactly what it is for
a hundred years and intends to remain so. The house has a blue door. It
has always had a blue door. You have never asked why.

You take the long way. You cross the fish market twice — once going, once
coming back on yourself through the stall row. You stop at a corner for
ninety seconds and look at nothing in particular. You count the people
who pass. You note the ones who slow.

Nobody slows.

You knock on the blue door.

Farnsworth answers it the way he always answers it — as if he was already
on his way to the door before you knocked. He is your father's age, or
close to it. Heavy through the shoulders, gone grey, with the particular
stillness of a man who has seen enough of the world to stop being surprised
by it. He was a factor for thirty years. He knows everyone in Liege who
is worth knowing and several people who are not.

He looks at you. He looks past you at the street.

FARNSWORTH: "You're in port."

You tell him you are leaving in the morning.

FARNSWORTH: "Then you'd better come in."

He feeds you. He always feeds you. Something from a pot that has been
on the fire since this morning — thick, dark, with bread that is two
days old and better for it. You eat. He watches you eat the way people
watch you when they are deciding how much to say.

You ask him what he has heard.

He is quiet for a moment.

FARNSWORTH: "The fog around Sinochia has been moving. Liege navigators
are saying the margin shifted — approach lines that were reliable last
season are inside the fog now. Two, three days of distance in some
corridors. That is not weather. Fog does not move like that."

He refills your cup without asking.

FARNSWORTH: "And there is a retired admiral who has been meeting with
a Dagnarius factor. Three times this week. In the same place, which
means either he is not worried about being seen or he wants someone
to see him. I do not know which."

He looks at you steadily.

FARNSWORTH: "You are going north."

It is not a question. You do not confirm it. He does not need you to.

FARNSWORTH: "Be careful in Dagnarius. Someone is paying triple rate
for discreet couriers there. No questions about the cargo. That kind
of money means the cargo is something someone does not want traced."

He walks you to the door.

FARNSWORTH: "Come back when you can. Your father would want to know
you are still moving."

You step back into the street. You stand still for thirty seconds.
Nobody is waiting for you.

You take the long way to the second stop.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STOP TWO: CHEWZWIKI
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Chewzwiki's shop is in the old quarter, wedged between a cobbler and
a place that sells dried herbs and has been selling dried herbs since
before you were born. The sign above the door is hand-lettered and
has been repainted so many times the original text is anyone's guess.
Inside: shelves from floor to ceiling, organized in a system that makes
sense only to Chewzwiki, lit by three lamps that are never all working
at the same time.

You have been buying and selling here since you were young enough that
Chewzwiki used to let you sit on the counter. Small things. Curiosities.
Objects that had no obvious value and therefore had whatever value you
could argue for. It was the best education in commerce you ever received.

You double back once on the way — cut down an alley, come out two
streets over, walk a block north before turning south again. You stop
at a lamp post and check your boot. You are not checking your boot.
You are watching the reflection in a shop window across the street.

Nobody is there.

You push open the door. The bell above it rings the same note it has
always rung.

Chewzwiki is behind the counter, examining something small through a
lens. He does not look up immediately. This is normal. He finishes
what he is doing, sets the lens down, and looks at you with the
expression of a man who has been expecting you for approximately
the last hour.

CHEWZWIKI: "You look like you need something."

You tell him you are looking for anything interesting that has come
through recently.

He considers this.

CHEWZWIKI: "A Pote vessel docked three nights ago under a false flag.
I know because the cargo that came off it ended up in the market and
the provenance was wrong — the stamps were Dagnarius but the goods
were not Dagnarius goods. Someone is moving things through Liege
that they do not want traced back to the Axis."

He sets the small object he was examining on the counter between you.
It is a seal stamp. Small, bronze, worn smooth on the edges.

CHEWZWIKI: "Someone dropped this near the old dock. I found it two
days ago. I do not know what it opens or what it was used to seal.
But the mark on it is not any guild or house I recognize."

You look at the stamp. The mark is a circle with a line through it
and a small glyph in the lower quadrant. You have seen this mark
before. Not recently. Not in a context you can immediately place.
But you have seen it.

CHEWZWIKI: "Take it if you want. I have no use for it and I do not
want it in the shop."

You pocket the stamp.

You buy a small thing — a brass compass rose, decorative, no practical
use, the kind of thing you have been buying from him since you were
twelve. He wraps it in paper without being asked. The price is what
it has always been: slightly too much, and worth it.

You leave. You stand outside for a moment. The street is quiet.

You go to the third stop.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STOP THREE: CHARLES DARING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Charles Daring keeps a room above a cartographer's supply shop in the
professional quarter — the part of Liege where the people who make
things for sailors live, as opposed to the part where sailors live.
Instrument makers, chart printers, compass grinders. The streets here
smell of ink and metal shavings.

You met Daring a couple of years ago in Akamara. You put in as a crewman
on his survey vessel for a season — trawling islands, taking soundings,
arguing about notation conventions at anchor in the evenings. He is
meticulous, opinionated, and one of the best working cartographers in
the Alliance. He is also the kind of man who notices things that other
people do not notice, because his entire profession is built on the
difference between what is there and what the chart says is there.

You take the most direct route to his building. You have been careful
twice already. If someone is still following you after two careful
routes, a third careful route will not help. What you want now is to
arrive somewhere familiar and see if anyone arrives near you.

Nobody does.

You knock. He opens the door with ink on his hands, which means he
was working, which means he is always working.

DARING: "I heard you were in port."

You ask him how he heard.

DARING: "Harbor registry. I check it every morning. Old habit."

He lets you in. The room is what it always is — charts everywhere,
a drafting table that takes up a third of the floor, reference books
stacked in columns that have their own internal logic. He clears a
chair for you by moving a rolled chart to the floor with the casual
disregard of a man who has too many charts to be precious about any
of them.

You ask him what he is working on.

He pauses. Just slightly.

DARING: "A commission. Updating the Sinochia margin charts. Standard
work — the fog shifts, the charts need updating."

He says it the way you say something that is true but not complete.

You ask him about the fog shift.

DARING: "The margin has moved — two, maybe three days of sailing distance
in the northern corridors, confirmed by three independent navigator
reports. That is not drift. That is movement with a direction."

He looks at the chart on his drafting table. You can see the fog
margin marked in grey wash, with a dotted line showing the previous
boundary.

DARING: "The interesting thing is not that it moved. Fog moves.
The interesting thing is what it moved away from."

He taps a point on the chart. You lean forward to look.

It is a point in the interior of the fog margin. No label. No
coordinates. Just a small mark in a different ink than the rest
of the chart — older, darker, made by a different hand.

DARING: "That mark was on the original survey. Pre-Alliance. I
have no idea what it indicates. But the fog moved away from it.
Which means either the fog is responding to something at that
location, or something at that location is pushing the fog."

He rolls the chart up before you can look at it longer.

DARING: "I am not going to ask what you are doing in Liege or
where you are going next. But if you end up near the Sinochia
margin — and I suspect you might — I would be interested in
what you find."

He hands you a small folded paper. A copy of the margin chart,
hand-drawn, the pre-Alliance mark included.

DARING: "Consider it a professional courtesy. We trawled enough
islands together that I think you owe me a report."

You take the chart. You shake his hand. You leave.

Outside, the professional quarter is quiet. Lamp-lit. The smell
of ink and metal shavings follows you half a block before the
harbor smell takes over again.

Nobody has been following you all evening.

Either you are clean, or whoever is watching you is better than
you are.

You decide to assume the former and remain alert for the latter.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MERSLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Mersling is a good ways from the port — far enough that harbor
workers do not drink here, close enough that the city's middle layer
does: factors, clerks, off-duty customs officers, the kind of merchants
who have been in Liege long enough to have a regular table. It is not
a place where strangers are unwelcome. It is a place where strangers
are noticed.

You have been coming here since your apprenticeship days. The barkeep
knows your face. He does not know your name, which is exactly the
relationship you want with a barkeep in a city you visit irregularly.

You take a table near the back wall. Not the corner — the corner is
obvious. The back wall, slightly left of center, with a sightline to
the door and the bar and the table near the window where the regulars
sit. You order something dark and local and nurse it.

You listen.


WHAT THE MERSLING KNOWS TONIGHT:

The table near the window has three men who have been here since before
you arrived. They are factors or clerks — the kind of men who talk
about work because work is what they have. You catch pieces.

--- FIRST THREAD ---

One of them, the heavyset one with the good coat, is talking about
a naval captain who came back from patrol with three days missing
from his log. Confined to quarters. The other two know the story
already — they are nodding, adding details. The captain's name is
not mentioned. The patrol route is not mentioned. But the heavyset
man says: *the fog margin, obviously, where else do you lose three
days and come back not wanting to talk about it.*

The other two agree. One of them says the crown is buying back
debt from Tokyoshima merchant banks. The third says that means
something is being prepared, and the first two agree, and then
they lower their voices and you lose the thread.

--- SECOND THREAD ---

A woman at the bar, alone, drinking efficiently. She has the look
of someone who has been traveling — road dust on her boots, a bag
that has been packed and unpacked many times. She is not from Liege.
Her accent, when she orders a second drink, is New Portiguan.

She is not talking to anyone. But she is listening to everyone.
You recognize the posture because it is your posture.

You make a note of her and do not approach.

--- THIRD THREAD ---

Two men come in late — after the second hour of your stay. They
sit near the door, which is the table you did not take. They order
food, not drink, which means they are here to be somewhere, not
to drink. They talk quietly. You catch one phrase clearly because
the room goes briefly quiet at the right moment:

*— the old dock, but nobody goes down there —*

And then the room noise comes back up and you lose it.

You sit with that for a moment.

The old dock. The far pier. The join between old stone and new stone.

You are not the only person in Liege thinking about what is under
the old dock tonight.

--- FOURTH THREAD ---

The barkeep, when he comes to check your cup, leans slightly and
says — not quietly, just conversationally, the way barkeeps say
things they want you to hear without making it a thing:

"Liege intelligence is paying double rate for information on Acme
Corporation's shipping routes. Posted three days ago. Lot of
interest."

He moves on without waiting for a response.

You sit with that too.

Acme's shipping routes. Liege intelligence wants them. Which means
Liege intelligence does not have them. Which means Acme has been
moving things through these waters in ways that Liege — with the
finest navy in the Alliance and a harbor registry that logs every
vessel — cannot account for.

The Pote vessel under a false flag that Chewzwiki mentioned.
The cargo with wrong provenance.
The seal stamp in your pocket with the mark you almost recognize.

You finish your drink.

You leave a fair coin on the table — not generous enough to be
memorable, not mean enough to be remembered for the wrong reason.

You walk back toward the harbor.

The city is quieter now. The lamp-lighters have been through.
The fish market is dark. The pastry stand is dark. The customs
house has one light in an upper window.

You pass the Naval Registry. You do not look at it.

You pass the Anchor & Compass. The door is closed. The broadsheet
man is not on the wall outside. You note his absence the same way
you noted his presence.

You reach the harbor entrance arch. The night officer logs you
through without comment.

You walk the dock road to the far pier.

Your vessel is where you left it. Bramble is on deck, awake,
watching the lines. He looks at you when you come up the gangplank.
You give him a small nod. He gives you one back.

Everything is where it should be.

You go below. You sleep for four hours.

At first light, you go to Hound's Dream.

DM NOTES — THREADS LEFT OPEN

THE SEAL STAMP (from Chewzwiki):
  The mark — circle, line, lower glyph — is an Acme Corporation stamp.
  Someone dropped it near the old dock recently. The connection between
  Acme and whatever is on the harbor floor beneath the far pier is not
  yet established for the players. Leave unresolved.

THE FOG MOVEMENT (from Farnsworth + Daring):
  The Sinochia fog margin shifted two to three days of sailing distance
  in the northern corridors over the last month — the same six-week
  window when Fenn's approach corridor reopened and Acme's fixers
  arrived in Liege. The fog moved away from the pre-Alliance mark on
  Daring's chart. Cause unknown. Players do not know this yet.

THE PRE-ALLIANCE MARK ON DARING'S CHART:
  Daring's copy of the margin chart shows the same type of mark as
  the Emeritus Colony databanks. The players now have a hand-drawn
  chart with the location marked. This is the approach vector for
  the next leg.

THE TWO MEN AT THE MERSLING:
  "The old dock, but nobody goes down there." Identity unknown.
  Could be Liege intelligence. Could be Acme. Could be nothing.
  Leave unresolved.

THE NEW PORTIGUAN WOMAN AT THE BAR:
  Listening posture. Traveling gear. Not from Liege. No further
  information. Plant and leave. She may reappear in Dagnarius.

THE RETIRED ADMIRAL + DAGNARIUS FACTOR (from Farnsworth):
  Per the Liege rumor table (d8, result 8): a retired Liege admiral
  meeting privately with a Dagnarius factor, three times this week.
  Cross-reference: Fenn is a retired naval officer. Fenn has been
  in Liege for three months. The admiral is not Fenn — Fenn is a
  commodore, not an admiral — but the meetings may be connected to
  the same Foundation network. Or they may be something else entirely.
