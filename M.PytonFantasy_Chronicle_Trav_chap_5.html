Campaign Master Chronicle: Chapter 5

🧭 Chapter 5: Return to Known Space (The Amaran Reach)

The players cross back over the frontier boundary from the wild, uncharted southern oceans, sailing directly into the known waters of the Alliance of 13 Kingdoms. They cross onto the mapped regional grid near Tokyo / Tokyoshima (06,07), Shima (06,08), or the volatile independent buffer waters of Sender (05,06).

Their ultimate destination is Liege (03,04)—a massive, sovereign duchy nestled deep within the western Alliance core. They originally set sail from Akamara (03,03), the grand, high-ranking capital of the Alliance. To deliver the mysterious diplomatic pouch to Liege, they must chart a course straight through a hornet's nest of political conspiracy, strict military blockades, and a terrifying Axis frontline.

🛡️ 1. The Uniform Question: To Wear or to Hide?

Whether they are still wearing the high-quality uniforms scavenged from the vacant island is an incredible tactical dilemma for your players:

  • If they wear them: The master chronicle noted that these uniforms bear a country symbol entirely unrecognized by any history book in the 13 Kingdoms. If they sail into an Alliance port or get boarded by an Alliance patrol wearing these clothes, they will immediately be flagged as foreign spies, smugglers, or agents of the "Big Lie."
  • The Smuggler's Choice: They will likely need to hide the uniforms under standard cloaks or travel gear to move unnoticed, or wear them proudly if they are ready to announce themselves as the Vanguard of Admiral Hall's new fleet.

🗺️ 2. Mapping the Charted Waters: The Fantasy Fleet Translation

Using your custom layout coordinates, we can chart exactly what this journey looks like across your 80% global ocean:

🟢 The Alliance Territories (The Core Alliance Reaches)

  • Akamara (03,03): The grand capital port. It is the heart of Alliance command, heavily fortified, and crawling with royal inquisitors loyal to the status quo.
  • Liege (03,04): A highly powerful, heavily fortified western duchy. While technically part of the Alliance, it operates with immense political sovereignty. This is where the players must deliver the pouch.
  • Manchu Empire (04,03), New Portigua (04,04), NewFoundland/Akira (06,05), Dark World / Dark Sun (06,06), Tokyo/Tokyoshima (06,07), Shining Crest (06,07), Ruskaya (07,06): Major allied port cities, naval bastions, trading hubs, and naval bases. They are heavily patrolled by Alliance war-galleons like the Nashua. Passing through these waters requires strict transit paperwork and royal seals.

🔴 The Axis Threat & Blockades (The Axis Slave Reaches)

True to your lore, these kingdoms take no prisoners—only slaves. They have pushed deep into the charts, creating a terrifying gauntlet between the players and their destination. They have effectively cut the region in half. If the players try to sail south through these waters, they will face aggressive slave-mongering armadas and raiding fleets.

  • Pote (04,06), Cush (03,07), Qenit (03,08): A massive, aggressive frontline Axis armada. Any ship flying Alliance colors or lacking an Axis slave-trade permit will be attacked on sight, its crew clapped in irons.

🟡 The Frontier Buffer (The Independent Reaches)

These realms maintain strict neutrality, serving as perfect frontier safe-havens, black markets, or places to gather rumors.

  • Dagnarius (06,03) & Xosha Declarative (06,04): Northern independent trading ports where the players can pull in without Alliance paperwork, though they might spot Acme spies lurking in the taverns.
  • Bartog (05,04) & Sender (05,06): Sandwiched directly between the Axis and Alliance lines. These waters are highly volatile "buffer zones" teeming with privateers and mercenaries.

⚪ Barren Resource Spots & Production Hubs (The Wild Resource Reaches)

These are massive, rugged landmasses. They are hostile, barren expanses containing buried, undiscovered, or newly exploited raw materials, alongside ancient structural processing plants left over from the old world ships.

  • Sinochia (03,05), Shima (06,08), and Chart 05,07: Rough, resource-rich landmasses. While highly valuable due to undiscovered caches and production assets, their coastal waters are cursed by permanent supernatural fog and treacherous reef lines, making them perfect maps to hide from pursuing fleets.

⚫ The Ruined Charts (Designated by "R")

  • R242 (03,06), R341 (04,05), R343 (04,07), R344 (04,08), R541 (07,05), R551 (07,07): These are completely abandoned, haunted waters or ruined islands destroyed during the cataclysm 300 years ago. Nobody sails here willingly due to wild magic anomalies or sea monsters.

🗺️ 3. Finding Liege & The Route of Heroics

Depending on where the characters sail, the narrative for Episode 5 perfectly locks into place:

  • The Northern Stealth Approach: If the players choose to navigate around the dense network of Alliance blockades to reach Liege (03,04) or Akamara (03,03), they must face the fact that although this is the "home team," because they possess foreign technology (the mask and potentially the heart matrix) and secret knowledge, their own people are now their greatest threat. They must play a high-stakes game of maritime stealth, using the Barren Resource Reaches or the Ruined Charts to sneak past their own country's navy.

🛡️ Tactical Challenges & Narrative Branches

1. The Uniform Dilemma

The players enter these waters wearing (or carrying) the highly advanced, masterfully tailored uniforms recovered from the vacant island. The insignia on these uniforms belongs to an unrecognized civilization, completely contradicting the history taught in Akamara. If an Alliance patrol vessel boards them and discovers these garments, the crew will immediately be arrested as dangerous political subversives. They must decide whether to stash the uniforms in the lead-lined cold box, disguise them with common traveling cloaks, or fly them proudly to signal Admiral Hall's secret network.

2. Plotting the Gauntlet Run (Three Potential Routes)

To get from the Tokyo/Shima/Sender entry zone to Liege (03,04), the players must choose how to navigate the maps:

  • The Iron Gauntlet (The Axis Breakthrough): Sailing directly west from Sender (05,06) through Pote (04,06). This is the fastest route to Liege, but it requires them to use the 50% speed bonus of the Lignum Vitae mask to outrun and outgun the heavy war-galleons of the Axis slave fleet.
  • The Blind Doldrums (The Storm Route): Nosing the sloop into the treacherous, fog-enshrouded Barren Resource Reaches. These waters are ignored by standard merchant fleets due to dense coastal mists and jagged rocks. The Axis fleets avoid them, but the players will have to deal with the mask's Storm Magnetism, risking lightning strikes on their black powder while navigating purely by the mask's vibrations.
  • The Inquisitor's Screen (The Alliance Route): Hugging the eastern coastlines up through Dark World (06,06) and Newfoundland/Akira (06,05), then cutting west across New Portigua (04,04). While safe from Axis pirates, this route is heavily guarded by Alliance customs patrols. They will face intense boarding inspections, demanding official flight liaison paperwork, transit seals, and an accounting of why their sloop is moving at an impossible 150% speed.

⚓ Tokyoshima Dockside Encounter: The Border Gate

The Setup: The players make landfall back in civilized territory at Tokyoshima (06,07). Because the Alliance aggressively enforces the "Great Lie," all incoming ships are directed through a massive, iron-reinforced sea gate guarded by an Alliance Customs Cutter.

The Complication: As the sloop slides into the inspection slip, Inquisitor Vance boards with four armored Alliance marines. He demands their official flight liaison logs and transit seals from Chapter 1. He immediately notices two massive red flags: the impossible trajectory timeline and the foreign uniforms if they fail to hide them inside the lead-lined cold box.

📜 The Tokyoshima Crisis: Diplomatic Papers & Forbidden Logs

Having their official diplomatic papers gives the players a massive tactical advantage, but it sets up a high-stakes battle of wits. In a gritty 1E style, papers aren't an automatic "get out of jail free" card—they are a tool the players must actively wield. Here is how those diplomatic papers alter the Tokyoshima Dockside Encounter and how you can run it behind the GM screen:

🛡️ How the Papers Help (The Player's Shield)

  • The Legal Armor: Inquisitor Vance cannot simply arrest the characters or impound the sloop on a whim. Valid royal transit seals from a Port Master carry real political weight.
  • The Counter-Interrogation: The players can use the papers to flip the script on Vance. They can demand to know why a ship on an official crown delivery assignment is being aggressively detained and delayed, threatening to report Vance’s obstruction to Admiral Hall or the high lords.
  • Mechanical Bonus: If you are running social checks, presenting these authentic documents grants the players a massive +4 bonus to their Charisma or Fast-Talk checks when managing the border guard.

⚠️ The Catch: The Timeline Trap

While the papers protect them from being treated like standard pirates, they actually draw attention to the campaign's core mechanical mystery:

  • The Impossible Log: Inquisitor Vance’s job is to verify naval logs. When he looks at the stamp from Port City A and compares it to the current calendar date, he will realize the ship covered the distance twice as fast as any vessel should be humanly capable of.
  • The Suspense: Vance will narrow his eyes, tap the papers, and ask: "These seals are authentic, Captain. But unless you discovered an uncharted sea-portal, your sloop would have to fly to get from the frontier to Tokyoshima in ten days. Explain your speed."

🚨 The Logbook Trap: Places That Don't Exist

Looking at the naval logs reveals a much bigger problem. If Inquisitor Vance checks their official logs, he isn’t just looking at an impossible timeline—he is looking at ink-and-quill entries documenting landfalls at Cordoba, Emeritus, and Tarchoo. According to the Alliance history books, these places do not exist, and the waters they sit in are supposed to be an empty, post-cataclysmic void. By simply turning in their actual logs alongside their diplomatic papers, the players will accidentally expose the "Great Lie" to a low-level bureaucrat.

The Trap: Vance's Reaction

When Inquisitor Vance reads the logbook, his face will go completely pale. He won't just think they are smugglers; he will think they are unhinged, mutated by deep-sea madness, or a threat to national security. Vance will look up, gesture to his armored marines to draw their halberds, and say:

"Captain... this logbook details a supply replenishment at 'Tarchoo' and a charting expedition through 'Cordoba.' These charts have been empty water for three centuries. Writing these names is a treasonous offense against the Crown. Where did you really go?"

🎲 Tactical Pathways to Success

To survive this logbook crisis, the players must use their +20% competence bonus and their specialized assets to pivot instantly:

  • 1. The Forgery / "The Smuggler's Cut": If the players anticipate this problem before handing over the book, they can use their ship's workshop and naval expertise to quickly construct a "Double Log." They present a sanitized, fake logbook to Vance showing they spent the last month stranded in the doldrums of a standard blank Chart, while hiding the true, dangerous logbook inside the lead-lined box with the uniforms.
  • 2. Invoking Admiral Hall's Immunity: The players can double down on their diplomatic status. They can refuse to answer Vance's questions, slam the papers on the desk, and state: "Our course was classified by order of Admiral Hall of the Recurrent Foundation. If you have questions about these coordinates, take them up with the High Command—unless you want to explain to the Admiral why you breached military confidentiality." This turns their diplomatic papers into a political weapon, forcing Vance to back down out of fear for his own career.
  • 3. The Whistleblower Climax (The Daring Escape): If Vance refuses to let them go and attempts to arrest them for heresy, the diplomatic phase ends and player action begins. The players must launch a midnight breakout—knocking out the guards, using their ship's cannons to blow apart the iron chains of the Tokyoshima sea gate, and engaging the Lignum Vitae mask to sprint out of the harbor before the Alliance war-galleons can raise their anchors.

📜 The Overarching Mystery Expands

This encounter perfectly reinforces that they cannot go back to their old lives. The moment they logged those forbidden islands, they crossed a line. If they manage to talk their way out of Tokyoshima, Vance will immediately send a high-priority carrier pigeon to the capital city of Akamara (03,03). The Alliance Kingdom Management will now know that a small cargo sloop is sailing toward Liege (03,04) carrying undeniable proof of the old world. The race to deliver the pouch before royal assassins or inquisitors intercept them is officially on!

🎭 The Independent Reaches Event: The Grand Admiralty Auction

While the central Alliance government remains completely oblivious to Admiral Hall’s secret network, the Prime Minister knows exactly what the Admiral is up to. The Prime Minister understands Hall's fierce dedication to protecting the Alliance and has quietly allowed this event to proceed as a covert test of the characters' capabilities. The Alliance navy has seized a cache of black-market assets from a high-sea smuggler and is hosting a standard, state-sanctioned auction across the neutral ports of Sender (05,06), Xosha (06,04), and Dagnarius (06,03). The players must intercept this event because one of the routine auction lots contains a set of Secret Naval Approaches—the only charts detailing the safe entry vectors past the automated coastal defense batteries of Liege (03,04).

  • The Sender Gala (05,06) - Corporate Infiltration: Held inside a neutral, cliffside manor, the auction is being swarmed by Acme Corporation agents sent by Dr. Zephyr to buy up old-world secrets. The players must use their +20% competence bonus to out-gamble the corporate spies, use their diplomatic papers to secure high-tier bidding status, or swipe the charts before Acme can lock them away.
  • The Xosha Standoff (06,04) - A Bureaucratic Gamble: A routine Alliance naval escort has anchored in Xosha to oversee the legal auction transfer. The players can strategically wield their official diplomatic papers to bypass the mid-level bureaucrats, convincing the local port master that the Liege charts fall under Admiral Hall’s classified jurisdiction, forcing a legal hand-over without raising suspicion.
  • The Dagnarius Raid (06,03) - Underworld Interception: In this lawless, pirate-choked free port, a corrupt Alliance supply officer is secretly planning to sell the seized chart lot under the table to local privateers. Backed by their 4 Nashua deckhands, the players must orchestrate a precise dockside sting or midnight raid to seize the charts from the transport brig before the illicit deal closes.
