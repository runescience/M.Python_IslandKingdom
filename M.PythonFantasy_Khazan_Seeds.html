The Khazan Steppe Empire

The Realm of Grass and Clay — World Seeds

The lands of the Khazan Empire are defined by a striking contrast: an immense, unyielding sea of grass that stretches to the edge of the world, punctuated by sprawling, white-walled river cities that serve as the economic heart of the known world. It is a land shaped entirely by scale, wind, and the relentless flow of trade.

1. The Endless Sea of Grass

The Wild Frontier

  • The Horizon: The dominant feature of the landscape is its absolute, dizzying flatness. Standing in the deep steppe, the horizon stretches out in a perfect 360-degree line, making the sky feel unnaturally massive and overwhelming.
  • The Flora & Color: The land is a blanket of tough, wind-resistant feather grass and wild sage that ripples like ocean waves in the wind. The colors shift dramatically with the seasons — vibrant green and blanketed in wild tulips during the short spring, baking into a shimmering, golden sea of dry straw by late summer.
  • The Kurgans: The only permanent landmarks in the open grass are kurgans — ancient, artificial burial mounds of long-dead kings. These small, dirt hills dot the flat plains, often guarded by weathered stone statues called balbals, carved to look like silent warriors staring eternally into the wind.

2. A Climate of Brutal Extremes

  • The Scorching Summer: With no mountains or forests to block the elements, the summer sun beats down mercilessly, bringing dry, dusty heatwaves and sudden, cataclysmic thunderstorms that split the massive sky.
  • The Merciless Winter: In winter, arctic winds howl across the flat plains unimpeded. Temperatures plummet, and the dangerous buran — a blinding blizzard with howling, freezing winds — can bury travelers and livestock in minutes.

3. The Great Trading Rivers

The Lifelines

  • The Arteries of Empire: Water is the ultimate currency. Slow, wide, muddy rivers cut through the grass, creating lush green ribbons of willow trees and fertile soil.
  • The Moving Capitals: Along these riverbanks, the nomadic tribes gather in massive tent-cities made of yurts — circular, felt-lined tents. The grandest of these are built on heavy wooden wagons pulled by dozens of oxen, moving in an organized, miles-long grid.

4. The Oasis Cities

The Bastions of Wealth

Where the rivers meet ancient trade routes, the empire has constructed massive, permanent cities. They are not dark, cramped stone fortresses, but vibrant, wide-open metropolises.

  • The Dual-Layout: These cities are split into permanent quarters and open-air plazas. During the harsh winter, thousands of nomads move their tents inside the city walls for protection. In the spring, they pack up and vanish back into the grasslands, leaving the brick districts to the merchants.
  • Earth and Mud-Brick Architecture: Without mountain quarries, the cities are built from durable, thick sun-dried river mud bricks, coated in smooth white plaster to reflect the intense summer heat. Only the supreme ruler, the Khagan, is permitted to build with fired red or yellow brick — his heavily fortified palace towers over the white city like a beacon.
  • The Earthen Ramparts: Instead of stone walls, the cities are protected by incredibly wide ramparts of packed earth and clay, surrounded by deep, river-fed moats. These massive dirt walls easily absorb the impact of siege weapons without shattering.
  • The Melting Pot: Because these cities command the global trade routes, they are chaotic explosions of culture. A single city is strictly organized into autonomous quarters — traditional wood-carved halls for northern fur traders, mud-brick bazaars for southern spice merchants, and secure diplomatic districts, all rubbing shoulders in a vibrant, multicultural empire.
