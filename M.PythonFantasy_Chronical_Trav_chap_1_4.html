Campaign Master Chronicle: From Traveller Core to Grounded High-Fantasy

🌍 The Global Lore: The 300-Year Great Lie

The players originate from an Alliance of 13 Kingdoms that collectively govern the largest continental landmass on the globe. Supposedly, this world is empty, but in reality, it is much bigger than Earth*¹. The characters wouldn't immediately realize the massive scale of the planet because their bodies have become much stronger through evolution*². However, because the world is so colossal, it is much harder to get around, consisting of 80% ocean*³. There are fewer overall kingdoms scattered across this vast expanse, but they are incredibly powerful.

  • The Political Landscape: 10 kingdoms are actively Allied, 3 maintain strict Neutrality.
  • The Axis Threat: There are 3 separate, unhinged Axis Kingdoms on the fringes. They do not take prisoners; they take slaves.
  • The Core Conspiracy: 300 years ago, a world-ending event occurred. History books were aggressively purged of all foreign records by the Alliance kingdom management, who decided to keep all knowledge of the old world completely away from the public. The population was told that they were the absolute sole survivors of the planet, and that the Great Barriers and Reefs harbor world-ending terrors capable of destroying anything they touch. It is a "big lie" that is very, very, very slowly unraveling.
  • The True Global Reality: While the Alliance advanced rapidly into an advanced civilization over the last three centuries, other civilized city-states and independent realms survived across the globe. Right now, these other kingdoms have developed the capability to reach the Alliance's waters, but the masses of the Alliance remain completely oblivious to their existence.
  • The Portal Network & The Bigger Story: At one point in ancient history, there was advanced technology that allowed instant movement across distant global regions via magical transit portals. Part of the larger overarching campaign mystery involves rekindling these gates. For now, the campaign operates as a weekly serial of exciting, self-contained adventures with recurring bad guys. The characters will constantly run into frontier-ism, oddities, and live-or-die colonies. Over time, they will learn that they possess the unique ability to go farther and faster than anyone else on the planet—though there is a BIG MAYBE that someone else out there can do the exact same thing.

🏛️ Master Background Info & Campaign Factions

Admiral Hall (The Anti-Antagonist)

The players' hated uncle has many deep secrets. He is a high-ranking official who has secretly anticipated the new geopolitical needs of the changing world. He knows the big lie cannot last forever, and he is covertly structuring events so the characters can eventually carry the banner of the kingdom when they are ready. This outpost on the vacant island is his secret project, sponsored directly by powerful figures who trust him to rebuild society.

He belongs to a secretive group called the Recurrent Foundation of the Hand. This organization acts much like a secret order of masons striving to rebuild the world, safeguard powerful ancient artifacts, and fiercely keep out hostile outer influences. His organization knows about the Lignum Vitae entity and has been actively experimenting with it to improve their naval fleet. While the 10 Allied colonies plus the 3 Neutrals directly benefit from the Order's secret findings, only a few select people from their government know about this place.

Dr. Zephyr / "Z-fold" (The Antagonist)

Dr. Zephyr, also known as Z-fold, owns a massive, corporate-style syndicate known as Acme. He is one of the active negative forces operating against the Order. He experiments relentlessly on farming, animals, and people—constantly exploiting unnatural things to disrupt the fragile balance of the Alliance. Hostile outside forces like Zephyr are trying to penetrate the Foundation's network to steal their technology, threatening to spread their own ill-gotten findings, unapproved dangerous inventions, and unnatural findings throughout the alliance to establish a permanent foothold.

The Entity: R. Asilom

The artifact's cargo is not a simple monster; it is a once-large but broken group that contains the essence of change, chaos, and destruction. Its true name is R. Asilom. It is trying to reconstitute warp points around the world and rekindle the ancient transit gates to move its knowledge and rebuild a world full of magic and magical beings of prior ages. Its main ally in this endeavor is Dr. Zephyr, while its chief opponent is Admiral Hall, who will stop at nothing to keep the alliance safe and stop the entity from opening the gates. Admiral Hall orchestrated the players' initial trip specifically to send the entity back to its native spawning grounds.

🌊 The Logistical Challenge: Long-Term Voyage Survival

The Lignum Vitae mask creates a massive tactical problem for the players to solve. If it aggressively accelerates decay, it fundamentally alters how they have to plan long-term maritime travel. They aren't locked out of 2-month voyages, but they can no longer rely on standard medieval provisions like salt pork and hardtack. To survive a 2-month sail with the artifact on board, the crew will have to adopt new, highly active survival strategies:

1. Active Foraging and "Island Hopping"

Instead of sailing in a straight line across open ocean, a 2-month voyage becomes a series of tactical pitstops. Course charts must hug coastlines, barrier reefs, or archipelagos. Every 5 to 7 days, they must drop anchor at an uncharted island, send out hunting parties, gather fresh fruit, and replenish water before the artifact rots it.

2. Radical Food & Water Storage Solutions

  • The Towing Method: Construct a small, streamlined cargo raft to tow 50 feet behind the sloop. Keeping the bulk of provisions out of the artifact's immediate aura preserves it normally, though it risks storm loss.
  • Lead/Iron Lining: Build a heavily insulated, air-tight box in the hold lined with lead sheets or iron plates scavenged from island workshops to shield items from biological drain. Earthenware ceramic jugs or glass carboys from the outpost can similarly block the drain on fresh water.
  • Magical Preservation: Spellcasters can burn low-level utility spells like Purify Food and Drink or Gentle Repose daily to offset the rotting effect.

3. Living Off the Sea

Crew must fish and harvest rain-catch water using canvas deck arrays. Because the ship moves at 50% extra speed, standard fishing lines snap. They must use their +20% skill bonus to engineer heavy-duty trolling lines or harpoon giant reef fish and turtles while moving at high speeds.

4. Water Hazard: The Race Against Dehydration

The mask's biological drain causes fresh water in wooden casks to turn stagnant, slimy, or brackish in just 7 to 10 days. Survival strategies include:

  • Towed Water Rafts: Keeps water outside the mask's aura, though the immense weight drags on the sloop, forcing them to sacrifice some of their 50% speed bonus.
  • Rain-Catch Array: Rigging wide canvas tarps across the deck to funnel storm rainwater directly into clean buckets, turning the mask's Storm Magnetism curse into a survival cure.
  • Ceramic or Glass Storage: Utilizing earthenware jars, stone containers, or glass carboys, as non-organic barriers shield contents from rotting quite as fast.
  • Distillation/Desalination: Building a crude copper pipe and iron pot still over the ship's galley fire to boil seawater and harvest fresh water, consuming precious firewood.

🧭 The Chronicle: Step-by-Step Serial Campaign

Chapter 1: The Hired Trip & The Misjump

  1. The Setup & The Job (A to B): Approached by despised noble relative Admiral Hall for a deceptive "milk run" to deliver a sealed leather pouch of "peace treaties" from Port City A to Duchy B.
  2. Avoiding Onlookers (The "Jump Point"): Ordered to sneak out at night to a hidden, rocky cove 15 miles down the coast to catch the Great Trade Current unseen by local pirates.
  3. The 100D Boundary & Lockdown: Reaching deep ocean limits, they find a total maritime quarantine blockade. King orders long-range carrier pigeons and signal towers dark (Sector Silence).
  4. The Carrier Nashua: The gargantuan warship Galleon intercepts the players, forcing them to board for interrogation while royal guards secretly search their sloop's hold for contraband.
  5. The Pirate Attack & Hull Breach: Pirate fire-ships ram the Nashua. Lower decks rapidly flood with water and thick smoke. Players must grab leather diving lungs/air bladders to survive fumes and navigate chaotic zero-g style flooding to reach their sloop.
  6. The Mining Drill Escape: Collapsed rigging traps their vessel. Players use an iron-tipped boarding ram/axes to smash through burning debris under archer fire. Escaping blindly into a tempest, their rudder snaps and they are thrown wildly off course.
  7. The Sabotaged Cargo: A week later, the trunk erupts with lightning. The gelatinous life form (broken pieces of R. Asilom) fuses into the ship's masthead, seizing absolute trajectory control for a month and marooning them far beyond the Great Reefs.
  8. Artifact Properties: Lignum Vitae Mermaid Mask: detaches/transfers to other ships. Grants +50% speed increase and an unnatural +20% competence bonus to all skills. Cursed with Storm Magnetism (attracts lightning) and Biological Drain (rots food, spoils water in 7-10 days unless boxed in lead).
  9. Current Game State: The Vacant Island: Awaken on an uncharted island. Sloop is salvageable. Find a specialized Commercial Cargo Sloop (Reinforced Hull, 2 Mounted Cannons, 10 shots) in a hidden boathouse. The island outpost features functional homes, unrecognized country crests, oil-line self-destruct traps, and a cache of uncut garnets (150gp), weapons (+1 to hit), and armor (+1 AC).

Chapter 2: The Fire Arrow Fleet & The Itsy Bitsy Spider

  • Part 1: Race for the Outpost: Neutral city privateers arrive to seize the island's secrets. Players must trigger the fire-arrow mechanism to self-destruct the village, then engage the privateers. Strategic boarding allows them to capture the enemy boat for their fleet.
  • Part 2: Repairs & Signal Flare: While repairing rigging, lookouts spot a faint stone signal tower burning brightly just past the edge of known barrier charts.
  • Point of Interest: "Itsy Bitsy Spider": Former secret research outpost of the Hand that was reactivated by the players' mask proximity. Dr. Zephyr's Acme Corporation bought the abandoned site to breed giant reef-spider mutations. The spiders broke containment, slaughtered the staff, and now roam the dark. A dead scientist's note reveals the solution: flush phosphorus through the water supply. Spiders flee from bright light, forcing a high-stakes Twilight Run to reach the bunker controls before total darkness hits.

Chapter 3: Frontier Settlement & The Canyon Contract

  • The Reef-Spider Infestation:*⁴ Two crew members are bitten by small reef-spiders during Chapter 2. Induces Slow-Rot Fever (Save vs. Poison at +2). Every 3 days, roll 1d6; lose 1 point from a corresponding ability score. Permanent cure requires Ghost-Prickly Cactus sap from Sundown Atoll.
  • Frontier Settlement: Sundown Atoll: A white coral-brick, desert-frontier town operating outside the 13 Kingdoms. Survives on irrigation farming and rock-salt mining. Plagued by the Shinymen (Option A: Acme's Mirror-Plate Privateers; Option B: Crystalline Skinned Mutant Raiders; Option C: Ancient Brass Clockwork Automatons).
  • The Canyon Contract: The Shinymen block the Rust-Canyon Wellhead. To secure the Cactus sap antidote, the two primary player characters must complete a two-part contract for the Marshal:
    1. Defend the Warehouse District: Repel a midnight raid. Players use their ship's +20% skill attunement aura near the docks to transform commoners into a hyper-efficient defensive wall.
    2. The Sloop's Cannons Support: Position their sloop along the waterfront thoroughfare to rain mobile cannon fire and grape-shot directly into the Shinymen's attacking flanks.
    3. The Rust-Canyon Run: Launch a surgical counter-strike into the badlands to clear the wellhead and harvest the Ghost-Prickly Cactus sap to save their dying crew.

🚀 Chapter 4: The Cordoba Shallows & The Mystery of The Heart

Part 1: The Cordoba Shallows & Ruins of Emeritus

The Phenomenon & The Signal: Escaping an encounter with an Axis Empire Heavy Cruiser, the players steer their reinforced sloop into the Cordoba Shallows. Instantly, the region's intense magnetic field causes their navigation compasses to spin wildly. Suddenly, the Lignum Vitae mask thrums, picking up a rhythmic arcane transmission echoing across the water: the melody of "Werewolves of London." Because the players recovered a strange clockwork music box playing this exact tune back on the Chapter 1 resource island, they recognize it as a coded transponder signal for the long-lost Emeritus Colony.

Braving the Acid Fog: Cordoba is a jagged, dead island entirely shrouded in a toxic Chloride Atmosphere—a dense, yellow-green fog of choking acid mist that aggressively eats away at skin and lung tissue. Furthermore, the island suffers from extreme magic-warped temperatures, sitting at an icy 19°F during the day and plunging to a vacuum-like -113°F at night. To survive, the players must deploy their grounded fantasy workarounds:

  • Modifying their Chapter 1 leather diving bladders by lining them with lead sheeting or thick animal grease to create airtight, pre-filled fresh air respirators.
  • Coating their exposed skin in the heavy, pungent sea-reptile tallow grease used by the Sundown Atoll militia to create a temporary chemical barrier against the acid burn.

Operating strictly within the daytime window, they must infiltrate the frozen, dead bunker of Emeritus. Inside, they discover a diary revealing a horrific 120-year colony ship transit log—with the last 3 years spent trapped in this regional system after an unknown global gravity well altered their course. Scrawled madness-graffiti lines the walls: "All things to Cordoba" and "Itzy Bitzy spiders came down...", permanently connecting this graveyard to Dr. Zephyr's Acme network. Here, they secure the old world databanks, revealing the local grid secrets.

Part 2: The Tarchoo Frontier & The Gin Mill

The Tarchoo Docks: Navigating out of the magnetic trap, they arrive at the arid island of Tarchoo, characterized by massive succulent growths and indigenous desert wildlife. They pass the automated crystal security defenses of Colony L1730 to reach the main settlement (Population: 125+ rough miners and outland farmers). All local life and agriculture are sustained by water drawn from a central mystical forest known as The Heart.

The Gin Joint Intrigue: Entering the local tavern, The Gin Mill, to gather intelligence, the players encounter a distinct cast of frontier characters:

  • Guy Keno: A slick underworld figure sporting a heavy fur jacket, a necklace of tiger teeth, black mascara, and purple eye shadow. Spotting the players' high-speed, reinforced sloop at the docks, he immediately begins scheming a tavern ambush to steal their vessel for black-market trade.
  • Ty Haliday: The hardened local Protectorate who struggles to maintain a fragile peace between the townies and the wilderness.
  • Fran Fisher ("Francis"): A sharp, red-haired survivalist wearing signature black leather boots, who provides the players with vital frontier warnings.

The Dual Threat: Through the tavern talk, the players discover two hidden factions ruling the island. Burrowing beneath the outlands are the Yashon—a sentient, mole-like indigenous race who scavenged the original colony ship wreckage. The Yashon view human blood as an intense, highly addictive drug and regularly launch subterranean tunnel ambushes against anyone who bleeds in the wilds. Conversely, deep within the forest dwell the Gaxo—a stand-offish, reclusive race of telepaths who communicate strictly through dream-weaving and rigidly defend the inner forest from trespassers.

Part 3: The Mystery of The Heart & The Great Choice

The Pulse of the Old World: As the players stand on the coast, their Lignum Vitae mask begins vibrating violently whenever it is pointed toward the center of the succulent forest. Arcane energy emanations pulsing directly from the woodland confirm that a highly advanced piece of old-world technology is buried beneath the root systems. To reach it, they must coordinate a guard with their 4 Nashua deckhands to prevent Guy Keno's thugs from hijacking their sloop, navigate the telepathic dream-warnings of the Gaxo, and survive the blood-addicted tunnel ambushes of the Yashon.

The Heart Clearing: At the epicenter of the forest, the players discover a pristine clearing of bioluminescent, silver-leafed trees wrapped around the smooth, un-rusting alloy hull of a 300-year-old human colony ship. The Gaxo stand guard, testing the players' intentions via telepathy. Here, the players face a massive, campaign-defining branching choice:

The Choice Tactical & Narrative Consequences
Route A: Extract the Prize
(The Core Navigation Matrix)		 The players unearth a grapefruit-sized obsidian sphere webbed with blue leyline circuits. Slotting it into their masthead projects a 3D hologram map of the globe, exposing the "Great Lie" by revealing that the world is massive, full of colossal civilizations, and charting the coordinates of the ancient Planar Portals.
The Twist: Extracting it triggers a massive energy flare. The Yashon go into a feral frenzy, bursting from the ground, forcing a thrilling, heroic race back to the beach through a collapsing forest as Acme scouts close in.
Route B: Leave it Untouched
(The Gaxo Alliance)		 The players make a deeply heroic roleplaying choice to respect the holy site. In gratitude, the Gaxo High Elder uses telepathy to permanently imprint the exact coordinate memories of the navigation matrix directly into the players' minds—granting them the global map and portal locations without plundering the site.
The Twist: As they finish the ritual, Guy Keno's mercenaries or Acme enforcers burst into the clearing to steal the tech by force. The players must execute a dramatic heroic stand, fighting alongside the dream-weaving Gaxo to defend the ancient hull. This cements a lifelong alliance with the Gaxo and establishes Acme as a powerful, recurring enemy.
