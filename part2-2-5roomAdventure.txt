A 5-Location Outdoor Adventure

THE WALLS (why players can't go around)
  - The fog is impassable without a guide. No chart covers the interior.
  - Compass drift compounds every hour inside. Wrong heading = lost.
  - The Warden does not leave the fog. It follows vessels that broadcast.
  - Fenn's charts end at a specific point. Past it, he cannot help.

THE SPLIT (Location 3 → 4a or 4b)
  Players who engage the Warden take 4a. Slower. Harder. Pays off at 5.
  Players who push past it take 4b — Fenn's limit, faster, but they
  arrive at Dale's anchorage without the Warden resolved. Dale sees it
  immediately. The patrol window shrinks.

CROSS-LOCATION OBJECT: DARING'S CHART
  If waterproofed in Location 1 — usable through the whole voyage.
  If not waterproofed — degrades two hours into Location 3. Players
  navigate Location 4 from memory. The chart is the river water from
  the stepping-stones. Preparation in room one pays off in room four.


================================================================================
THE HOOK
================================================================================

A woman named Wendy Dale is stranded in Sinochia fog waters. She has
one year of records documenting something wrong happening inside the fog.
Acme Corporation wants those records destroyed and Dale with them.
Commodore Fenn has the contract. He needs the player's vessel and crew.
The pay is good. Dale will not wait more than three days.

THE STAKES
  Success: Dale and her records reach safety. Acme loses the information.
  Failure: Dale dies. Records destroyed. Acme wins this round clean.
  Secondary: if the Acme periapt was planted on the masthead in Liege,
  Zephyr has been reading their position daily and a retrieval team is
  already moving toward the fog margin.


================================================================================
RUMOR TABLE (d4 — gather in Liege before departure)
================================================================================

1. TRUE — The fog margin has shifted. Approach lines reliable last season
   are inside the fog now. Three navigators have refused the northern
   corridors this month.
   (Source: any harbor worker, Farnsworth, Daring)

2. TRUE — Acme has had a man in Liege harbor for six weeks posing as a
   factor. Liege intelligence posted a bounty on Acme shipping information
   three days ago. Word got around.
   (Source: barkeep at the Mersling)

3. HALF-TRUE — There is something in the fog that follows ships. Sailors
   who came back from the margin won't go back.
   (True: the Warden exists and follows vessels that broadcast.
   False: it does not leave the fog. Sailors are misremembering fear.)

4. LIE — Wendy Dale left Sinochia two weeks ago. Someone saw her in
   Dagnarius.
   (Acme disinformation. Dale is still in Sinochia. Players who chase
   this waste a week and tip Acme off that they are looking.)


================================================================================
LOCATION 1: THE LAST NIGHT IN LIEGE
The Gatehouse. Preparation determines what tools the player carries in.
================================================================================

THE WALLS
  The player leaves at first light. Everything they do not do tonight
  they cannot do inside the fog. This location is bounded by time,
  not terrain.

BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION
  Liege harbor at night. Lamp-lit stone, the smell of fish and tar,
  the knock of hulls against the pier. Hound's Dream at the far end
  of the far pier, dark hull, one light at the stern. The player has
  until first light.

WHAT EXISTS HERE — THINGS TO TOUCH, FIND, DECIDE

  HOUND'S DREAM (Fenn's vessel):
    The hold is accessible before departure. If the player searches it,
    they find the Acme periapt — a small carved bone charm on a cord,
    looped over the masthead like a sailor's good-luck token. Warm to the
    touch if held. Faintly humming. Looks like a cheap trinket.
    If they don't search, they won't find it. Nobody will tell them
    to look.
    Telegraph: on the walk to the pier, the player passes the Brothers
    Kael loading cargo two docks over. They are watching. If the player
    clocked them earlier in Liege, they know what this means.

  DARING'S CHART:
    In the player's possession from the previous scene. The ink is
    standard cartographer's ink — not waterproofed. A sealed chart
    tube protects it. An unsealed pocket does not.
    If the player asks Fenn about the fog before departure: "It is wet
    inside. Wetter than you expect. Protect anything you need to read."
    He will not say this twice.

  FENN (at the stern table):
    Answers direct questions. Does not volunteer.
    - Ask about the route: "Southwest. Alliance waters two days, then
      open sea. Fog margin end of the week. I navigate the approach.
      Dale navigates the interior. You provide the vessel and the guns."
    - Ask about the fog: "The compass drifts inside. Watch the water
      temperature. Warmer current on the correct heading."
    - Ask what to prepare: "Sleep. You won't get much once we're in."
    - Ask about the mask: "It will be noticed. Be ready to make a
      decision about it. I don't know what decision. That depends on
      what notices it."

  THE PERIAPT (GM decision point):
    Decide now whether Mira Solt planted it. A periapt is a small carved
    stone or bone charm on a cord — looped over the masthead, it looks
    like a normal sailor's good-luck token. Zephyr has the paired focus
    (a crystal ball) and scries through it once per day to read the
    vessel's position. It does not work inside the fog.
    If yes — an Acme cutter leaves Liege within hours of the player.
    It has one week to reach the fog margin. It will be waiting when
    they come out. If no — Acme has lost the trail for now.
    Commit to one. Do not change it mid-voyage.

WHAT THE PLAYER CARRIES OUT OF LOCATION 1:
  - Daring's chart: sealed or unsealed. Their choice.
  - Knowledge of the periapt: found or not found. Their choice.
  - Fenn's navigation tips: absorbed or ignored. Their choice.
  - One night of sleep, or not.


================================================================================
LOCATION 2: OPEN WATER — THE MASK WAKES
The Corridor. One week of sailing. The last stretch before the fog.
================================================================================

THE WALLS
  Open sea. The Sinochia coastal current pushes against the hull from
  the southwest by day three — they are committed to the heading.
  Turning back now costs three days. The fog margin is ahead.

BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION
  Alliance waters thinning to open sea. The color of the water changes
  by day three — darker, colder, the green gone out of it. By day five
  the horizon flattens. The light goes sourceless. No shadows. The fog
  is not visible yet but the world is already behaving differently.

WHAT EXISTS HERE — THINGS TO TOUCH, FIND, DECIDE

  THE MASK VIBRATING (day 4):
    The masthead begins vibrating at irregular intervals. Subtle.
    Bramble notices if he is on deck and watching. He says nothing
    unless the player asks him directly.
    The vibration is directional. It is not pointing at Dale's position.
    It is pointing toward the pre-Alliance mark on Daring's chart —
    a different heading entirely.
    If the player uses the mask as a compass, they drift off course.
    If they ask Fenn: "That is not our heading." He adjusts course.
    He watches the masthead. He does not explain further.

  BRAMBLE WATCHING FENN:
    Fenn navigates from the bow. Small corrections to heading, no
    explanation. He is reading the water — surface temperature,
    current behavior, the way the swell runs.
    If the player directs Bramble to study what Fenn is doing,
    Bramble learns the pattern. He will be able to use it in
    Location 4b. If the player doesn't direct him, he doesn't
    presume. He does his job and watches the horizon.

  LIEGE NAVAL PATROL (day 1-2):
    A Liege patrol vessel comes alongside. Routine clearance.
    Fenn's papers are good. The vessel is logged and cleared.
    If the Harwick Dray customs flag was never resolved in Liege,
    the patrol officer hesitates, checks twice, files a secondary
    note. Not an arrest. A clock that starts ticking back in Liege.

  FENN IN THE EVENINGS:
    The only time Fenn talks without being asked is in the evenings,
    at anchor or hove-to. He will say one thing each evening if the
    player is present. In order:
    Day 2: "Hall has been running assets for thirty years. You are
            not the first. You may be the most useful."
    Day 4: "The fog does not lie to you. It just stops telling you
            things you relied on. That is different."
    Day 6: "My charts end about two hours inside the margin. I told
            you that. I want to make sure you heard it."

WHAT THE PLAYER CARRIES OUT OF LOCATION 2:
  - Whether Bramble has the navigation pattern or not.
  - Whether the mask's direction problem has been identified or not.
  - Whether Daring's chart is still legible (if unsealed, it degrades
    two hours into Location 3 — the player has one last chance to
    seal it here if they think of it).


================================================================================
LOCATION 3: THE FOG MARGIN — ECHO VESSELS
The Puzzle Room. Instruments lie. The fog tests what the player trusts.
================================================================================

THE WALLS
  The fog margin is a gradient — the player will not know they are
  inside it until the horizon is gone. The banks are the fog itself:
  impenetrable, directionless, identical in every direction. There is
  no going around. There is only through.

BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION
  The horizon disappears over the course of an hour. The water surface
  goes flat and grey, like old glass. Smells of salt and cold stone —
  the smell of a room closed for a long time. Sound carries wrong.
  Voices from the bow sound close even when the speaker is far away.
  The compass needle drifts. One degree. Then two.

WHAT EXISTS HERE — THINGS TO TOUCH, FIND, DECIDE

  THE ECHO VESSELS:
    Shapes in the fog. Ships that passed through before them,
    imperfectly replicated. Wrong rigging. Wrong running light color.
    Headings that don't match the wind. They move with purpose but
    arrive nowhere.
    If the player chases one: it leads deeper into the fog on a
    heading that is not northwest by north. Costs time. The echo
    dissolves after twenty minutes of pursuit, leaving them off course.
    If the player asks Fenn: "The fog remembers what passes through it.
    Don't chase them."

  THEIR OWN ECHO:
    One of the echo vessels is Hound's Dream. Slightly wrong — the
    rigging a different configuration, the running lights the wrong
    color, the heading slightly off the wind. Running parallel,
    fifty yards to port. Visible for ten minutes before the fog
    takes it.
    Fenn sees it. He does not react. He has seen this before.
    If the player asks: "It means we have been noted."
    Telegraph: this is the moment the Warden begins tracking them.
    The player does not know this yet. They will feel it in Location 4.

  THE COMPASS DRIFT:
    One to two degrees per hour, compounding. Fenn corrects by
    dead reckoning and water behavior — surface temperature, current
    timing, the behavior of the swell at specific depths. He does
    not explain his corrections unless asked directly.
    If the player watches him work and asks: he will show them.
    He will point at the water surface. "Warmer here. We are on
    the correct heading. Two degrees colder and we correct north."
    This is the same information he gave in Location 1. It means
    more now that they can see it working.

  DARING'S CHART DEGRADING (if unsealed):
    Two hours inside the margin, the ink begins to run. The fog
    margin boundary blurs first. The pre-Alliance mark holds longer —
    it was drawn in older, denser ink — but by hour three it is
    gone too. The player is now navigating from memory.
    Do not announce this. Describe it: "The chart in your pocket
    is wet. When you unfold it, the lines have moved."

THE SPLIT — END OF LOCATION 3:
  The Warden makes itself known here. A shape at the edge of
  visibility. The water surface disturbing as something large
  passes beneath the hull. The mask's vibration changes character.

  Players who stop to engage it → Location 4a: The Warden.
  Players who push past it toward Fenn's limit → Location 4b.
  Both paths reach Location 5. The cost is different.


================================================================================
LOCATION 4a: THE WARDEN
The Roleplay / Puzzle Option. Slower. Pays off at Location 5.
================================================================================

THE WALLS
  The Warden does not attack. But it does not leave. It circles.
  The fog thickens around the vessel while it investigates — visibility
  drops to ten feet. Moving forward in this is navigating blind.
  The player cannot proceed until they resolve it or outlast it.

BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION
  A shape slightly denser than the surrounding grey, moving at the
  edge of visibility. The water surface disturbs as something large
  passes beneath the hull — a displacement wave that rocks the vessel
  without wind. The mask vibrates urgently. Not directional. Reactive.
  Like a dog that has heard something it recognizes.

WHAT EXISTS HERE — THINGS TO TOUCH, FIND, DECIDE

  WHAT THE WARDEN IS:
    The player does not know. They have to figure it out from behavior.
    It circles. It does not attack. It investigates.
    Fenn, if asked: "I have heard of it. The Sinochia clans call it
    the Warden. I have never seen it. I was told it responds to things
    that don't belong."
    The mask's behavior is information: it is reacting to something
    it recognizes. The vibration is not fear. It is attention.

  THREE WAYS TO RESOLVE IT — PLAYER DISCOVERS, NOT TOLD:

    COVER THE MASTHEAD:
      Canvas, rope, anything that physically muffles the masthead.
      Reduces the signal. The Warden loses specific interest.
      Circles twice more. Withdraws.
      Fenn will hint at this if asked how to make it stop:
      "Reduce what it is following."
      Outcome: Warden withdraws. Passage clears. The Warden does
      not remember this vessel as anything other than a nuisance
      that went quiet.

    HOLD COURSE AND WAIT:
      Do nothing. Maintain speed. Do not react.
      The Warden comes close enough that its displacement wave
      rocks the hull hard. Twenty minutes of this. Then it decides
      they are passing through and withdraws.
      Outcome: Warden withdraws. Passage clears. Time cost: twenty
      minutes plus crew morale damage — any NPC crew members are
      rattled. Bramble holds.

    LET THE MASK RESPOND:
      Stop trying to control it. Hold position. Let the vibration
      do what it does.
      The mask produces a low tone. Not music. A frequency.
      The Warden stops circling. The fog thins slightly around
      the vessel. The water surface settles.
      Nobody aboard knows this is possible. The player discovers
      it by reasoning from the mask's behavior, or by trying it.
      Outcome: best long-term result. The Warden recognizes the
      vessel. Future fog voyages — it does not investigate.
      Fenn watches this happen with the expression of a man
      revising something he thought he understood.

WHAT THE PLAYER CARRIES OUT OF LOCATION 4a:
  - The Warden resolved. Dale will not see it at the anchorage.
    The patrol window stays full.
  - If they let the mask respond: a relationship with the Warden
    that will matter on every future fog voyage.
  - Time cost: twenty minutes to an hour depending on method.


================================================================================
LOCATION 4b: FENN'S LIMIT
The Combat / Skill Option. Faster. Arrives at Location 5 with the
Warden unresolved.
================================================================================

THE WALLS
  Fenn stops the vessel. His charts end here. The fog looks identical
  in every direction. There are no landmarks. The compass is drifting
  two to three degrees per hour now. Moving forward without a method
  means getting lost.

BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION
  The fog is the same in every direction. The bow barely visible from
  the stern. The creak of rigging is very loud. Fenn stops the vessel
  and turns to face the player. He is not apologetic. He is precise.

WHAT EXISTS HERE — THINGS TO TOUCH, FIND, DECIDE

  FENN'S HANDOFF:
    FENN: "This is as far as my charts go. Dale's position is three
    hours northwest by north from here. Roughly. The fog shifts the
    approach. I have never been to her position."
    He pauses.
    FENN: "Your compass will drift two to three degrees per hour
    from here. Correct by water temperature — warmer current on
    the correct heading. Your navigator has been watching me work.
    He knows the pattern if you pointed him at it."
    He looks at the player.
    FENN: "I will hold position here. Come back on the reciprocal
    heading. I will be here."
    He says it with the certainty of a man who has waited in worse places.

  THE NAVIGATION PROBLEM:
    Three tools available — player has to assemble them:

    BRAMBLE: if the player directed him to watch Fenn in Location 2,
    he has the water temperature pattern. He can hold the heading.
    He will drift slightly — he is not Fenn — but he can do it.
    If the player did not direct him: he can try, but drift is worse.
    Either way, Bramble does what the player tells him. He does not
    take over.

    THE MASK: its pull is toward the pre-Alliance mark — northeast.
    Dale is northwest. Nearly opposite. If the player has figured out
    the mask's direction problem (from Location 2), they can use it
    as a negative reference: keep the pull to starboard, they are
    on the correct heading. If they haven't figured it out, the mask
    is just noise.

    DARING'S CHART: if sealed and legible, the pre-Alliance mark
    gives them a fixed reference point. If degraded, it is gone.

  FENN EXPOSED:
    Fenn is holding position at the edge of his charts. If the
    periapt was planted, an Acme cutter is at the margin.
    Fenn does not know about the chip. Whether the player told him
    is on them. If they didn't tell him, he is waiting alone at
    a position Acme can calculate.

  THE WARDEN (still following):
    The player pushed past it. It is still there. They cannot see
    it in the fog but the mask's vibration has not settled. It is
    still reactive. Still following.
    They will arrive at Location 5 with it behind them.

WHAT THE PLAYER CARRIES OUT OF LOCATION 4b:
  - A heading and three navigation tools of varying quality.
  - The Warden unresolved — Dale will see it the moment they arrive.
  - Fenn alone at the midpoint, possibly exposed.
  - Faster arrival at Location 5 than the 4a path.


================================================================================
LOCATION 5: DALE'S ANCHORAGE
The Climax. Everything the player prepared for or failed to prepare
for arrives here at once.
================================================================================

THE WALLS
  A low rock shelf, barely above the waterline, accessible only from
  the water. The fog is thinner here, close to the rock. The Sinochia
  patrol comes through this anchorage every six hours. The window is
  ninety minutes — less if the Warden is still following the vessel,
  because the patrol reads the Warden following a ship as trespass.

BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION
  Three structures on a low rock shelf. Old stone, older than the
  Alliance. One lamp burning in the chart house window — a slow-burn
  wick on a timer. The mooring line has a specific knot pattern.
  Dale is not here.

WHAT EXISTS HERE — THINGS TO TOUCH, FIND, DECIDE

  THE KNOT MARKER:
    A specific knot on the mooring line. Dale's signal — she moved,
    ninety minutes further northwest, secondary position. The knot
    tells anyone who knows to look where she went.
    If the player examines the mooring line: they find it. They go
    direct. Ninety minutes to Dale.
    If the player doesn't examine it and searches instead: time cost.
    The patrol window shrinks. If the Warden is also unresolved,
    the window may already be gone.

  THE SUMMARY PAGE (hidden reward):
    Inside the chart house, under the drafting table, a second
    waterproofed packet. Dale left it in case the primary records
    were lost. One page. A year of fog movement compressed into
    a single diagram. The source marked with an X. No label.
    No coordinates. Just a location relative to the current margin.
    The player will not find this unless they search the chart house.
    It is not announced. It is just there.

  THE WARDEN (if unresolved — from 4b path):
    Dale sees it the moment the vessel arrives.
    DALE (not yet visible, voice from the fog): "You brought it.
    How long do we have?"
    The patrol window is now reduced by half. The player must
    resolve the Warden here, under time pressure, or leave
    immediately and hope the patrol doesn't come early.

  DALE'S SECONDARY POSITION (ninety minutes northwest):
    A smaller rock shelf. No structures. Dale in a small tender,
    waterproofed case in her lap, watching the fog. Three days
    of supplies. She has been watching for them.

    She looks at the vessel. She looks at the masthead. She looks
    at the player.

    DALE: "You found the knot." (if they did)
    DALE: "You didn't find the knot. How long did that cost us?" (if not)

    Dale — wants: out, with the records intact. Not rescued.
    Extracted. Speaks in short sentences. Does not waste words.

    What she will say:
    - Ask about the records: "A year of margin movement. The pattern
      has a source. I know roughly where. I don't know what it is."
    - Ask about the mask: "It's been pulling northeast since you
      entered the fog. That's the direction the fog is moving away
      from. I don't think that's a coincidence."
    - Ask about the Warden: "Did you resolve it or is it still
      following you?" If still following: "Then we need to move now."
    - Ask if she knows anyone on the patrol: "One person. I would
      prefer not to need them."

  THE PATROL (environmental hazard):
    If the window runs out — or if the Warden is still following
    and the patrol reads it — two Sinochia clan vessels come out
    of the fog. Not hostile by default. Hostile to vessels that
    don't belong.
    If the Warden was resolved (4a path): the patrol sees a vessel
    the fog accepts. They are cautious, not aggressive. Dale's
    contact on the patrol is enough to talk through it.
    If the Warden was not resolved (4b path): the patrol sees a
    vessel the fog is following. They move to intercept. Dale's
    contact is the only card left to play — and Dale will play it,
    but she will not be happy about it.
    DALE: "I said I would prefer not to need them."


================================================================================
REWARDS
================================================================================

DALE'S TRUST (earned, not given):
  If the player found the knot, managed the Warden, and arrived
  with time to spare — Dale opens the waterproofed case on the
  return voyage and shows them the records. Not describes. Shows.
  DALE: "The fog is moving away from something. Something that was
  here before the Alliance, before the fog, before any of this.
  And whatever it is — your mast knows what it is."
  This is the real reward. It points directly at R242.

THE SUMMARY PAGE (if found at the anchorage):
  One page. A year of fog movement in a single diagram. The source
  marked with an X. Useful now. Essential later.

THE WARDEN'S RECOGNITION (if resolved by letting the mask respond):
  Not an object. A relationship. Future fog voyages: the Warden
  does not investigate the vessel. The Sinochia clans notice that
  the fog does not react to this ship the way it reacts to strangers.
  Doors open that do not open for outsiders.

THE ACME CUTTER (if chip was planted — return voyage):
  Waiting at the margin. Three crew, two Shinyman Enforcers, one
  specialist (records handler, not a fighter).
  Telegraph the danger: as they approach the margin, the mask's
  vibration changes again — not the Warden, something else. Metal
  and purposeful. Dale notices it too: "We have company outside."
  The cutter crew has been waiting a week. They are cold and tired.
  The specialist separates from the enforcers easily if the player
  is clever. Fenn has a Foundation signal flare he has not mentioned.
  He will use it without asking if the situation is bad enough.


================================================================================
FAILING FORWARD
================================================================================

FAIL — Player doesn't take the contract:
  Dale waits her full window, attempts to move on her own, is taken
  by the Acme cutter at the margin. Records destroyed. Dale held.
  Hall sends another message. Not a contract this time. A request.
  The player owes him one.

FAIL — Player loses Dale to the Acme cutter on the return:
  The summary page (if found) and the Sinochia copies at the anchorage
  survive. Dale is alive — Acme needs her to explain the records before
  destroying them. There is a window to get her back. Shorter than the
  first window. The Sinochia copies cannot be destroyed without Acme
  entering Sinochia waters directly.

FAIL — Player navigates badly and misses Dale entirely:
  Dale moves again. Leaves another marker. The player re-enters the fog.
  The Sinochia patrol has now logged an unknown vessel in their waters.
  The next entry is not clean. The Warden, if unresolved, is still there.
