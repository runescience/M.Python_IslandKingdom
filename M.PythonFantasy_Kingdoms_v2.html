Kingdom Compendium: The Known World

Every kingdom in the campaign world, drawn from real-world cultural analogs and filtered through 300 years of isolation and the Great Lie.

The Alliance Territories

Akamara Alliance

Analog: The AmericasGrid: 03,03Pop: 80mOwner: All kingdoms (shared capital)

The grand capital of the Alliance, owned collectively by all ten allied kingdoms. Draws from the broad spirit of the Americas: a melting pot of ambition, frontier energy, and democratic idealism, tempered by the weight of being the world's administrative heart.

  • Culture: Cosmopolitan and transactional. Every language, guild, and political faction has a foothold here. Akamara is where deals are made and broken.
  • Governance: A council of representatives from all ten Allied kingdoms — perpetually gridlocked and perpetually negotiated.
  • Military: Home to the Alliance's largest naval fleet, including warships like the Nashua. The most heavily fortified harbor in the known world.
  • Atmosphere: Grand, loud, and crawling with royal inquisitors, spies, and merchants. Opportunity and surveillance in equal measure.

Liege Alliance

Analog: United KingdomGrid: 03,04Pop: 300mPossessions: 7

The most powerful sovereign duchy in the Alliance — technically a member, but operating with immense political independence. Constitutional monarchy, legendary navy, global trading empire, and an unshakeable belief in its own exceptionalism. Destination of the players' diplomatic pouch.

  • Culture: Formal, hierarchical, deeply proud of tradition. Values order, contract law, and institutional rule. They invented half the Alliance's legal framework.
  • Naval Power: Second only to Akamara in fleet size, arguably superior in seamanship. Liege captains are considered the finest navigators in the known world.
  • Trade: Merchant houses operate across every Allied port. Their currency is the most stable in the Alliance.
  • Atmosphere: Stately and fog-shrouded. Fortified harbors, stone architecture, a bureaucracy that runs like clockwork — or claims to.

Manchu Empire Alliance

Analog: Asian (Manchurian / northern imperial)Grid: 04,03Pop: 300mPossessions: 1

A proud and ancient kingdom ruled by a despot king whose favor is not optional — everyone must be seen to admire him, and most have learned it is easier to mean it. Conservative in tradition, difficult to navigate bureaucratically, and selectively reactive rather than slow. When Manchu moves, it has already decided.

  • Culture: Hierarchical and scholarly. Art, philosophy, and engineering are prized. Social harmony is expected and enforced through layers of court protocol and bureaucratic procedure.
  • Governance: A despot king sits above an elaborate civil service. The bureaucracy is genuinely difficult — forms, seals, intermediaries, and waiting. Getting anything done officially requires patience or the right contact.
  • Economy: A large internal market. Self-sufficient enough to be selective about outside trade, which gives their merchants quiet leverage.
  • Atmosphere: Ancient, layered, and proud. Outsiders are received with formal courtesy and watched carefully. The king's portrait is everywhere.
  • Balance: Manchu is one of ten equal Alliance members with the same stake and the same vote as everyone else.
  • Rivalry: Manchu recognizes Sinochia as a sovereign state — formally, correctly, and with barely concealed contempt. It views Sinochia as a competitor it intends to outlast. Nothing is said openly. Everything is understood.

Sinochia Alliance

Analog: Asian (southern coastal / trading diaspora)Grid: 03,05Pop: 600mPossessions: 4

Where Manchu represents imperial inland tradition, Sinochia is its coastal mercantile counterpart — the southern asian of seafarers, traders, and clan networks that spread across every port in the known world. Its waters are permanently shrouded in supernatural fog and treacherous reef lines, making it the best hiding place in the known world for ships fleeing pursuit.

  • Culture: Entrepreneurial, clan-based, intensely practical. Traders, shipbuilders, and navigators. Family loyalty and commercial networks are the twin pillars of society.
  • Resources: Rich in undiscovered raw material caches and ancient processing infrastructure from the old world.
  • Atmosphere: Fog-wrapped and labyrinthine. Locals know every current; outsiders get lost and die.
  • Strategic Value: The fog makes Sinochia's waters the best place to hide from a pursuing fleet.
  • Balance: Sinochia is one of ten equal Alliance members. Its fog and geography are defensive advantages, not a path to dominance — it has the same stake and the same vote as everyone else.
  • Rivalry: Sinochia views Manchu as an inland bureaucracy with no understanding of the sea and no legitimate claim over island territories it cannot navigate to. The fog is partly why Manchu cannot enforce its claims — you cannot project imperial authority into waters where your ships get lost and die. A letter of introduction from a Manchu official is a liability in a Sinochia port, and vice versa.

New Portigua Alliance

Analog: Portugal / Spain (PortoSpania)Grid: 04,04Pop: 30mPossessions: 9

Small in population but enormous in reach. The great Age-of-Exploration kingdom of the Alliance — nine island territories, more than almost any other kingdom. Punches far above its weight through sheer navigational daring and a sprawling network of colonial outposts.

  • Culture: Adventurous, devout, intensely proud of their exploratory heritage. New Portiguan sailors are the most widely traveled people in the Alliance. They have charts no one else has seen.
  • Governance: Constitutional monarchy with a powerful merchant-noble class. The crown funds exploration; the merchants fund the crown.
  • Economy: Trade routes, colonial extraction, and the sale of navigational knowledge. Their cartographers are worth their weight in gold.
  • Atmosphere: Sun-bleached harbor towns, tiled plazas, salt and spice. Warm, proud, and quietly aware they know more about the world than they let on.

NewFoundland / Akira Alliance

Analog: Norse / Germanic (frontier wilderness)Grid: 06,05Pop: 200mPossessions: 3

A cultural hybrid of Norse seafaring endurance and Germanic precision craftsmanship. Vast, cold, and resource-rich — a frontier kingdom that builds the finest ships in the Alliance and harvests from environments no one else can survive in. They do not boast about this. They don't need to.

  • Culture: Stoic, communal, and deeply practical. Individual glory means nothing without the crew behind you. Craftsmanship is a point of honor — shoddy work is a personal failing, not a professional one.
  • Industry: Shipbuilding, precision engineering, and resource extraction from extreme cold-water environments. Their timber, iron work, and hull designs are sought by every Allied navy.
  • Governance: A thing council of clan chiefs and guild masters. Decisions are debated at length and rarely reversed once made.
  • Atmosphere: Cold, vast, and quietly magnificent. Enormous forests, deep harbors, longhouse feasting halls, and workshops where master craftspeople work in near-silence.

Tokyoshima Alliance

Analog: Japan (metropolitan core)Grid: 06,07Pop: 80mPossessions: 0

The urban, cultural, and commercial heart of Japan's presence in the Alliance. Where NewFoundland/Akira represents Japan's frontier engineering spirit, Tokyoshima is its aesthetic and mercantile soul: art, ceremony, financial markets, and razor-sharp social hierarchy.

  • Culture: Intensely refined and protocol-driven. Every interaction has a correct form. Outsiders who learn the forms are welcomed; those who ignore them are frozen out permanently.
  • Economy: A financial and trading hub. Their merchant banks hold debt from half the Alliance.
  • Governance: Constitutional monarchy with a powerful shogunate-style administrative class beneath the imperial surface.
  • Atmosphere: Immaculate, layered, beautiful. Lantern-lit harbor districts and the constant low hum of commerce conducted with perfect composure.

Ombreval / Dark Valley Alliance

Analog: French nobility / biblical cities of refugeGrid: 06,06Pop: 20mPossessions: 0

A kingdom ringed by mountains so tall they swallow the morning sun until the tenth hour and reclaim it again by late afternoon. In the permanent half-light between, one of the most quietly elegant civilizations in the known world has taken root. Ombreval is a kingdom of refuge — not a hiding place for criminals, but a formal institution of mercy with the weight of ancient law behind it. At its heights, French-influenced nobles debate philosophy over candlelit suppers. At its gates, the displaced and the wrongly pursued wait to have their cases heard. Both are Ombreval. Neither contradicts the other.

  • Nobility & Court: The monarchy and aristocracy are old, cultured, and take their role as protectors of refuge seriously — not as charity, but as a point of honor. The court values art, architecture, music, and fine cuisine. Displaced artists, craftsmen, and scholars from every corner of the world have washed up here over the centuries, and the nobility has absorbed the best of all of them. The result is a court culture richer and stranger than any purely French equivalent.
  • Cities: The streets are a labyrinth — built that way deliberately. Bounty hunters and foreign soldiers find themselves turned around within minutes. Locals navigate by memory. Newcomers are escorted. The cities are orderly within their own logic; cold-weather crops fill the market stalls — root vegetables, hardy grains, fungi, smoked meats — grown in the mountain shadow where full sun never reaches. The people do not go hungry.
  • The Nameless Legion: Ombreval's military has two wings. The Wardens are internal and defensive — sworn to the crown, they never leave Ombreval's borders. Their entire mandate is protecting citizens and enforcing the refuge law. They do not go to war abroad. The second wing is the Nameless Legion — the expeditionary force. They are called upon when the Alliance requires Dark Sun's contribution to a joint campaign; when Ombreval needs to project power without involving the crown directly, as the Legion fights under its own banner rather than the royal one, giving the monarchy plausible distance; when a granted refugee must be escorted safely to Ombreval through hostile territory; and for mercenary contracts abroad, which fund the kingdom and keep the Legion sharp between wars. Anyone may join: exiles, disgraced soldiers, men and women who have nothing left to lose. Upon enlistment, a Legionnaire surrenders their name and is known only by rank and number until their term ends. Those who complete their service are granted full Ombreval citizenship and a new name if they want one. Legionnaires fight harder than most conscript armies — they have no homeland by birth to defend, only the one that took them in. The Legion asks nothing about your past. It asks only what you are willing to become.
  • Atmosphere: Dim, cool, and more beautiful than outsiders expect. The mountain shadow gives everything a blue-grey cast even at midday. Lanterns burn in windows at all hours. Grand courtly architecture sits alongside narrow refugee quarters and Legion barracks. The cities smell of woodsmoke, stone, and whatever the season's kitchen is producing. No one asks where you came from. Everyone here left something behind.

Khazan Steppe Empire Alliance

Analog: RussiaGrid: 07,06Pop: 100mPossessions: 3

The great northern giant of the Alliance — vast, cold, and possessed of a cultural depth and resilience that outsiders perpetually underestimate. An ally you want and an enemy you never want to make.

  • Culture: Communal, fatalistic, fiercely proud. Khazanis believe suffering builds character and that their kingdom has been tested by history in ways no other has. They are probably right.
  • Military: Enormous standing army and a navy built for endurance over speed. Khazani ships survive conditions that sink everyone else.
  • Economy: Timber, furs, grain, and mineral wealth from territories so vast they have never been fully mapped. Self-sufficient enough to be diplomatically independent.
  • Atmosphere: Vast, cold, beautiful in a way that feels indifferent to human comfort. Fortified port cities, enormous feasting halls, and a hospitality that is absolute once you are inside.

Analog: European Union (continental Europe)Grid: 05,05Pop: 60m eachPossessions: 0

The Alliance's two great administrative island territories — small in population but enormous in institutional influence. Drawing from the EU tradition of multilateral governance and civil law, these are where the Alliance's legal framework, trade agreements, and diplomatic protocols are actually written and maintained.

  • Culture: Technocratic, multilingual, obsessed with process. The EuroPublic believes the right committee, given enough time, can solve any problem.
  • Governance: A rotating council of appointed commissioners representing all Allied kingdoms. Produces an extraordinary volume of binding regulation.
  • Economy: No independent resource base — entirely dependent on Alliance trade and administrative fees. Politically neutral, financially vulnerable.
  • Atmosphere: Clean, orderly, slightly sterile. Grand meeting halls and the quiet hum of bureaucrats who genuinely believe paperwork saves lives.

The Axis Slave Reaches

The three Axis kingdoms take no prisoners — only slaves. Populations are listed as unreported because they do not distinguish between citizens and captives in their census records. All three maintain massive armadas and attack any ship flying Alliance colors on sight.

Cush Axis

Analog: Ancient Nubia / Kingdom of KushGrid: 03,07Pop: UnreportedPossessions: 2Kontors: 7Mining: 3

Draws from the ancient Kingdom of Kush — the great Nubian civilization that rivaled and at times conquered Egypt, known for its warrior culture, iron-smelting, and fierce independence. The oldest of the three Axis powers and the most militarily sophisticated. Their iron-hulled warships are the most feared vessels on the Axis frontline.

  • Culture: Martial, hierarchical, built around a warrior-priest caste that controls both military and religious authority. Strength is the only currency that matters.
  • Military: The most disciplined Axis fleet. Heavily armored, slow, nearly impossible to sink. Their boarding parties are legendary for their ferocity.
  • Slave Economy: Uses enslaved labor for mining and construction on a scale that dwarfs the other Axis kingdoms. Their seven kontors are built and maintained entirely by captive labor.
  • Atmosphere: Monumental and terrifying. Enormous iron fortresses, war-drums that carry across the water, a military culture that has been at war so long it no longer remembers peace.

Pote Axis

Analog: Ancient Carthage / PhoeniciaGrid: 04,06Pop: UnreportedPossessions: 1Kontors: 7Mining: 3

Draws from the Carthaginian and Phoenician tradition — a maritime mercantile empire built on trade, naval supremacy, and a ruthless willingness to do what other civilizations considered unthinkable. Has taken that energy and weaponized it entirely toward slave-trading and resource extraction.

  • Culture: Mercantile at its core, but the commodity is people. Views every interaction as a transaction and every person as a potential asset or liability.
  • Naval Power: Fast, maneuverable raiding fleets designed for interception and capture rather than destruction. Dead slaves have no value.
  • Economy: The Axis slave trade's primary broker. Pote processes, categorizes, and distributes captives to the other Axis kingdoms and black-market buyers in the neutral zone.
  • Atmosphere: Coldly efficient and deeply unsettling. Their ports are immaculate. Their records are meticulous. The horror is in the normalcy of it all.

Qenit Axis

Analog: Ancient EgyptGrid: 03,08Pop: UnreportedPossessions: 1Kontors: 7Mining: 3

Draws from ancient Egypt — the god-king pharaonic tradition, monumental building culture, and a belief in divine right so absolute that the ruler is literally considered a living deity. Qenit's pharaoh-class rulers have used that theological framework to justify a slave economy of staggering scale. Every monument, every kontor, every warship is built by captive hands.

  • Culture: Theocratic and monumental. The ruling class genuinely believes they are divine intermediaries between the mortal world and cosmic order. This is sincere, which makes it more dangerous than propaganda.
  • Architecture: Builds on a scale that defies comprehension. Their island fortresses are the largest structures in the known world, built to last ten thousand years.
  • Military: Enormous but slow-moving. Relies on overwhelming force rather than tactical sophistication. They do not need to be clever when they can simply bring more ships.
  • Atmosphere: Awe-inspiring and suffocating. Gold, stone, and the absolute silence of a society where questioning the divine order is not merely illegal — it is literally inconceivable to most of its inhabitants.

Designer Notes: The Manchu / Sinochia Rivalry

Two of the Alliance's most powerful kingdoms are in permanent low-grade conflict with each other. This is not open war — both hold Alliance council seats and are bound by the same treaties. It is something more corrosive.

  • Power balance: Neither kingdom is a main player or a dominant force. Both have equal stakes in the Alliance alongside the other eight members. The rivalry is a background tension, not a world-defining conflict.
  • Manchu's position: Sinochia is a dedicated independent competitor. Manchu recognizes its sovereignty — through gritted teeth. Publicly cordial, privately seething. Manchu's long game is to outlast Sinochia commercially and politically, drive it into irrelevance, and never once admit that is the plan.
  • Sinochia's position: Manchu is a landlocked bureaucracy that has never understood the sea. Its island claim is illegitimate. The fog ensures it can never be enforced.
  • The council problem: Both kingdoms vote in Akamara. Every disputed issue between them deadlocks the already gridlocked council further. Other kingdoms quietly take sides based on their own interests.
  • Taiwan analog: Sinochia culturally owns the disputed island — settled by Fujianese and Cantonese coastal migrants, entrepreneurial and maritime. Manchu claims it on imperial grounds. Neither will back down.
  • Campaign implication: A party with credentials from one kingdom is viewed with suspicion by the other. Missions that require cooperation between them are the most politically dangerous jobs in the Alliance.

Designer Notes: The Ghost Island

There is a small chance of a ghost island appearing in any ocean hex. It may be a myth. No chart agrees on where it was. Sailors who claim to have seen it can't agree on what it looked like.

What It Could Be

  • A living creature — a sea turtle or whale-island so old it has grown a forest on its back. Sailors who land don't realize it's alive until it dives.
  • A fold in reality — a genuine island that exists outside normal space. Appears on no current chart, vanishes before you can mark it. Possibly tied to the portal network already woven into the world's history.
  • A ghost ship grown vast — a vessel so ancient and encrusted it now resembles an island from a distance. Something still moves on it.

How It Works Mechanically

  • No fixed grid coordinate — small random chance of appearing in any ocean hex each session.
  • Players who have seen it cannot agree on where it was or what it looked like.
  • It appears on very old charts with no name — only a question mark in a different hand than the rest of the map.

What It Means Politically

  • Every kingdom has a claim on it if it is real — it would be the most contested territory in the known world.
  • Dagnarius free-traders would pay a fortune for reliable sighting data.
  • New Portigua's cartographers have the most recorded sightings and will not share them.

The Myth Angle

Keep it ambiguous indefinitely. The moment it is confirmed real it becomes a quest objective and loses half its power. As a myth it does more work — it tells you something about every NPC who believes or dismisses it. Sailors who have seen it are changed by it. That change is the real story.

Designer Notes: Races & Peoples of the Known World

The 17 kingdoms are politically human-dominated but the world is fully populated with fantasy races, most integrated into daily life rather than segregated into enemy factions.

Races with Economic Roles

  • Dwarves — hold a near-monopoly on mining. License operations to humans and other races. Will sell non-gold mines outright. Respected, powerful, and protective of their economic position.
  • Goblins — master craftsmen. Some of the finest precision work in the known world comes from goblin workshops. Not hated or persecuted — valued. Occasionally at odds with dwarves over mining contracts and raw material access.
  • Elves — still forest-oriented. Found in wooded coastal regions, island interiors, and ancient groves. Present in cities but not urban by nature. Long-lived enough to remember things no human kingdom does.
  • Orcs — frequently tribal. Present across the world in varying degrees of integration. Tribal orcs exist beyond city boundaries; integrated orcs are found in mercenary companies, labor guilds, and frontier settlements.

Other Known Races

  • Trolls, Lizardmen, Sahuagin, and many others — some integrated into coastal and underwater trade networks, others tribal or hostile depending on region and history.
  • Population follows realistic settlement patterns — dense in cities, thinning through towns and villages to sparse frontier. Correct ratios of settlements for world scale.
  • Many races and civilizations remain undiscovered beyond the Ruined Charts.

Character Classes in the World

All standard 1st edition classes exist and are distributed naturally across the world: Fighters, Rogues, Clerics, Mages, Bards, Assassins, Cavaliers, Barbarians, Witches, Paladins, and others. No class is unique to any one kingdom — though some kingdoms produce more of certain types by culture and tradition.

Designer Notes: Steel Technology (600 AD)

Steel exists by 600 AD but unevenly distributed across the world.

  • India — most advanced. Wootz steel (Damascus steel's source material) produced since ~300 BC. Indian steel was exported to the Middle East and considered the finest in the world.
  • Asia (Manchu / Sinochia) — blast furnace technology, cast iron and early steel at scale. Most sophisticated iron/steel industry on the planet at this point.
  • Middle East — working with imported Indian wootz, forging it into Damascus blades.
  • Europe (Liege) — still mostly iron. Pattern-welded blades exist but true steel is rare and poorly understood. Won't get reliable steel production until ~1000-1100 AD. Liege may have acquired steel through trade given their naval power.
  • Cush (Axis) — a major historical iron-smelting culture. Iron yes, steel possibly.
  • Africa / Americas — iron working in sub-Saharan Africa, no steel yet.

Campaign implication: Manchu and Sinochia have a significant military-industrial advantage in steel production. Liege and New Portigua likely acquire it through trade. The Axis kingdoms have iron at scale but steel is rare among them except possibly Cush.

Designer Notes: Kontors

The concept of autonomous trade hubs draws from real historical models — think the East India Company's "factories," or the Hanseatic League's Kontors. A fixed, autonomous outpost with its own rules, its own guards, and its own ledgers.

Alternatives Considered

  • Kontors — historically accurate to the Hanseatic model, exotic enough to feel fantasy, implies autonomy and trade in one word. Dagnarius with 12 Kontors feels exactly right.
  • Factors / Factoria — Age of Exploration term for a trading post with autonomous authority. New Portigua with 9 island possessions and several factoria fits the Portugal analog perfectly.
  • Free Ports — simpler, immediately understood, implies autonomy and commerce without any sci-fi baggage.

Kontors is the preferred term — it's a real historical word that most players won't immediately recognize, which gives it that fantasy-world flavor while being grounded in exactly the right cultural tradition (independent merchant republics, which is what Dagnarius, Bartog, and others are all about).

The Axis kingdoms having Kontors built by slave labor also has a dark irony to it — they've taken a symbol of free trade and turned it into something else entirely.
