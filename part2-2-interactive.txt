================================================================================
PART 2-2 — THE FOG VOYAGE: GM OUTLINE (INTERACTIVE)
Liege to Wendy Dale. Player-driven.
================================================================================

HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT
-------------------------
This is not a sequence of scenes. It is a set of facts about the world
that are true before the player does anything. Run the world honestly.
Let the player discover problems. Do not solve problems for them.

Each section is: WHAT IS TRUE / WHAT THE PLAYER DOESN'T KNOW YET /
HOW THEY FIND OUT (if they look).

The voyage has three legs:
  LEG 1 — Open water. Liege to the fog margin. Approximately one week.
  LEG 2 — The fog margin and interior. Fenn navigates. Half a day.
  LEG 3 — Alone in the fog. Three hours to Dale.

Nothing is gated. The player can push fast, go slow, ask questions,
ignore things, make mistakes. All of that is valid. The world responds
to what they do, not to what stage they are in.


================================================================================
BEFORE DEPARTURE — WHAT FENN GIVES THEM
================================================================================

WHAT IS TRUE:
  Fenn provides a heading and a rough timeline. He does not give them
  a chart of the fog interior — he doesn't have one. What he gives them:
    - The approach heading into the margin (southwest, correcting for
      the Sinochia coastal current)
    - Dale's position described as: "three hours northwest by north
      from the point where my charts end"
    - One piece of practical knowledge: "The compass drifts inside.
      Watch the water temperature. Warmer current runs on the correct
      heading."
    - A warning: "The mask will be noticed. I don't know by what.
      Be ready to make a decision about it."

  That is all he gives them. He does not explain the Warden. He does
  not explain the echo vessels. He has not been deep enough in to know.

WHAT THE PLAYER DOESN'T KNOW:
  - What "be ready to make a decision" means in practice.
  - That Daring's chart will degrade in the fog moisture. The ink
    is not waterproofed. They have it in a coat pocket or a chart
    tube — if the tube is sealed, it survives. If not, they have
    maybe two hours of legibility once inside the margin.
  - Whether the Acme tracking chip was planted. This is still
    unresolved from Liege.

GM NOTE — THE CHART:
  Do not tell the player the chart will degrade. If they ask about
  waterproofing their charts before departure, they thought of it
  and it is fine. If they didn't ask, the chart degrades. This is
  not a trap — it is a consequence of preparation. Either outcome
  is interesting.

GM NOTE — THE TRACKING CHIP:
  Decide now whether Mira Solt planted it. If yes: an Acme cutter
  is already following at distance. It will not enter the fog —
  Acme does not sail the fog willingly. But it will be waiting
  at the margin when they come out. If no: Acme has lost the trail
  for now. Either is fine. Pick one and commit.


================================================================================
LEG 1 — OPEN WATER: LIEGE TO THE FOG MARGIN
Approximately one week. Alliance waters thinning to open sea.
================================================================================

WHAT IS TRUE:
  - The first two days are Liege home waters. Alliance naval patrols
    are routine here. Fenn's Foundation papers cover any inspection.
    A Liege patrol vessel will come alongside on day one or two —
    standard clearance check. Fenn handles it.
  - By day three the patrols thin out. The water changes color —
    darker, colder. The Sinochia coastal current begins to push
    against the hull from the southwest.
  - The mask begins vibrating around day four. Subtle at first.
    Toward the fog. Bramble notices if he is watching the masthead.
  - The light changes in the final two days. Flatter. Less shadow.
    The horizon looks wrong in a way that is hard to name. This is
    the fog's edge effect — it reaches further than the fog itself.
  - If the tracking chip was planted, an Acme cutter left Liege
    within hours of them. It has a week to get into position at
    the fog margin. It will be there when they come out.

WHAT THE PLAYER DOESN'T KNOW:
  - The mask's vibration is directional. It is not pointing at Dale's
    position. It is pointing toward the pre-Alliance mark on Daring's
    chart, which is not where they are going. If the player is using
    the mask as a compass, they are being pulled off course.
  - A week of open water is time the player can use. Fenn will answer
    questions. Bramble can be directed to study Fenn's navigation.
    Daring's chart can be waterproofed now if the player thinks of it.
    These preparations matter. The fog does not give second chances.

WHAT FENN WILL AND WON'T SAY:
  Fenn answers direct questions honestly. He does not volunteer.
    - Ask about the mask vibrating: "It responds to old things.
      There are old things ahead."
    - Ask what direction it's pointing: he looks. He frowns slightly.
      "That is not our heading." He does not explain further.
      He adjusts course and watches the masthead.
    - Ask about the week ahead: "Use the time. Ask what you need
      to ask. Once we are in the fog I will be busy."

DECISIONS THE PLAYER MIGHT MAKE HERE:
  - Do they ask Fenn about the mask's direction, or assume it's
    pointing at Dale?
  - Do they seal Daring's chart against moisture?
  - Do they direct Bramble to study Fenn's navigation methods?
  - Do they tell Fenn about the tracking chip?
  - Do they push hard and arrive at the margin tired, or pace
    themselves?

  None of these have a right answer. All of them have consequences.


================================================================================
LEG 2 — THE MARGIN: WHERE INSTRUMENTS LIE
Half a day. Fenn navigating. Compass unreliable.
================================================================================

WHAT IS TRUE:
  - The fog margin is a gradient, not a wall. The player will not
    know they are in it until the horizon is gone.
  - The compass drifts. One to two degrees per hour, compounding.
    Fenn corrects by dead reckoning and water behavior. He does not
    explain his corrections unless asked.
  - Echo vessels appear. Shapes in the fog — ships that have passed
    through, imperfectly replicated. Wrong rigging. Wrong light color.
    Headings that don't match the wind. They are not threats.
  - One echo will be their own vessel. Slightly wrong. Running
    parallel, fifty yards to port, for about ten minutes.
  - The mask's pull intensifies. Still northeast. Still not Dale.

WHAT THE PLAYER DOESN'T KNOW:
  - The echo vessels are harmless. They may not know this.
  - Their own echo appearing means the fog has already recorded
    their passage — which means something in the fog knows they
    are here. Fenn knows this implication. He will not say it
    unless asked directly: "What does it mean that we saw ourselves?"
    His answer: "It means we have been noted."
  - The Warden is already tracking them. It has been since they
    entered the margin. The player has no way to know this yet.

WHAT FENN WILL AND WON'T SAY:
  - Ask about the echo vessels: "The fog remembers what passes
    through it. Don't chase them."
  - Ask about their own echo: "It means we have been noted."
    Nothing more.
  - Ask if they are on course: he checks the water surface, checks
    his personal chart marks, gives a heading correction. He is
    right. He does not explain how he knows.
  - Ask him to go faster: "Faster means less time to correct.
    Your choice."

FENN'S LIMIT — THE PLAYER CAN DISCOVER THIS:
  If the player watches Fenn navigate carefully, they will notice
  he is working from a set of personal marks on his chart that
  end at a specific point. Past that point, his chart is blank.
  He has not said this. If they ask: "My charts end about two
  hours in. I told you that before we left." He did. Whether
  they remembered is on them.

  If they didn't notice and didn't ask, they will find out when
  he stops the vessel in Leg 3.

DECISIONS THE PLAYER MIGHT MAKE HERE:
  - Do they try to follow the echo vessels? (They lead nowhere
    useful and cost time.)
  - Do they ask about their own echo, or let it go?
  - Do they watch how Fenn navigates, or leave it to him?
    Watching matters — Bramble can learn the pattern if the
    player directs him to pay attention.
  - Do they try to use the mask as a compass? If yes, they
    are being pulled northeast, not toward Dale. They need
    to figure this out before Leg 3.


================================================================================
THE WARDEN — A PROBLEM THAT EXISTS THROUGHOUT LEGS 2 AND 3
Not a stage. A presence. It is there when the mask is broadcasting.
================================================================================

WHAT IS TRUE:
  The Sinochia fog waters have something in them. The locals call it
  the Warden. It is not a creature. It is a function — it maintains
  the fog, tracks what moves through it, and investigates things that
  broadcast. The mask broadcasts. The Warden has been following them
  since the margin.

  The player will become aware of it as:
    - A displacement in the fog. A shape slightly denser than the
      surrounding grey, at the edge of visibility.
    - The water surface disturbing slightly as something large passes
      beneath the hull.
    - The mask's vibration changing character — less directional,
      more reactive.

  The Warden is not attacking. It is investigating. It will keep
  investigating until the player does something about it or it
  decides they are not worth further attention.

WHAT THE PLAYER DOESN'T KNOW:
  - What it is.
  - Whether it is dangerous.
  - That the mask recognizes it — the change in vibration is the
    mask reacting, not malfunctioning.
  - That covering the masthead reduces the signal and reduces the
    Warden's interest. Fenn knows this. He will not volunteer it.
    If asked "how do we make it stop following us," he will say:
    "Reduce what it is following."
  - That letting the mask respond — not muffling it, not running,
    just holding position and letting the vibration do what it does —
    produces a low tone that the Warden recognizes. This is the
    best long-term outcome. Nobody aboard knows this. The player
    has to discover it by trying it, or by reasoning from what
    the mask has done before.

HOW THE PLAYER FINDS OUT WHAT IT IS:
  They don't, fully. Not this voyage. What they can learn:
    - Fenn, if asked: "I have heard of it. The Sinochia clans
      call it the Warden. I have never seen it. I was told it
      responds to things that don't belong."
    - Dale, if they reach her and ask: "It's been here longer
      than the fog. It doesn't kill. It watches. If it decides
      you belong here, it leaves you alone. If it decides you
      don't, it makes passage very difficult."
    - The mask itself: its behavior is information. The player
      has to read it.

WHAT HAPPENS IF THEY IGNORE IT:
  The Warden does not attack. But it does not leave. It will
  follow them to Dale's anchorage. Dale will see it and know
  immediately that the player did not resolve it. Her reaction:
  "You brought it here. That is going to be a problem when
  the patrol comes through."

  The Warden's presence at the anchorage during the Sinochia
  patrol is a complication — the patrol knows what the Warden
  following a vessel means. It means the vessel does not belong.

WHAT HAPPENS IF THEY HANDLE IT WELL:
  The Warden withdraws. The fog thins slightly around the vessel.
  Passage becomes easier. On the return trip, it does not reappear.
  Future fog voyages: it will recognize the vessel and not investigate.


================================================================================
LEG 3 — ALONE IN THE FOG: THREE HOURS TO DALE
No Fenn. No reliable compass. Player navigates.
================================================================================

WHAT IS TRUE:
  Fenn stops the vessel. His charts end here. He gives them what
  he has: a general heading (northwest by north), the water
  temperature trick, and the knowledge that Bramble has been
  watching him work.

  Then he waits. He will be at this position when they return.
  He is exposed here — if the tracking chip was planted, an Acme
  cutter is at the margin. Fenn does not know about the chip.
  The player may or may not have told him.

WHAT THE PLAYER DOESN'T KNOW:
  - Daring's chart, if it degraded, is now unreadable. They are
    navigating from memory and from what Bramble observed.
  - The mask's pull is northeast. Dale is northwest. These are
    nearly opposite. The mask is a useful negative reference —
    keep the pull to starboard and they are on the correct heading —
    but only if the player figures this out. It is not obvious.
  - Dale has not stayed put. She moved once, two days ago, when
    a Sinochia patrol came closer than expected. She left a marker
    at the original position — a specific knot pattern on a mooring
    line at the anchorage dock — and moved to a secondary position
    ninety minutes further northwest. The marker tells anyone who
    knows to look where she went. The player does not know to look
    for it unless they arrive at an empty anchorage and search.

WHAT BRAMBLE CAN DO:
  If the player directed Bramble to watch Fenn during Leg 2, he
  has the pattern. He can hold the heading using water temperature
  and dead reckoning. He is not as good as Fenn. He will drift
  slightly. But he can do it.

  If the player did not direct Bramble to watch, he does not have
  the pattern. He can still try — he is a competent navigator —
  but the drift will be worse and the player will need to correct
  more actively.

  Either way, Bramble does not take over. He does what the player
  tells him to do. If the player doesn't give him direction, he
  holds the last known heading and says nothing.

ARRIVING AT DALE'S POSITION:
  If they navigate correctly: the anchorage. Three structures on
  a low rock shelf. One lamp in the chart house window.
  Dale is not there. The lamp is on a timer — a slow-burn wick
  she set before she moved. The mooring line has a specific knot
  pattern. Whether the player notices and reads it correctly
  determines whether they find her quickly or spend time searching.

  If they navigate with significant drift: they miss the anchorage
  entirely. The fog looks the same in every direction. They need
  to backtrack — which means returning through the area where the
  Warden was, which may or may not still be watching.

DALE'S SECONDARY POSITION:
  Ninety minutes further northwest. A smaller rock shelf, no
  structures, just Dale in a small tender with the waterproofed
  case and three days of supplies. She has been watching the fog.

  When they find her:
  She looks at the vessel. She looks at the masthead. She looks
  at the player.

  DALE: "You found the knot."

  If they didn't find the knot and just searched until they
  found her:

  DALE: "You didn't find the knot. How long did that cost you?"

  She is not unkind. She is practical. Time is the resource
  and she wants to know how much they have left.


================================================================================
DALE AT THE ANCHORAGE — WHAT SHE KNOWS AND WHAT SHE WANTS
================================================================================

WHAT IS TRUE:
  Dale has one year of fog margin documentation in the waterproofed
  case. She has already made Sinochia copies and left them at the
  original anchorage. She is not leaving empty-handed and she is
  not leaving without the primary records.

  She has a ninety-minute window before the Sinochia patrol comes
  through — less if the Warden is still following the vessel, because
  the patrol reads the Warden's behavior.

  She knows the fog. She can navigate the return to Fenn's position
  faster than the player got here. She will do this from the bow,
  without instruments, without explaining herself.

WHAT THE PLAYER DOESN'T KNOW:
  - The Sinochia patrol is not hostile by default. It is hostile
    to vessels that don't belong. If the Warden is still following
    them, the patrol will treat them as trespassers. If the Warden
    has withdrawn, the patrol will treat them as unknown but not
    immediately threatening.
  - Dale has a relationship with one of the patrol clan members.
    She will not use it unless the player is about to make things
    significantly worse. She will not mention it exists unless asked
    directly: "Do you know anyone on the patrol?" Her answer: "One
    person. I would prefer not to need them."
  - The records contain more than fog margin data. Dale knows this.
    She will not say it here. She will say it when they are clear
    of the fog and she has had time to decide how much to trust
    the player.

WHAT DALE WILL AND WON'T SAY:
  - Ask about the records: "A year of margin movement. The pattern
    has a source. I know roughly where. I don't know what."
  - Ask about the mask: "It's been pulling northeast since you
    entered the fog. That direction is where the fog is moving
    away from. I don't think that's a coincidence."
  - Ask about the Warden: "It doesn't kill. It watches. Did you
    resolve it or is it still following you?"
  - Ask about the patrol: "Ninety minutes. Less if you brought
    the Warden with you. Can we go?"

DECISIONS THE PLAYER MIGHT MAKE HERE:
  - Do they leave immediately or take time to look around the
    anchorage? (Looking around costs time but may find something
    useful — Dale left more than the knot marker.)
  - Do they tell Dale about the Warden situation honestly?
  - Do they ask about the patrol contact, or leave it alone?
  - Do they ask about the records now, or wait?


================================================================================
THE RETURN — WHAT WAITS AT THE MARGIN
================================================================================

WHAT IS TRUE:
  Dale navigates the return. Faster. She knows these waters.

  At the margin, one of two things is waiting, depending on the
  tracking chip decision made before departure:

  IF THE CHIP WAS PLANTED:
    An Acme cutter is holding position just outside the fog margin.
    It has been there for two days. It is not going to enter the fog.
    It is waiting for the player to come out.
    Three crew, two Shinyman Enforcers, one specialist. Their job
    is to take Dale and the records. Not the player, not the vessel —
    just Dale and the records. They will offer to let the player go
    if they hand over Dale. They will not honor this offer.

  IF THE CHIP WAS NOT PLANTED:
    The margin is clear. Open water. Liege is a week behind them.
    Fenn sets course for the rendezvous. The threat is behind them,
    for now.

  Either way, Fenn is at his position when they return. He looks at
  Dale for a moment.

  FENN: "You look well."
  DALE: "I look like someone who has been in a fog anchorage for
  three days. Let's go."

WHAT THE PLAYER DOESN'T KNOW (IF CHIP WAS PLANTED):
  - The cutter has been there long enough that the crew is tired
    and cold. They are not at their best.
  - The specialist is not a fighter. She is there to handle the
    records. If the player separates her from the enforcers, she
    will not resist.
  - Fenn has a signal flare. Foundation-coded. If fired, a
    Foundation vessel will respond within four hours. He has not
    mentioned this. He will use it if the situation is bad enough,
    without asking permission.

================================================================================
OPEN THREADS AFTER THIS VOYAGE
================================================================================

  - The Warden: how the player handled it determines how future
    fog voyages go. It remembers.
  - The Sinochia copies: at the original anchorage. Cannot be
    destroyed without entering Sinochia waters.
  - Dale's records contain more than fog margin data. She has
    not said what. She will, when she trusts the player enough.
  - The pre-Alliance mark: Dale confirms it is the source of
    the fog movement. The mask confirms the direction. R242
    is the next leg.
  - The Liege patrol officer who cleared them on day one:
    logged the vessel and heading. Liege intelligence has a
    record of this departure. Not an immediate threat — but
    if the customs flag from Harwick Dray was never resolved,
    this log entry will surface eventually.
  - Fenn's signal flare: the player may not know it exists.
    If he used it, a Foundation vessel is coming. If he didn't,
    it is still there.
