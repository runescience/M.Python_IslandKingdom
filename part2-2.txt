================================================================================
PART 2 — THE FOG VOYAGE
Five Stages: Liege to Wendy Dale
================================================================================

STRUCTURE OVERVIEW
------------------
Fenn navigates the first half. He knows the approach corridors, the old
Alliance survey lines, the safe headings. He does not know what is inside
the fog — nobody does, except people who live there.

At the midpoint he stops. He has taken them as far as he can. From here,
Dale's position is three hours deeper into the fog, and the only way to
reach it is to go in without a reliable chart.

Five stages across this arc. Each one has a distinct character.

  STAGE 1 — EXPLORATION: Open Water (Liege to the fog margin, one week)
  STAGE 2 — TRICK: The Margin (the fog edge, where things are not what they seem)
  STAGE 3 — CONFLICT: The Interior (something in the fog has noticed the mask)
  STAGE 4 — RESOLUTION: The Handoff (Fenn's limit, Dale's position, the choice)
  STAGE 5 — REWARD: Dale and the Records (what they came for, and what it costs)

Travel time: approximately one week from Liege to the fog margin
(Liege 03,04 to Sinochia 03,05 — one hex, one week's sailing), then
several hours inside. Fenn is aboard for all of it.


================================================================================
STAGE 1 — EXPLORATION: THE DAGNARIUS PASSAGE
================================================================================

WHAT THIS IS:
  Open water. One week of sailing from Liege. Alliance waters
  thinning to open sea as they approach the Sinochia margin.
  The players have time to breathe, to talk, to notice things. This is the
  stage where the world shows them what they are sailing into before they
  are inside it.

SETTING:
  One week of sailing. Alliance waters for the first two days, then
  open sea. The Sinochia coastal current begins pushing against the
  hull from the southwest around day three. The mask starts vibrating
  around day four — subtle, toward the fog. The light changes in the
  final two days. Flatter. Less shadow. The horizon looks wrong in a
  way that is hard to name.

FENN'S BEHAVIOR:
  He navigates from the bow. He does not explain his choices. He makes
  small corrections to heading that the players can observe but not
  account for — there is no visible reason to adjust course, but he
  adjusts it anyway, and the adjustment is always correct.

  On the second evening, if the players ask him about the fog, he will
  say one thing:

  FENN: "The margin is not a wall. It is a gradient. You will not know
  you are in it until you have been in it for an hour. By then the
  question is not whether to enter — it is how to move inside it."

  He does not elaborate.

THE MASK:
  On the second day, the mask begins vibrating at irregular intervals.
  Not toward Liege — toward the fog. The vibration is subtle enough that
  only someone watching for it will notice. Bramble notices. He does not
  say anything. He watches the masthead the way you watch a compass that
  is starting to drift.

ENCOUNTER — THE LIEGE PATROL:
  A Liege naval patrol vessel comes alongside on day one or two.
  Routine clearance check. Fenn handles it. The papers are good.
  The vessel is logged and cleared. This is unremarkable.

WHAT THE PLAYERS LEARN HERE:
  - Fenn has Foundation-level documentation. He is more connected than
    he presents.
  - The mask is responding to something ahead. Whatever is in the fog
    is already aware of them, or will be.
  - Alliance waters give way to open sea. A week of sailing before
    the fog margin. Time to prepare, ask questions, watch Fenn work.

REWARD (STAGE 1):
  Information. The players have a clear picture of what they are sailing
  toward. They have seen Fenn's competence. They have a day of calm water
  to prepare, check equipment, and decide how they want to handle what
  comes next.


================================================================================
STAGE 2 — TRICK: THE MARGIN
================================================================================

WHAT THIS IS:
  The fog edge. This is where the world starts lying to you. Not
  dramatically — not monsters and lightning. Quietly. The kind of lying
  that makes you doubt your instruments before you doubt the fog.

SETTING:
  The fog margin is not a wall. Fenn was right. It is a gradient — a
  thickening of the air that happens over the course of an hour until
  the horizon is gone and the light is sourceless and flat. The water
  is calm. Too calm. No chop, no swell. The surface looks like grey
  glass. Sound carries differently — voices from the deck sound close
  even when the speaker is at the bow.

  The fog smells of salt and something else. Cold stone. The smell of
  a room that has been closed for a long time.

THE TRICK — ECHO VESSELS:
  At the margin, the players will see other vessels. Shapes in the fog.
  Sails. Running lights. The silhouettes of ships moving on parallel
  courses.

  They are not there.

  The fog margin produces acoustic and visual echoes of vessels that
  have passed through it — not ghosts, not supernatural in the dramatic
  sense, but a property of the fog itself. The echoes are imperfect.
  A vessel that passed through three days ago will appear as a slightly
  wrong version of itself — the rigging slightly off, the running lights
  the wrong color, the heading slightly inconsistent with the wind.

  The trick: one of the echo vessels is the players' own ship. A version
  of Hound's Dream, slightly wrong, running a parallel course fifty yards
  to port. It will be visible for approximately ten minutes before the
  fog swallows it.

  Fenn sees it. He does not react. He has seen this before.

  If the players ask him about it:

  FENN: "The margin shows you what has passed through. Sometimes it
  shows you what is about to. I have never been able to tell which
  is which."

  He adjusts course by two degrees and does not look at the echo again.

THE NAVIGATION PROBLEM:
  Inside the margin, the players' compass begins to drift. Not wildly —
  just a degree or two of inconsistency that compounds over time. Fenn
  navigates by dead reckoning and by a set of small personal marks he
  has made on his own charts — distances from known fog features, timing
  of current changes, the behavior of the water surface at specific
  depths.

  He is doing something the players cannot fully replicate. They can
  observe it. They can learn from it. But they cannot do it themselves
  yet.

  Bramble's navigation bonus applies here: he can track Fenn's corrections
  and begin to understand the pattern. This will matter in Stage 4.

THE MASK ESCALATES:
  Inside the margin, the mask's vibration becomes directional. It is
  pointing somewhere specific — not toward Dale's position, which is
  ahead, but off to the northeast. Toward the pre-Alliance mark on
  Daring's chart.

  The mask is pulling toward something. The players can follow the pull
  or ignore it. Fenn will not follow it — his job is Dale, not the
  mask's pull. But it is there, and it is getting stronger.

  DM NOTE: The pre-Alliance mark is not accessible this voyage. It is
  a thread for later. The mask's pull is information, not a directive.
  Do not let it derail the mission — but do not let the players forget
  it either.

WHAT THE PLAYERS LEARN HERE:
  - The fog is not navigable by standard instruments. It requires
    knowledge that Fenn has and they don't — yet.
  - The echo vessels are a property of the margin, not a threat. But
    they are disorienting, and disorientation is the fog's primary weapon.
  - The mask is pulling toward something that is not their destination.
    The Lignum Vitae relic has its own pull inside these waters.

REWARD (STAGE 2):
  Passage. They are through the margin and into the interior. Fenn's
  navigation has held. The compass drift is manageable. They are where
  they need to be, and they have learned something about how the fog works.


================================================================================
STAGE 3 — CONFLICT: THE INTERIOR
================================================================================

WHAT THIS IS:
  Something in the fog has noticed the mask. This is the conflict Fenn
  warned them about. It is not an Acme retrieval team — that threat is
  behind them, in open water. This is something older, and it is not
  hostile in the way a person is hostile. It is territorial.

SETTING:
  The interior of the fog is quieter than the margin. The echo vessels
  are gone. The water is still. The fog is thick enough that the bow
  is barely visible from the stern. Sound is muffled except for the
  creak of the rigging, which is very loud.

  The light is wrong. It is midday but the light is the color of late
  afternoon. There are no shadows.

THE THING IN THE FOG — THE WARDEN:
  The Sinochia fog waters have a guardian. Not a creature in the
  conventional sense — more accurately, a presence. The locals call
  it the Warden. It is not malevolent. It is a function: it maintains
  the fog, it tracks what moves through it, and it responds to things
  that do not belong.

  The mask does not belong. The mask is broadcasting — pre-Alliance
  resonance, the Lignum Vitae pull toward the mark on Daring's chart.
  The Warden has been tracking them since the margin. Now it moves
  to investigate.

WHAT THE WARDEN DOES:
  It does not attack. It circles. The players will become aware of it
  as a displacement in the fog — a shape that is slightly denser than
  the surrounding grey, moving at the edge of visibility. It is large.
  The water surface disturbs slightly as it passes beneath the hull.

  The mask responds to it. The vibration changes character — less
  directional, more urgent. The Lignum Vitae relic is reacting to
  something it recognizes. Whatever the Warden is, the mask has
  encountered something like it before.

THE CONFLICT — THREE OPTIONS:

  OPTION A — SILENCE THE MASK (Trick/Skill):
    The players can attempt to dampen the mask's broadcast. They cannot
    remove it, but they can cover it — heavy canvas, rope, anything that
    physically muffles the masthead. This will not stop the Warden from
    knowing they are there, but it will reduce the signal enough that
    the Warden loses specific interest.

    Fenn knows this. If asked, he will tell them. He will not volunteer it.

    Mechanical: A successful seamanship or rigging check. Bramble can
    assist. If successful, the Warden circles twice more and then
    withdraws. If failed, the Warden moves closer.

  OPTION B — HOLD COURSE AND WAIT (Endurance):
    The Warden is not attacking. It is investigating. If the players
    hold course, maintain speed, and do not react to it, it will
    eventually determine that they are passing through rather than
    stopping, and it will withdraw.

    This requires nerve. The Warden will come close enough that its
    displacement wave rocks the hull. The fog will thicken around the
    vessel for approximately twenty minutes. It will feel like being
    watched by something very large in a very small room.

    Mechanical: A morale or resolve check for the crew. Bramble holds.
    Any NPC crew members may not.

  OPTION C — ACKNOWLEDGE IT (Roleplay/Lore):
    If the players allow the mask to respond — stop trying to muffle
    it, hold position, let the vibration do what it is doing — the
    mask will produce a low tone. Not music. A frequency. The Warden
    will stop circling.

    This is the correct option in the long term. It establishes a
    relationship between the players and the Warden that will matter
    in future fog voyages. But it requires trusting the mask's nature,
    which is not a small thing.

    Fenn does not know about this option. He will watch it happen
    with the expression of a man revising his understanding of
    something he thought he understood.

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER:
  Regardless of which option the players choose, the Warden withdraws.
  The fog thins slightly. The water surface settles. The mask's vibration
  returns to its directional pull — toward the pre-Alliance mark, steady,
  insistent.

  Fenn exhales. Just once. Then he adjusts course and says nothing.

WHAT THE PLAYERS LEARN HERE:
  - The fog has a guardian. It is not hostile, but it is not neutral
    either. It responds to what the mask broadcasts.
  - The Lignum Vitae mask has a relationship with the Warden that
    predates the Alliance. It has history in these waters.
  - How they handled the Warden will affect future fog voyages. The
    Warden remembers.

REWARD (STAGE 3):
  Passage through the interior. The Warden has withdrawn. The route
  to Dale's position is clear. And if they chose Option C, they have
  something more: a tentative acknowledgment from the oldest thing
  in these waters that they are not entirely unwelcome.


================================================================================
STAGE 4 — RESOLUTION: THE HANDOFF
================================================================================

WHAT THIS IS:
  Fenn's limit. He has brought them as far as he can. From here, the
  navigation requires knowledge he does not have. This is the stage
  where the players have to operate without their most competent NPC,
  and where what they have learned in Stages 1-3 becomes the resource
  they use to proceed.

SETTING:
  A specific point in the fog interior — Fenn knows it by a combination
  of elapsed time, current behavior, and a personal mark on his chart.
  There is nothing visually distinctive about this location. The fog
  looks the same as it has for the last two hours. But Fenn stops the
  vessel.

FENN'S LIMIT:
  He brings them to a stop and turns to face the players. He is not
  apologetic. He is precise.

  FENN: "This is as far as my charts go. Dale's position is three
  hours deeper on a heading of roughly northwest by north — but
  roughly is the problem. The fog shifts the approach. I have never
  been to her position. I know the general direction. I do not know
  the specific route."

  He pauses.

  FENN: "Your compass will drift another two to three degrees per
  hour from here. You correct for it by watching the water surface —
  the current runs slightly warmer on the correct heading. Not much.
  A degree of temperature. But it is consistent."

  He looks at Bramble.

  FENN: "Your navigator has been watching me work. He knows the
  pattern. He can hold the heading if he trusts what he has learned."

  He looks back at the players.

  FENN: "I will hold position here. When you have Dale, come back
  on the reciprocal heading. I will be here."

  He says it with the certainty of a man who has waited in worse places.

THE NAVIGATION CHALLENGE:
  Three hours of fog navigation without Fenn. The compass drifts.
  The current temperature differential is real but subtle. Bramble
  can track it — his navigation bonus applies, and what he observed
  in Stage 2 gives him the pattern.

  The mask helps. Its directional pull toward the pre-Alliance mark
  is northeast; Dale's position is northwest. The players can use the
  mask as a negative reference — if the pull is directly to starboard,
  they are on the correct heading. If it shifts forward or aft, they
  have drifted.

  This is not a guaranteed success. It is a skill challenge with
  meaningful failure states:
    - Minor drift: They arrive at Dale's position an hour late. She
      is still there, but she has been watching the fog for signs of
      pursuit and she is wound tight.
    - Major drift: They miss Dale's position entirely and have to
      backtrack. This costs time and brings them back through the
      area where the Warden withdrew — which may or may not still
      be watching.

WHAT THE PLAYERS LEARN HERE:
  - Fenn's competence has a limit. He is not omniscient. He is
    experienced, and experience has edges.
  - The mask is a navigation tool as well as a liability. Its
    directional pull can be used as a reference bearing.
  - Bramble is more capable than he presents. He has been learning
    the whole voyage. This is where that pays off.

REWARD (STAGE 4):
  They find Dale's position. The navigation worked — imperfectly,
  probably, but it worked. They are three hours deeper into the fog
  than anyone without local knowledge should be able to reach. That
  is not nothing.


================================================================================
STAGE 5 — REWARD: DALE AND THE RECORDS
================================================================================

WHAT THIS IS:
  The mission objective. But the reward is not just Dale and her charts —
  it is what the charts contain, and what that means for everything the
  players have been moving toward since the harbor pylon.

SETTING:
  Dale's position is a small Sinochia clan anchorage — three structures
  on a low rock shelf, barely above the waterline, accessible only from
  the water. The fog is thinner here, close to the rock. The structures
  are old. One of them is a chart house — a working space, not a home.
  Dale has been using it for a year.

  A single lamp is burning in the chart house window. She has been
  watching for them.

WENDY DALE — FIRST IMPRESSION:
  She is not what the players might have expected from Fenn's description.
  She is not frightened. She is not grateful. She is practical, slightly
  impatient, and carrying a waterproofed case that she does not put down.

  She looks at the vessel. She looks at the masthead. She looks at the
  players.

  DALE: "You're late. I've been watching the fog for two hours."

  She steps aboard without waiting to be invited.

  DALE: "Fenn is at the midpoint?"

  You confirm it.

  DALE: "Good. We need to move. Sinochia patrol comes through this
  anchorage every six hours. We have approximately ninety minutes."

THE RECORDS:
  The waterproofed case contains one year of fog margin documentation.
  Dale will not open it here. She will not describe its contents in
  detail. What she will say, if pressed:

  DALE: "The margin has been moving consistently northwest for eleven
  months. Not drifting — moving. There is a pattern to it. The pattern
  has a source. I know approximately where the source is. I do not know
  what it is."

  She looks at the masthead again.

  DALE: "That thing on your mast. It has been pulling toward the
  northeast since you entered the fog. I could feel it from the
  anchorage. Whatever it is responding to — that is the direction
  the fog is moving away from."

  She says it matter-of-factly, the way you say something you have
  been sitting with for a long time and are relieved to finally say
  out loud.

  DALE: "I think they are connected. The mark on the old charts and
  whatever is in your masthead. I think they are part of the same
  thing. I do not know what that thing is."

  She picks up the case.

  DALE: "That is why Acme wants my records destroyed. Not because
  of the fog. Because of what the fog is moving away from."

THE COST:
  Before they leave, Dale goes back into the chart house. She comes
  out with a second, smaller case. She sets it on the dock.

  DALE: "Sinochia copies. Everything I documented, duplicated. I
  am leaving these here. If something happens to me and the primary
  records, the copies stay in Sinochia waters. They will find their
  way to the clan archivists eventually."

  She looks at the players.

  DALE: "I am not leaving Sinochia empty-handed. I am leaving with
  the records and with the knowledge. The copies stay. That is the
  arrangement I made with myself when I decided to go."

  She steps aboard. She does not look back at the chart house.

  DALE: "Now let's go find Fenn."

WHAT THE PLAYERS HAVE AT THE END OF STAGE 5:
  - Wendy Dale, aboard, with the primary fog records
  - Confirmation that the mask's pull and the fog's movement are
    connected — both pointing toward the pre-Alliance mark on
    Daring's chart
  - A clearer picture of what Acme is actually protecting: not
    just the fog charts, but the source of the fog movement itself
  - The knowledge that Sinochia copies exist — which means the
    records cannot be fully destroyed even if Acme gets to Dale
  - A ninety-minute window before the Sinochia patrol arrives

REWARD (STAGE 5):
  The mission objective, plus the thread that makes the next leg
  inevitable. The pre-Alliance mark on Daring's chart is not just
  a cartographic curiosity. It is the source. And now two people
  aboard the vessel — Dale and the mask — are pointing at it.


================================================================================
RETURN PASSAGE — NOTES
================================================================================

The return to Fenn's position is faster. Dale knows these waters.
She navigates from the bow without instruments, by current feel and
fog behavior. She does not explain what she is doing. She just does it.

Fenn, when they reach him, looks at Dale for a long moment.

FENN: "You look well."

DALE: "I look like someone who has been in a fog anchorage for
three days. Let's go."

The return through the margin is uneventful. The echo vessels appear
again. Dale ignores them. The Warden does not reappear — or if it does,
it does not make itself known.

They clear the margin at dusk on the second day. Open water. The fog
is behind them. Open water. Liege is a week back.

Fenn sets course for the rendezvous point. He does not say where
that is yet.

Dale opens the waterproofed case for the first time since boarding.
She checks the records. She closes it again. She looks at the masthead.

She does not say anything. But she is thinking.


================================================================================
DM NOTES — STAGE MECHANICS SUMMARY
================================================================================

STAGE 1 (Exploration):
  - Dagnarius cutter encounter: removed — wrong geography. Replaced
    with a routine Liege patrol clearance on day one. Unremarkable.
  - Mask begins responding. Bramble notices.
  - Reward: information and a clear day to prepare.

STAGE 2 (Trick):
  - Echo vessels: disorienting, not dangerous. The players' own echo
    is the key image. Fenn's non-reaction is the tell.
  - Compass drift: navigation challenge begins. Bramble starts
    tracking Fenn's corrections.
  - Mask pull toward pre-Alliance mark: plant the thread, do not
    resolve it.
  - Reward: passage through the margin.

STAGE 3 (Conflict):
  - The Warden: three resolution options. Option C (acknowledge it)
    is the best long-term choice but requires trusting the mask.
  - No combat. The Warden is not an enemy. Treating it as one is
    a mistake with future consequences.
  - Reward: passage through the interior, and potentially a
    relationship with the Warden.

STAGE 4 (Resolution):
  - Fenn's limit: he stops here. Players navigate the last three
    hours without him.
  - Bramble + mask as navigation tools. Failure states are time
    costs, not dead ends.
  - Reward: Dale's position reached.

STAGE 5 (Reward):
  - Dale is competent and unsentimental. She is not a rescue object.
    She has already made her own arrangements (the copies).
  - The connection between the mask and the fog movement is confirmed
    by Dale independently. This is the payoff for everything since
    the harbor pylon.
  - The ninety-minute window creates urgency without combat.
  - Reward: Dale, the records, and the thread that makes R242 inevitable.

OPEN THREADS ADDED THIS STAGE:
  - The Warden: remembers how the players handled it. Will respond
    differently on future fog voyages depending on Stage 3 choice.
  - The Sinochia copies: exist, in the clan anchorage. Cannot be
    destroyed by Acme without entering Sinochia waters directly.
  - The pre-Alliance mark: confirmed as the source of the fog
    movement by Dale's independent documentation. R242 is next.
  - The officer who logged the vessel: Liege naval registry. Not a
    threat now. Becomes one if the Harwick Dray customs flag was
    never resolved.
