================================================================================
MODULE: THE HIRED TRIP & THE CROSSING
Chapter 1 — A milk run that isn't.
================================================================================

THE HOOK
--------
Admiral Hall — the player's despised noble relative — has a job. A sealed
leather pouch of "peace treaties" needs to go from Port City A (Akamara,
03,03) to Duchy B (Liege, 03,04). Simple courier work. One hex. One week.
He is paying well. He is being vague about why he needs someone he
personally dislikes for a job this routine.

The player takes it or they don't. If they take it, they leave at night,
from a hidden cove fifteen miles down the coast, to catch the Great Trade
Current unseen by local pirates. Hall's instructions. Not negotiable.

That is the last thing that goes according to plan.

THE STAKES
----------
If the player refuses:
  Hall finds another courier. The pouch reaches Liege. The player misses
  the event that changes everything. Hall does not forget that they said no.

If the player takes it and delivers cleanly:
  They never find out what was in the pouch. Hall thanks them. Pays them.
  Moves on. The player has a small amount of money and a large amount of
  questions.

If the player takes it and the Crossing happens:
  Everything changes. The pouch is gone. The player is somewhere they
  cannot chart. The mask is fused to their masthead. They are not going
  home the way they came.

The real stakes: the player is being used. Hall knows what is in the
pouch. He knows what the pouch will do. He is not telling them because
they would not go if he did. Whether that makes him a villain or a
strategist is a question the campaign will answer slowly.


================================================================================
RUMOR TABLE (roll d4 or pick)
================================================================================

Players can gather these in Akamara before departure — dockside, at the
harbor authority, from other captains. Not all are true.

1. TRUE — "The 100D boundary is under a Sector Silence. King's orders.
   Long-range carrier pigeons are dark. Signal towers too. Nobody knows
   why. It went up three days ago."
   Source: harbor authority clerk, any dockside worker.

2. TRUE — "The Carrier Nashua is running the deep water corridor. She's
   a warship galleon — full complement, royal guards aboard. She's been
   stopping everything that crosses the 100D line."
   Source: any captain who came in from the deep water in the last week.

3. HALF-TRUE — "There are pirates working the Trade Current. Fire-ships.
   They've been hitting the big galleons — too armored to board, so they
   burn them instead."
   True: the pirates exist and use fire-ships. False: they are not
   random. They are targeting the Nashua specifically. The player's
   vessel is not their target — unless it gets in the way.

4. LIE — "Hall's courier runs are always clean. He's done this a hundred
   times. Whatever he's sending, it's routine."
   Source: anyone who works for Hall or owes him a favor. This is not
   a deliberate lie — they believe it. They are wrong.


================================================================================
LOCATIONS (7)
================================================================================

--- 1. THE HIDDEN COVE (departure point, night) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
Fifteen miles down the coast from Akamara. Rocky approach, no lights.
The Trade Current is audible before it is visible — a low, constant
pressure against the hull. Smells of deep water and cold stone. Hall
is not here. His instructions are.

What players can do here:
  - Inspect the sealed pouch before departure. It is leather, waxed,
    heavy for its size. The seal is Hall's — unbroken. If they break
    the seal: the pouch contains a leather case inside a leather case.
    The inner case is locked. The lock is not a standard lock. It is
    something older. They cannot open it without tools they do not have.
    Hall will know the outer seal was broken. He will not say anything
    about it. He will remember.
  - Check the vessel before departure. Everything is in order. Hall
    provisioned it himself. If they look carefully: the provisions are
    better than a one-week run requires. Enough for three weeks. Hall
    planned for something going wrong. He did not tell them this.
  - Catch the Trade Current. This is the last easy thing.

--- 2. THE 100D BOUNDARY (deep water, day 3) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
Open ocean. The horizon is clean in every direction. The Trade Current
is running well. Then: a signal tower on a distant rock, dark. Another
one, further along, also dark. The silence is wrong. The sea is not
empty — there are vessels out here — but nothing is communicating.

What players can do here:
  - Try to signal the towers. No response. The towers are operational —
    the lamps are there, the mechanisms are intact — but nobody is
    answering. Sector Silence means the operators have orders not to
    respond to anything.
  - Ask other vessels what is happening. Most will not stop. One small
    trader, running back toward Akamara, will say: "The Nashua is ahead.
    She's stopping everything. Turn back if you can." He does not stop
    to explain further. He is afraid.
  - Continue forward. The Nashua is ahead. There is no route around her
    that does not add three days. The player has a deadline.

--- 3. THE CARRIER NASHUA (deep water, day 3-4) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
A warship galleon. The largest vessel the player has likely seen. She
sits across the Trade Current like a wall. Signal flags flying: heave
to, prepare for boarding. Royal guards visible on the rail. The hull
is painted black below the waterline. She smells of cannon smoke and
salt and something chemical — the fire-retardant the navy uses on
warship decks.

What players can do here:
  - Comply with the boarding order. The royal guards search the hold.
    They are looking for something specific — they do not say what.
    They find the pouch. They examine it. The outer seal is Hall's.
    They log it and return it. They are not looking for Hall's courier.
    They are looking for something else. The player is cleared and told
    to hold position until the Nashua moves.
  - Resist the boarding order. This is a bad idea. The Nashua has
    cannons. The player does not. Fenn will not help them resist a
    royal warship. If they try: they are boarded by force, the pouch
    is taken, and they are held in the brig. The Crossing still happens
    — but the player is not in control of their vessel when it does.
  - Talk to the interrogating officer. He is professional, not hostile.
    He will answer one question honestly if asked directly: "What are
    you looking for?" His answer: "Contraband biological material.
    Something that should not be in Alliance waters." He will not say
    more. He does not know more.
  - Look around while being held. The Nashua's lower decks are visible
    from the boarding area. There are crates stacked in the hold —
    marked with a symbol the player does not recognize. Not Alliance.
    Not any kingdom they know. The guards do not want them looking at
    the crates. They will be redirected if they stare too long.

GM NOTE — THE CRATES:
  The crates contain the relic — the thing that will fuse to the player's
  masthead. The Nashua intercepted a smuggling vessel three days ago and
  seized the cargo. The royal guards do not know what they have. The
  interrogating officer does not know what he has. Hall knows. Hall sent
  the player on this route knowing the Nashua would be here. He did not
  tell them this either.

--- 4. THE PIRATE ATTACK (deep water, day 4, while held alongside the Nashua) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
Fire. Not a gradual thing — immediate. A fire-ship comes out of the
dark on the Nashua's port side, moving fast, trailing flame. Then
another. The Nashua's crew is already moving — they have been watching
for this. The boarding party on the player's vessel is recalled. The
player is told to cast off and hold clear.

They do not cast off in time.

The fire-ships ram the Nashua. The lower decks flood with water and
thick smoke. The player's vessel is still alongside. The rigging is
tangled. The Nashua is listing.

What players can do here:
  - Cut the rigging. They have axes. The rigging is heavy and on fire
    in places. This takes time and costs them — burns, cuts, one crew
    member down if they are not careful. But it is the only way out.
  - Use the leather diving lungs / air bladders from the hold. Hall
    provisioned these. The player did not know why. Now they do. The
    smoke in the lower decks is survivable with the bladders. Without
    them, it is not.
  - Navigate the flooding. The Nashua's lower decks are filling fast.
    If the player needs to go below — to retrieve the pouch if it was
    taken, to find a crew member, to reach the crates — the flooding
    is the obstacle. Zero-visibility, rising water, structural collapse.
    The bladders help. A light helps more.
  - Get clear. This is the goal. The Nashua is not their problem. Their
    vessel is their problem. Get the rigging cut, get the hull clear,
    get into open water before the Nashua goes down or the fire spreads.

GM NOTE — THE CRATES AGAIN:
  In the chaos, one of the crates breaks open. The relic is loose in
  the flooding lower deck. The player may or may not see this. If they
  go below: they see something in the water that is not debris. It is
  moving toward the hull. Toward the waterline. Toward the direction of
  their vessel. They cannot stop it. They can only get out.

--- 5. THE ESCAPE (deep water, day 4, immediately after) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
Open water. Dark. The Nashua is burning behind them. The fire-ships
are gone — they hit and ran. The player's vessel is moving but the
rudder is damaged. The Trade Current is pushing them southwest. The
compass is spinning. A storm is building from the northwest — the
kind that comes up fast in deep water and does not apologize.

What players can do here:
  - Assess the damage. The rudder is cracked — functional but unreliable.
    The rigging is cut in places. One crew member is down (burns or
    smoke, not dead). The pouch is still aboard. The hold is intact.
  - Try to correct course. The rudder will not hold a precise heading.
    They can steer broadly but not accurately. The storm is going to
    push them regardless.
  - Ride out the storm. This is not optional. The storm hits. The
    player can make choices about how to ride it — reduce sail, sea
    anchor, run before it — but they cannot avoid it. The rudder
    snaps completely in the first hour. After that, they are at the
    mercy of the current.
  - Find the boarding ram / axes. Hall provisioned these too. If the
    collapsed rigging traps the vessel against debris, the ram is how
    they get free. Under archer fire from the pirates if the pirates
    are still in the area. They may not be. The player does not know.

The storm lasts two days. When it ends, they are somewhere they cannot
chart. The Trade Current is gone. The compass is still spinning. The
horizon looks wrong — the light is different here, the water color is
different, the birds are different. They are past the Great Reefs.
Nobody from the Alliance goes past the Great Reefs. The Alliance says
there is nothing past the Great Reefs. The Alliance is wrong.

--- 6. THE LOST AT SEA (deep water, day 7 — one week after departure) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
The storm is over. The vessel is drifting. The crew is exhausted. The
pouch is still in the hold. Then: the hold erupts with lightning.

Not a storm — the pouch. The inner case has opened. Something is
moving through the hull. The player can feel it in the deck planking —
a vibration that is not the sea. It moves toward the bow. Toward the
masthead. And then it stops.

The masthead is different. There is something on it now — a carved
wooden mask, dark wood, old, fused to the masthead as if it grew there.
The vessel is moving. Not drifting — moving. On a heading. The rudder
is responding but the player did not set the heading. Something else did.

The mask is in control. For now.

What players can do here:
  - Try to remove the mask. It will not come off. It is not attached
    with fasteners — it is part of the wood now. Cutting it would mean
    cutting the masthead. The vessel would lose the mast.
  - Try to change the heading. The rudder responds but the vessel
    corrects back to the original heading within minutes. The mask
    knows where it is going. The player does not.
  - Examine the mask. It is Lignum Vitae — a wood so dense it sinks.
    It should not be floating. It should not be on a masthead. The
    carving is a face — not human, not animal, something in between.
    The eyes are open. They have been open since it appeared. They
    do not blink.
  - Check the provisions. The food in the hold is wrong. The salt
    pork has gone grey. The hardtack is soft. The water in the casks
    smells stale. This happened in hours. The mask is doing this.
    They have enough for three days if they are careful. They need
    to find land.

GM NOTE — THE MASK'S PROPERTIES:
  The Lignum Vitae Mermaid Mask grants +50% speed increase and an
  unnatural +20% competence bonus to all skills. It is cursed with
  Storm Magnetism (attracts lightning) and Biological Drain (rots
  food, spoils water in 7-10 days unless boxed in lead or ceramic).
  The player does not know any of this yet. They will learn it by
  living with it.

  The mask is taking them somewhere specific. The heading is southwest.
  The player cannot override it for approximately one month. After that,
  the mask will release trajectory control — but it will still be on
  the masthead. It will still be watching.

--- 7. THE VACANT ISLAND (arrival, day 8-10) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
Land. Low, green, no visible settlement from the water. The mask
brings them into a natural harbor — the heading corrects itself
precisely as they approach, threading between rocks that would have
wrecked them if they had been steering. The mask knows this harbor.
It has been here before.

The island is not empty. It was occupied recently — the structures
are intact, the oil lamps have fuel, the gardens are overgrown but
not dead. Someone left in a hurry or was taken in a hurry. The crests
on the buildings are not Alliance. Not any kingdom the player knows.

What players can do here:
  - Find the boathouse. Hidden, partially submerged, accessible from
    the harbor. Inside: a Commercial Cargo Sloop — reinforced hull,
    two mounted cannons, ten shots. Better than their current vessel.
    The hull is sound. The rigging is intact. It has been waiting.
  - Find the cache. Uncut garnets, 150gp value. Weapons with a +1 to
    hit. Armor with +1 AC. Stored in a sealed room with a lock that
    is not difficult to pick. Hall's provisioning was good. This is
    better.
  - Find the oil-line self-destruct traps. They are not hidden — they
    are visible if the player looks at the structural supports. Someone
    built this place to be destroyed if necessary. The trigger mechanism
    is in the main building. The player can disarm it, use it, or
    leave it alone. Leaving it alone is a choice with consequences.
  - Find the unrecognized crests. They are everywhere — carved into
    doorframes, painted on the boathouse wall, stamped into the cache
    containers. The player cannot identify them. They are not in any
    Alliance reference. They are not Axis. They are something else.
    Something older. The mask's vibration changes slightly when the
    player is near the crests. It recognizes them.
  - Assess the food situation. The island has fresh water — a spring
    in the interior. The gardens have fruit, overgrown but edible.
    There are fish in the harbor. The Biological Drain problem is
    solvable here, temporarily. They need to solve it permanently
    before they leave.

GM NOTE — THE ISLAND'S OWNER:
  This outpost belongs to the Recurrent Foundation of the Hand —
  Admiral Hall's secret organization. Hall sent the player here
  deliberately. The mask brought them here because the mask knows
  what the Foundation is and where its outposts are. The player
  is standing in the middle of a conspiracy they do not yet understand.
  The island is the first piece of evidence. They will not understand
  what it means until much later.


================================================================================
THE MASK — PERSISTENT PRESENCE (not a room, a condition)
================================================================================

BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
A carved wooden face on the masthead. Dark wood. Open eyes. Warm to
the touch even in cold weather. The vibration is constant — not
unpleasant, but present. The crew notices it. They do not talk about
it. They have started orienting toward it when they are uncertain.

What it does:
  - +50% speed. The vessel moves faster than it should. The wake is
    wrong for the hull speed. Other vessels notice.
  - +20% competence. Everything the crew does, they do slightly better.
    Knots hold tighter. Navigation is more accurate. Repairs last longer.
    The player may not notice this for several sessions. It is subtle.
  - Storm Magnetism. Lightning finds the vessel. Not every storm —
    but in a storm, the lightning comes to them first. The crew will
    start to notice the pattern.
  - Biological Drain. Food rots. Water goes stale. The player has to
    solve this actively and continuously. It is not a one-time problem.
    It is a permanent condition of traveling with the mask.

What the player can do about the Biological Drain:
  - Tow a cargo raft fifty feet behind the vessel. Keeps provisions
    outside the mask's aura. Risks storm loss.
  - Line a storage box with lead sheets or iron plates. Shields contents
    from the drain. Heavy. Requires materials.
  - Use ceramic or glass containers for water. Non-organic barriers
    slow the drain significantly.
  - Fish and harvest rainwater. The mask's Storm Magnetism is a curse
    that can be turned into a water source. Canvas tarps across the
    deck, funneled into clean buckets.
  - Burn preservation spells daily if a spellcaster is aboard.

What the player cannot do:
  - Remove the mask. It is part of the masthead now.
  - Override the heading for approximately one month.
  - Explain to anyone in the Alliance what happened without sounding
    like they have lost their mind.


================================================================================
LOST AT SEA — WHAT IT MEANS
================================================================================

The player is past the Great Reefs. The Alliance says there is nothing
here. The Alliance has been lying for three hundred years.

The world is much larger than the Alliance knows. It is mostly ocean —
eighty percent ocean — and the other civilizations that survived the
cataclysm three hundred years ago have been developing independently
ever since. Some of them can reach Alliance waters now. The Alliance
masses do not know this. The player now knows this, because they are
standing in it.

The mask brought them here because this is where it needed to go.
The vacant island is one of the Foundation's outposts built to monitor
whatever the mask is connected to. The player has just become part of
that effort, whether they want to be or not.

Hall knew this was possible. He sent them anyway. He will have reasons.
The player will eventually get to ask him what they are.


================================================================================
FAILING FORWARD
================================================================================

The story does not end if the player fails. It gets more complicated.

FAIL: Player refuses Hall's contract.
  Hall finds another courier. The pouch reaches the Nashua's corridor.
  The Crossing happens to someone else. The mask is loose in the world
  without the player. Hall comes back to the player with a different
  ask — not a contract. A request. He needs someone who will not ask
  questions. He is running out of options.

FAIL: Player is in the Nashua's brig when the Crossing happens.
  The mask fuses to the Nashua's masthead instead of the player's.
  The Nashua goes off-course. The player is released in the chaos —
  the brig guard has other problems. They are on a warship that is
  now being controlled by an ancient entity, somewhere past the Great
  Reefs, with no vessel of their own. The boathouse on the vacant
  island still has the cargo sloop. Getting to it is the problem.

FAIL: Player loses the pouch before the Crossing.
  The Crossing still happens — the entity does not need the pouch to
  be intact, only to be near the player's vessel. The pouch was a
  container. The entity was the contents. The player just does not
  have the outer case anymore. Hall will want to know what happened
  to it. The player will have to explain.

FAIL: Player destroys the mask on the vacant island.
  They cannot. The Lignum Vitae is harder than any tool they have.
  But if they try — if they spend significant time and resources
  trying — the mask responds. The vibration changes. The eyes move.
  Just once. Just enough to make clear that it is aware of what they
  are doing. They stop trying. The mask goes back to normal. It
  remembers that they tried.

FAIL: Player triggers the self-destruct on the vacant island.
  The island burns. The boathouse survives — it is partially
  submerged and the fire does not reach it. The cache is gone.
  The crests are gone. The structures are gone. The Foundation
  loses an outpost. Hall will find out. He will not be pleased.
  The cargo sloop is still there. The spring is still there.
  The player can still leave. They just burned their only evidence
  of what this place was.


================================================================================
LOOT / REWARDS
================================================================================

THE SEALED POUCH (found at departure, carried through):
  The outer case is Hall's. The inner case is something older. After
  the Crossing, the inner case is open and empty. The outer case is
  intact. It is worth nothing as an object. It is worth everything
  as evidence — Hall sent them with this, knowing what was inside.
  The player has proof of that. Whether they use it is up to them.

THE CARGO SLOOP (found in the boathouse):
  Commercial Cargo Sloop. Reinforced hull. Two mounted cannons.
  Ten shots. Better than what they arrived in. The mask will transfer
  to this vessel's masthead if the player moves it — the mask goes
  where the player goes. The sloop is faster, more durable, and
  better armed. It is also now carrying the mask. The trade-off
  is permanent.

THE CACHE (found in the sealed room):
  Uncut garnets, 150gp. Weapons +1 to hit. Armor +1 AC. Enough to
  outfit the crew properly. The garnets are uncut — they need a
  jeweler to realize their value. The weapons and armor are
  immediately useful.

THE CRESTS (found throughout the island):
  Not an object. A question. The player has seen a symbol that does
  not appear in any Alliance reference. They will see it again. When
  they do, they will know they are in Foundation territory. This is
  the first piece of a map they do not know they are building.

THE MASK'S COMPETENCE BONUS (ongoing):
  Not an object. A condition. The player is better at everything now.
  They will not notice it immediately. They will notice it when they
  try something they would normally fail and succeed instead. The
  mask is not helping them. It is using them. The distinction matters
  later.

THE KNOWLEDGE THAT THE ALLIANCE LIED (earned by surviving):
  The world is bigger than the Alliance says. The Great Reefs are not
  a barrier — they are a border. The player is on the other side of
  it. Everything they were told about the world is incomplete at best
  and deliberately false at worst. This is not a reward that can be
  spent. It is a weight that changes how every future interaction lands.
