================================================================================
MODULE: THE FOG BETWEEN
Rescue Wendy Dale. Get her records to safety.
================================================================================

THE HOOK
--------
Commodore Fenn has a job: extract a woman named Wendy Dale from Sinochia
fog waters before Acme Corporation kills her. Dale has a year of records
documenting something wrong happening inside the fog. Acme wants those
records destroyed and Dale with them. Fenn needs a vessel, a crew, and
someone willing to sail into fog that compasses don't work in.

That's the player. The pay is good. The route is one week out, one week
back. Simple enough on paper.

THE STAKES
----------
If the player fails or doesn't go:
  Dale dies. Her records are destroyed. Whatever is causing the fog to
  move goes undocumented. Acme wins this round cleanly.

If the player succeeds:
  Dale and her records reach Ombreval. The Foundation gets the data.
  Whatever Acme is protecting stays exposed.

Secondary stake: the Acme periapt (if planted on the masthead) means
Zephyr has been reading their position daily. A retrieval team is already
moving. Success on the main mission doesn't mean safe.


================================================================================
RUMOR TABLE (roll d4 or pick)
================================================================================

Players can gather these in Liege the day before departure — dockside,
at the Mersling, from Farnsworth or Daring. Not all are true.

1. TRUE — "The fog margin around Sinochia has been moving. Approach lines
   that were reliable last season are inside the fog now. Navigators are
   refusing the northern corridors."
   Source: Farnsworth, Daring, any harbor worker.

2. TRUE — "Acme Corporation has had a man in Liege harbor for six weeks.
   Posing as a Dagnarius factor. Nobody knows what he's watching for."
   Source: Barkeep at the Mersling (Liege intelligence posted a bounty
   on Acme shipping information three days ago — word got around).

3. HALF-TRUE — "The Sinochia fog has a guardian. Sailors who've come back
   from the margin talk about something following their vessel. Most of
   them won't go back."
   True: the Warden exists. False: it doesn't follow vessels out of the
   fog. It stays inside. Sailors who say otherwise are misremembering
   fear as fact.

4. LIE — "Wendy Dale left Sinochia two weeks ago. Someone saw her in
   Dagnarius."
   Source: unknown. This is Acme disinformation — they want the player
   to go to Dagnarius instead of the fog. Dale is still in Sinochia.


================================================================================
LOCATIONS (6)
================================================================================

--- 1. HOUND'S DREAM (Fenn's vessel, the far pier) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
Dark hull, single mast, cleaner than it has any right to be for a vessel
at the far end of the far pier. Smells of tar and old rope. A folding
table at the stern with two cups already set out.

What players can do here:
  - Talk to Fenn. He answers direct questions. He does not volunteer.
  - Inspect the vessel before departure. If they look carefully at their
    own masthead, they may find the periapt — a small carved bone charm
    on a cord, looped over the masthead like a sailor's good-luck token.
    Warm to the touch if held. Faintly humming. Looks like a cheap trinket.
    If they don't look, they won't find it.
  - Prepare for the voyage: seal charts against moisture, stock supplies,
    ask Fenn what they need to know.

Fenn — wants: to complete the extraction cleanly, on schedule.
Speaks carefully. Will not be rushed. If pushed, he pushes back once
and then goes quiet.

--- 2. THE OPEN WATER (days 1-5, Liege to fog margin) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
Alliance waters thinning to open sea. The Sinochia coastal current
pushes against the hull from the southwest by day three. The horizon
flattens on day five. The light goes wrong — sourceless, no shadows —
before the fog is visible.

What players can do here:
  - Watch Fenn navigate. If they direct Bramble to study his methods,
    Bramble learns the pattern. This matters in the fog.
  - Notice the mask vibrating toward something that is not their heading.
    If they ask Fenn: "It responds to old things. That direction is not
    where we are going." He adjusts course. He watches the masthead.
  - Seal Daring's chart if they haven't. Once inside the fog, the ink
    degrades in two hours if unprotected. Nobody will tell them this.
  - Deal with the Mireille. See THE MIREILLE below.

Encounter — Liege naval patrol (day 1-2):
  Routine clearance. Fenn's papers are good. The vessel is logged and
  cleared. Unremarkable — unless the Harwick Dray customs flag was never
  resolved, in which case the patrol officer hesitates, checks twice,
  and files a secondary note. Not an arrest. A clock.

--- 3. THE FOG MARGIN (day 6, entry) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
Not a wall — a gradient. The horizon disappears over the course of an
hour. The water surface goes flat and grey, like old glass. Smells of
salt and cold stone, like a room closed for a long time. Sound carries
wrong — voices from the bow sound close even when the speaker is far.

What players can do here:
  - See echo vessels: shapes in the fog, ships that passed through
    before them, imperfectly replicated. Wrong rigging. Wrong light
    color. Headings that don't match the wind. Not threats.
  - See their own vessel echoed — slightly wrong, running parallel
    fifty yards to port. If they ask Fenn: "It means we have been noted."
  - Notice the compass drifting. Fenn corrects by dead reckoning and
    water behavior. He does not explain unless asked.

The Warden begins here. See THE WARDEN below.

GM NOTE — IF THE MIREILLE WAS RESOLVED BEFORE THE MARGIN:
  The Warden does not appear immediately. The fog thins slightly around
  the vessel on entry. Fenn notices. He says nothing. If asked: "The fog
  knows we are not a threat. That is unusual." The Warden still exists —
  it is watching — but it has already assessed the vessel via the Mireille
  and decided to wait rather than investigate. The player has one free
  pass. They should not know why.

GM NOTE — IF THE MIREILLE WAS NOT RESOLVED:
  Run the Warden encounter as written below. The fog has not pre-assessed
  the vessel. The Warden investigates from scratch.

--- 4. FENN'S LIMIT (fog interior, day 6 midpoint) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
The fog is the same in every direction. No landmarks. The bow barely
visible from the stern. The creak of rigging is very loud. Fenn stops
the vessel.

What happens:
  Fenn's charts end here. He gives the player what he has: a general
  heading (northwest by north), the water temperature trick (warmer
  current on the correct heading), and whatever Bramble has learned.
  Then he waits. He will be here when they return.

  He is exposed. If the periapt was planted, an Acme cutter is
  at the margin. Fenn does not know about the periapt. Whether the player
  told him is on them.

What players can do here:
  - Use the mask as a negative reference. It pulls toward the pre-Alliance
    mark (northeast). Dale is northwest. Keep the pull to starboard.
    This is not obvious. The player has to figure it out.
  - Direct Bramble to navigate using what he observed. If he watched
    Fenn, he can hold the heading. If he didn't, he can try — but drift
    will be worse.
  - Ask Fenn anything they haven't asked yet. This is the last chance.

--- 5. DALE'S ANCHORAGE (fog interior, ~3 hours northwest of Fenn) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
Three structures on a low rock shelf, barely above the waterline. The
fog is thinner here, close to the rock. One lamp burning in the chart
house window. The mooring line has a specific knot pattern. Dale is
not here.

What players can do here:
  - Read the knot marker. It points to Dale's secondary position,
    ninety minutes further northwest. If they read it, they go direct.
    If they don't, they search — which costs time and may bring the
    Sinochia patrol into the window.
  - Search the anchorage. Dale left a second waterproofed packet here —
    a summary of her findings, one page, in case the primary records
    were lost. The player won't know it's there unless they look.
  - The Warden, if unresolved, followed them here. Dale will see it
    the moment they arrive. Her reaction: "You brought it. That's
    going to be a problem."

--- 6. DALE'S SECONDARY POSITION (ninety minutes further northwest) ---
BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
A smaller rock shelf. No structures. Dale in a small tender, waterproofed
case in her lap, watching the fog. She has been here for two days.

Dale — wants: to get out with the records intact. Not to be rescued —
to be extracted. There is a difference and she will make it clear.
Speaks in short sentences. Does not waste words. Looks at the masthead
before she looks at the player.

DALE: "You found the knot." (if they did)
DALE: "You didn't find the knot. How long did that cost us?" (if they didn't)

GM NOTE — IF THE PLAYER BOARDED THE MIREILLE AND FOUND THE CHARTS:
  Dale looks at the player. Then at the masthead. Then back at the player.
  DALE: "You found the Mireille."
  She goes quiet for a moment. Not grief — something more complicated.
  DALE: "I thought she was lost. How many of the crew are still alive?"
  Whatever the player answers, Dale nods once and does not ask again.
  She knew the Mireille. She sent them into the fog six weeks ago to
  verify a position she had charted. They did not come back on schedule.
  She has been carrying that since. The player just told her what happened
  to them. This changes the dynamic — Dale trusts the player faster,
  opens the waterproofed case sooner, and does not make them earn it
  the same way.

What players can do here:
  - Ask about the records. She will say: the fog is moving away from
    something. She knows roughly where. She doesn't know what.
  - Ask about the mask. She will say: it's been pulling northeast since
    they entered the fog. That's the direction the fog is moving away from.
  - Ask about the Warden. She will say: "Did you resolve it or is it
    still following you?" If still following: "Then we need to move now."
  - Ask if she knows anyone on the Sinochia patrol. She will say:
    "One person. I would prefer not to need them."
  - Ask about the Mireille (if they encountered it). See GM NOTE above.


================================================================================
THE MIREILLE — OPEN WATER ENCOUNTER (days 1-5)
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS:
  A Sinochia fog-runner — a fast, shallow-draft vessel built for the
  interior reef lines. She went into the fog six weeks ago and came back
  wrong. The crew is alive but changed. They are not hostile. They are
  not rational. They are trying to get back in.

  The fog did something to them. Now they cannot stop moving toward it.
  The player's vessel — specifically the mask — is the strongest signal
  of the fog they can sense outside the margin. They are following the
  mask home.

  They are not attacking. But they will not stop.
  They are not dead. But they are not right.
  They will do whatever it takes to stay close to the mask.

HOW IT APPEARS — DAY BY DAY:

  DAY 1 (subtle):
    A vessel on the horizon, same heading. Not unusual. Bramble notes it
    without comment if the player asks him what he sees. Fenn has seen it
    too. Neither says anything unless asked.

  DAY 2 (clearer):
    Still there. Same distance. Fenn adjusts course slightly — a small
    correction, nothing dramatic. The vessel adjusts with them. If the
    player asks Fenn about it: "It's been there since yesterday." He says
    it the way you say something you have been watching and have not yet
    decided what to do about. He does not adjust course again.

  DAY 3 (conflict begins):
    Fenn stops adjusting. He holds course and watches. The vessel holds
    with them. It is not a patrol — wrong flag, wrong silhouette. Through
    a glass: hull damage visible. A section of rail gone. A dark stain
    along the waterline that might be old water damage or might be
    something else. No crew visible on deck, but the vessel is sailing.
    Someone is working the lines.
    Fenn, if asked: "That is the Mireille. She is a Sinochia fog-runner.
    She went into the margin six weeks ago. She was not supposed to come
    back out."
    He pauses.
    "She should not be sailing."

  DAY 4 (threat):
    The Mireille closes distance overnight. By morning she is close enough
    to read without a glass. No colors flying. No response to signals.
    The hull damage is worse than it looked — the vessel should not be
    sailing. One crew member is visible at the bow, standing still,
    facing the player's vessel. Not watching the horizon. Watching the
    masthead.
    The mask's vibration has changed. Not the directional pull toward the
    pre-Alliance mark. Something reactive. Something that recognizes.

  DAY 5 (resolution — player must act or it acts for them):
    The Mireille is close enough that her bow wake is visible from the
    stern. If the player does nothing, she will make contact before the
    fog margin. See WHAT HAPPENS IF IGNORED below.

WHAT HAPPENS WHEN PLAYERS DO SOMETHING:

  FIRE AN ARROW:
    It lands. Someone on deck moves — not to take cover, not to react
    with alarm. They move to pull the arrow out of the wood and look at
    it. Then they look at the player's vessel. They do not change course.
    This is the first moment the players understand something is wrong
    with these people. Normal sailors react to being shot at.
    Fenn, watching: "Don't do that again."
    If asked why: "Because now they know we are paying attention."

  FIRE A CANNON:
    The shot hits. Damage is visible — a section of rail gone, a hole
    at the waterline. The Mireille lists slightly. She does not stop.
    She corrects. The crew moves to compensate — mechanically, without
    urgency, without looking at the damage. They are keeping the vessel
    functional because they need it functional to keep following.
    A second shot below the waterline will start sinking her.
    If she sinks, the crew goes into the water. They will swim toward
    the player's vessel. They will not stop swimming. This is now a
    different problem — see CREW IN THE WATER below.
    Fenn: "You have made this significantly more complicated."

  HAIL THEM / TRY TO COMMUNICATE:
    One person comes to the rail. They look at the player. They open
    their mouth. What comes out is not language — it is the sound of
    the fog. The low tone the mask makes when the Warden is near.
    They are broadcasting the same frequency as the mask.
    Fenn goes very still when he hears it.
    Fenn: "That is not a good sign."
    If asked what it means: "It means the fog is using them to talk
    to us. Or to the mask. I am not certain which."
    He does not say anything else.

  SEND A BIRD:
    The bird lands on the Mireille's mast. Nothing happens for a while.
    Then one of the crew picks it up. They do not send it back. They
    hold it. Twenty minutes later the bird returns on its own — not
    sent — with a piece of cloth tied to its leg. The cloth has a mark
    on it: the same pre-Alliance glyph as the mark on Daring's chart.
    The crew did not write this. They do not know they wrote this.
    The fog wrote it through them.
    Fenn looks at the cloth for a long time. He does not touch it.
    Fenn: "Keep that."

  SEND A DIVER:
    The diver goes under and comes up fast. Something is under the
    Mireille — not the hull, under the water beneath the hull. A shape.
    Large. Not the Warden — the Warden is in the fog. This is something
    the fog-runner vessel is carrying without knowing it. An impression
    left in the water. The diver cannot describe it clearly. They just
    know they did not want to be near it.
    The diver will not go back under. Do not push them on this.

  BOARD HER:
    The Mireille's crew does not resist. They do not help. They stand
    and watch. Their eyes track the player's movement but they do not
    speak, do not gesture, do not react to anything except proximity
    to the masthead — if the player gets close to the mast, the nearest
    crew member takes one step toward them. Not aggressive. Drawn.

    The hold: full of fog charts. Sinochia proprietary charts, the kind
    that do not leave Sinochia waters. All of them are wrong. The
    coastlines are right but the interior is mapped in a way that does
    not match any known geography. Someone has been charting something
    that should not be chartable — a location inside the fog that the
    fog itself does not want charted.

    One crew member, if the player makes eye contact and holds it, says
    one word: "Dale."

    Then they look away. They do not say it again.

    The charts are worth taking. The word "Dale" is worth more.
    If the player takes the charts and returns to their vessel, the
    Mireille holds position for one hour and then resumes following.
    The boarding did not resolve anything. It only gave the player
    information.

  LET THE MASK RESPOND (the correct resolution):
    Stop trying to control it. Hold position. Let the vibration do
    what it does. Do not cover the masthead. Do not run. Hold.
    The mask produces a low tone — not music, a frequency.
    The Mireille stops. The crew sits down on the deck, all of them,
    at the same time. They are asleep or something like it.
    The vessel drifts.
    After a few minutes the crew begins to stir. They are confused.
    Cold. They do not know where they are or how long they have been
    at sea. One of them is sick over the rail. Another is crying
    without knowing why.
    They are back. The fog let them go because the mask answered
    correctly.
    Fenn watches this happen with the expression of a man revising
    something he thought he understood.
    Fenn: "I did not know it could do that."

WHAT HAPPENS IF IGNORED (player does nothing by day 5):
    The Mireille closes until her bow is almost touching the player's
    stern. Fenn says: "We need to make a decision."
    If the player still does nothing, the vessels make contact. One
    of the Mireille's crew reaches across and touches the masthead.
    The mask produces the tone. The Mireille's crew sits down. They
    are asleep or something like it. The vessel drifts.
    This is the resolution — but the player did not choose it. It
    happened to them. Fenn saw what happened. He will remember that
    the mask resolved it without the player's input. He does not say
    this. He files it.

CREW IN THE WATER (if the Mireille is sunk):
    The crew swims toward the player's vessel. They will not stop.
    They are not aggressive — they are drawn. They want to be near
    the mask. If pulled aboard, they are passive, confused, and will
    not speak except to occasionally say "Dale" or make the fog tone.
    They cannot be left in the water — they will drown eventually,
    still swimming toward the vessel.
    The player now has to decide what to do with people who are alive
    but wrong, in open water, days from shore, with no way to explain
    what happened to them.
    There is no clean answer. Fenn will not tell the player what to do.
    He will say: "They are Sinochia nationals. Whatever you decide,
    decide it before we reach the margin. We cannot bring them into
    the fog."

THE PAYOFF — WHAT THE MIREILLE GIVES THE PLAYER:
    If the player boarded and found the charts and the word "Dale":
    They arrive at Location 3 already knowing Dale is real, already
    knowing the fog has been mapped from the inside, already one step
    ahead of what Fenn told them. The charts in the hold show a location
    inside the fog that the Mireille's crew was sent to verify. The
    location matches the pre-Alliance mark on Daring's chart.

    When they find Dale, she looks at the player. Then at the masthead.
    DALE: "You found the Mireille."
    She goes quiet. Not grief — something more complicated.
    DALE: "I thought she was lost. How many of the crew are still alive?"
    Whatever the player answers, Dale nods once and does not ask again.
    She sent the Mireille in six weeks ago to verify a position. They
    did not come back on schedule. She has been carrying that since.
    The player just told her what happened to them.
    This changes the dynamic. Dale trusts the player faster. She opens
    the waterproofed case sooner. She does not make them earn it the
    same way.

    If the player resolved the Mireille by letting the mask respond:
    The Warden already assessed the vessel via the Mireille before the
    fog margin. The fog entry is easier. See Location 3 GM NOTE above.


================================================================================
THE WARDEN — PERSISTENT ENCOUNTER (not a room, a presence)
================================================================================

BOLD / SENSORY / ACTION:
A shape slightly denser than the surrounding grey, moving at the edge
of visibility. The water surface disturbs slightly as something large
passes beneath the hull. The mask's vibration changes — less directional,
more reactive, like a dog that has heard something.

What it is: a function of the fog. It maintains the fog, tracks what
moves through it, investigates things that broadcast. The mask broadcasts.
The Warden has been following them since the margin.

What it does: circles. Does not attack. Investigates.

What the player can do — they have to figure this out:
  - Cover the masthead (canvas, rope, anything). Reduces the signal.
    Fenn will hint at this if asked how to make it stop: "Reduce what
    it is following."
  - Hold course and ignore it. It will eventually decide they are
    passing through. Takes twenty minutes of nerve. The hull rocks.
  - Let the mask respond. Don't muffle it, don't run, hold position.
    The mask produces a low tone. The Warden stops circling. This is
    the best outcome — the Warden will recognize the vessel on future
    voyages. Nobody aboard knows this is possible. The player discovers
    it by trying it. If they already used the mask tone on the Mireille,
    they know it works. They have the answer before the question is asked.

If ignored entirely: it follows them to the anchorage. Dale sees it.
The Sinochia patrol reads the Warden following a vessel as trespass.
The window shrinks.


================================================================================
THE RETURN — WHAT WAITS AT THE MARGIN
================================================================================

Dale navigates the return. She knows these waters. Faster than the
trip in.

IF THE PERIAPT WAS PLANTED:
  An Acme cutter is holding position just outside the fog margin.
  Three crew, two Shinyman Enforcers, one specialist (records handler,
  not a fighter). They want Dale and the records. They will offer to
  let the player go. They will not honor this offer.

  Telegraph the danger: as they approach the margin, the mask's
  vibration changes again — not the Warden, something else. Something
  metal and purposeful. Dale notices it too. "We have company outside."

  The cutter crew is tired and cold — they've been waiting a week.
  The specialist separates easily from the enforcers if the player
  is clever about it.

  Fenn has a Foundation signal flare. He has not mentioned it. He will
  use it without asking if the situation is bad enough. A Foundation
  vessel responds within four hours.

IF THE PERIAPT WAS NOT PLANTED:
  The margin is clear. Open water. Fenn sets course for the rendezvous.

Either way — Fenn, when Dale boards:
  FENN: "You look well."
  DALE: "I look like someone who spent three days in a fog anchorage.
  Let's go."


================================================================================
FAILING FORWARD
================================================================================

The story does not end if the player fails. It gets more complicated.

FAIL: Player doesn't take the contract.
  Dale waits her full window, then attempts to move on her own. She
  makes it to the margin and is taken by the Acme cutter. The records
  are destroyed. Dale is held. Hall sends another message — this time
  not a contract. A request. The player owes him one.

FAIL: Player loses Dale to the Acme cutter on the return.
  The Sinochia copies exist at the anchorage. The records are not
  fully destroyed. Dale is alive — Acme needs her to explain the
  records before they destroy them. There is a window to get her back.
  It is shorter than the first window.

FAIL: Player navigates badly and misses Dale entirely.
  Dale moves again. She leaves another marker. The player has to
  re-enter the fog. The Sinochia patrol has now logged an unknown
  vessel in their waters. The next entry is not clean.

FAIL: Player sinks the Mireille and cannot manage the crew.
  The Mireille's crew drowns. They were Sinochia nationals. Sinochia
  will find out — the fog knows what happened in its waters. On the
  return voyage, the Sinochia patrol is not cautious. It is hostile.
  Dale's contact on the patrol is not enough. The player has to fight
  their way out or run. Either way, Sinochia remembers this vessel.
  Future fog voyages will not be clean.


================================================================================
LOOT / REWARDS
================================================================================

THE MIREILLE'S CHARTS (found if boarded):
  Sinochia proprietary fog charts, all of them wrong in the same way.
  The coastlines are correct. The interior is mapped to a location that
  should not be chartable — a fixed point inside the fog that the fog
  itself does not want found. The location matches the pre-Alliance mark
  on Daring's chart. This is the source of the fog movement. This is
  where R242 points. The player now has independent confirmation from
  inside the fog before Dale tells them anything.

DALE'S SUMMARY PAGE (found at the anchorage if searched):
  One page, waterproofed. A year of fog movement compressed into
  a single diagram. The source marked with an X. No label. No
  coordinates. Just a location relative to the current margin.
  Useful. Incomplete. Enough to know where to go next.

THE WARDEN'S RECOGNITION (if handled well):
  Not an object. A relationship. Future fog voyages: the Warden
  does not investigate the vessel. Passage is faster, quieter,
  and the Sinochia clans notice that the fog does not react to
  this ship the way it reacts to strangers.

DALE'S TRUST (earned over the return voyage):
  If the player handled the voyage competently — found the knot,
  managed the Warden, didn't panic — Dale will open the waterproofed
  case on the return and show them the records. Not describe them.
  Show them. This is the real reward: knowing what Acme is protecting,
  and why the pre-Alliance mark on Daring's chart matters.

  DALE: "The fog isn't moving randomly. It's moving away from something.
  Something that was here before the Alliance, before the fog, before
  any of this. And whatever it is — your mast knows what it is."

  If the player found the Mireille and boarded her, Dale says this
  differently:
  DALE: "The Mireille found it. That's what the charts show. That's
  what I sent them to verify. They found it and the fog didn't want
  them to leave with that knowledge. Your mast is the only reason
  they came back at all."
