Campaign Master Chronicle
From Traveller Core to Grounded High-Fantasy

The Global Lore: The 300-Year Lie

The players originate from an Alliance of 13 Kingdoms that collectively govern the largest continental landmass on the globe.

  • The Political Landscape: 10 kingdoms are actively Allied, 3 maintain strict Neutrality.
  • The Axis Threat: There are 3 separate, unhinged Axis Kingdoms on the fringes. They do not take prisoners; they take slaves.
  • The Core Conspiracy: 300 years ago, a world-ending event occurred. History books were aggressively purged of all foreign records. The population was told that they were the absolute sole survivors of the planet, and that the Great Barriers and Reefs harbor world-ending terrors capable of destroying anything they touch.
  • The Truth: While the Alliance advanced rapidly into an advanced civilization over the last three centuries, other civilized city-states survived across the globe. Now, world entities are "getting up to speed," activating, and altering the isolated reality of the world of islands.

The Story Beats

1. The Hired Trip (A to B)

The Setup: The players are a mercenary crew with a modest vessel (or hired to crew one). They are approached by a wealthy, insufferable noble relative they absolutely despise.

The Job: A deceptive "milk run." They are tasked to deliver a sealed leather diplomatic pouch containing what is claimed to be sensitive royal peace treaties, sailing from Port City A to Duchy B. It pays exceptionally well but appears to be an insultingly simple courier assignment.

2. Avoiding Onlookers (The "Jump Point")

The Secret Route: The flight liaison is the Port Master. He gives them a highly specific, restricted set of sailing coordinates. Instead of leaving out of the main harbor where spies can track their trajectory, they are ordered to sneak out at night and sail to a hidden, rocky cove 15 miles down the coast—The "Jump Point." From this specific coordinates, they can catch the edge of the Great Trade Current unseen by local pirates.

3. The 100D Boundary & The Lockdown

The Boundary: The "100D line" is the boundary where the coastal waters end and the deep ocean begins.

The Lockdown: As they reach the coordinates to catch the current, they see a massive royal blockade. The King has declared a Total Maritime Quarantine over the entire sector to hunt down a massive pirate fleet. No ships are allowed to enter the deep ocean current. All long-range carrier-pigeon networks and coastal signal towers are ordered to go completely dark (Sector Silence) so the pirates can't intercept military intelligence.

4. The Carrier Nashua & The Inspection

The Carrier: Waiting at the coordinates is the Nashua, a gargantuan, multi-deck Galleon Warship—a floating military base.

The Capture: The Nashua fires warning shots, intercepts the players' smaller vessel, and forces them to tie up alongside it. The players are ordered to board the warship. While the captain grill the players in the officer's quarters, royal guards secretly slip onto the players' ship to search their cargo hold for pirate contraband.

5. The Pirate Attack & The "Hull Breach"

The Raid: Suddenly, the pirate fleet launches a massive, coordinated surprise night attack on the Nashua. Swarms of pirate ships surround them. Fires break out everywhere.

The "Zero-G" Chaos: A pirate fireship rams the Nashua, causing a massive explosion that tears a gaping hole in the lower decks (the hull breach). The lower decks rapidly flood with rushing seawater and thick, choking smoke.

The Survival Gear: Instead of vacc suits, the players must grab heavy leather diving lungs/air bladders or just tie wet cloths over their faces to survive the smoke and toxic fumes as they fight through the tilted, drowning, chaotic corridors of the burning warship to get back to their ship.

6. The Mining Drill Escape & The Misjump

The Blocked Exit: They reach their ship, but the Nashua’s collapsing rigging and burning wooden debris have crashed down, trapping their vessel against the warship's hull.

The "Mining Drill": The players must use their ship's heavy, iron-tipped boarding ram or axes to aggressively hack, smash, and capstan-drive their way out of the burning debris while under fire from pirate archers.

The "Misjump": They successfully break free and flee into the dark storm to escape the pirates. Blindly cutting into the Great Trade Current during a raging tempest without a proper reading, their ship is violently swept away. The current throws them completely off course, damaging their rudder and breaking their mast. A week later, they wash up on a completely uncharted, hostile, forgotten continent—far away from their destination.

The Sabotaged Cargo & The Artifact

The trunk did not contain treaties. It held a crystalline, gelatinous life form that erupted with violent lightning attacks, fusing itself into the ship's masthead and seizing absolute control of their trajectory for an entire month, throwing them 12 weeks off course far beyond the Great Reefs.

Artifact: The Lignum Vitae Mermaid Mask

The entity's core has permanently hardened into a dense ironwood mermaid masthead. It can be detached and transferred to other ships.

  • The Perceived Bonus: Grants any ship it is attached to a staggering 50% speed increase over its class when rowed or sailed. While on board, the crew enjoys an unnatural, hyper-coordinated +20% competence bonus to all skills.
  • The Curse (Storm Magnetism): The mask acts as a literal lightning rod. In heavy weather, violent static arcs across the deck, threatening black powder supplies.
  • The Curse (Biological Drain): It aggressively drains surrounding vitality. Organic provisions rot instantly, and fresh water stored in standard wooden casks turns stagnant, slimy, and brackish in just 7 to 10 days.
  • The Tactical Paradox: To travel safely or preserve food/water normally, the crew must actively relax sails, slow rowing, or completely detach and box the mask in lead, sacrificing their blazing speed and skill bonuses.

Current Game State: The Vacant Island

The players and the surviving Nashua deckhands awaken on the shores of a mysterious, uncharted island. Their current vessel is heavily damaged but entirely salvageable using local materials and leather straps to bind the rudder.

The Logistical Challenge: Long-Term Voyage Survival

This is a massive tactical problem for the players to solve. If the Lignum Vitae mask aggressively accelerates decay, it fundamentally alters how they have to plan long-term maritime travel. They aren't locked out of 2-month voyages, but they can no longer rely on standard medieval provisions like salt pork and hardtack.

To survive a 2-month sail with the artifact on board, the crew will have to adopt new, highly active survival strategies:

1. Active Foraging and "Island Hopping"

Instead of sailing in a straight line across open ocean, a 2-month voyage becomes a series of tactical pitstops.

  • They must chart courses that hug coastlines, barrier reefs, or archipelagos.
  • Every 5 to 7 days, they must drop anchor at an uncharted island, send out hunting parties, gather fresh fruit, and replenish their water casks before the artifact can rot them.

2. Radical Food & Water Storage Solutions

Standard barrels won't cut it. The players and their Nashua ship workers will need to get creative:

  • The Towing Method: They can't keep livestock or large food crates on deck. They might have to construct a small, streamlined cargo raft to tow 50 feet behind the sloop. Keeping the bulk of their food and water out of the artifact's immediate "aura" preserves it normally, though it risks being lost in a heavy storm.
  • Lead/Iron Lining: If they can salvage enough lead sheeting or iron plates from the island's workshops, they can build a heavily insulated, air-tight "cold box" in the hold to shield the provisions from the mask's biological drain. Earthenware ceramic jugs or glass carboys from the outpost can similarly block the drain on fresh water.
  • Magical Preservation: If the party has a spellcaster, low-level spells like Purify Food and Drink or Gentle Repose suddenly become the most critical utility spells in the game, forcing them to burn spell slots every single day just to keep the crew fed.

3. Living Off the Sea

The crew will have to pivot completely to a real-time seafood diet and dynamic water collection.

  • They must constantly run fishing lines off the back of the sloop and harvest rain-catch water using canvas deck arrays.
  • The Twist: Because the ship moves at an unnatural 50% extra speed, standard fishing is much harder—the lines will snap or drag too fast. They will need to use their +20% skill bonus to engineer heavy-duty trolling lines or use harpoons to catch giant reef fish and sea turtles while moving at high speeds.

4. The Nuclear Option: Detaching the Mask

Because the mask can be moved, the players have the ultimate tactical choice. They can detach the mask and store it in a lead box during the long, boring stretches of the journey to preserve their food and water normally, and only mount it to the masthead when they need to outrun a pirate fleet, dodge a storm, or make a high-speed tactical sprint.

Water Hazard: The Race Against Dehydration

Yes, the masthead absolutely affects fresh water.

Just as it withers organic food, its biological drain causes fresh water stored in standard wooden casks to turn stagnant, slimy, or brackish twice as fast. In a standard fantasy setting, water sitting in a barrel already starts going bad after a few weeks; under the influence of the Lignum Vitae mask, a month’s worth of water will become undrinkable in just 7 to 10 days.

This turns a 2-month sail into a deadly race against dehydration. To survive long-distance voyages without dying of thirst, the crew has to exploit the environment or use specific workarounds:

How to Get and Keep Water on a 2-Month Sail

  • The Towing Strategy (with a twist): Just like food, water casks can be loaded onto a towed raft 50 feet behind the sloop to keep them outside the mask's immediate aura. However, water is incredibly heavy. Towing tons of water drags on the sloop, meaning they might have to sacrifice some of that 50% speed bonus to keep their water safe.
  • The Rain-Catch Array: Since they are sailing through a world plagued by unnatural storms (driven by the mask's Storm Magnetism), they can turn a curse into a cure. By rigging wide canvas tarps across the deck to funnel rainwater directly into clean buckets, they can constantly replenish their supply with fresh, untainted water.
  • Ceramic or Glass Storage: The biological drain easily bleeds through organic wooden barrels. If the players can scavenge earthenware ceramic jugs, stone jars, or glass carboys from the vacant island's workshop or food stores, the non-organic barriers will shield the water from rotting quite as fast.
  • Distillation/Desalination: If they have copper piping or iron pots from the workshop, the Nashua ship workers can build a crude stills system over the ship's galley fire. By boiling seawater and catching the steam, they can produce small amounts of emergency fresh water every day, though it consumes precious firewood.

The Core Tactical Choice

Because the mask can be detached, the players are forced to make a brilliant risk-vs-reward choice every single day on the open sea:

  1. Mount the Mask: Travel at blazing speeds with a +20% skill bonus, but your food rots, your water spoils, and you risk attracting a massive lightning storm.
  2. Box the Mask: Slow down to normal speeds and lose the skill bonus, but your remaining provisions and water stay fresh normally.

The Scenario: As the players prepare to leave the island for the very first time, they spot sails on the horizon. A fast-moving privateer vessel flying the colors of a Neutral Alliance city is racing directly toward the outpost.

The Mission: The privateers cannot be allowed to secure any information, technology, or artifacts from the island. The Naval Clash: Following the destruction of the island, the players engage the privateers in high-stakes combat. The crew must destroy the hostile forces. However, if they play their cards right during the boarding action, they have the tactical opportunity to capture and save the pirate boat for their own fleet.

Part 2: Repairs and the Signal Flare

The Scenario: Following the intense battle, the crew drops anchor to execute vital repairs on their vessel (or their newly captured prize ship).

The Discovery: While fixing the hull and rigging, the lookout spots a faint shimmer through the ocean mist. Somewhere in the next sector, sitting just past the edge of the known barrier charts, lies a mysterious island featuring a towering stone signal structure—its bright light lit and burning against the dark sky.

Adventure 2 Point of Interest: "Itsy Bitsy Spider"

The History of the Island

This small, isolated reef island features the stone signal tower spotted by the lookout. Originally, it was a classified research outpost operated by the Recurrent Foundation of the Hand, dedicated to breeding enhanced critters to protect their regional crops. The project was highly successful, after which the Foundation abandoned the facility, locked it down, and scrubbed its existence from all official knowledge base records. However, the immense proximity power of the players' newly mounted Lignum Vitae mask has accidentally tripped the grid, reactivating the tower's bright light.

The Acme Takeover

What happened since it was abandoned? Dr. Zephyr (Z-fold) discovered the lost coordinates of the island buried deep within ancient, forgotten city archives. Delighted to find a hidden base nestled safely within the deadly Great Reefs, he immediately bought the location. He replaced the Foundation's original scientific equipment with his own Acme gear, piggybacking off the existing arachnid research by introducing rare, exotic herbs harvested directly from the surrounding reefs.

The Experiment Gone Wrong

The experiment took a horrific turn. The introduction of the reef herbs caused the spiders to mutate and grow to monstrous, unnatural sizes. They broke containment, escaped into the facility, and aggressively slaughtered and ate every single one of their Acme captors. The final head scientist died while scrambling to write a desperate, frantic note outlining how to kill them: a concentrated phosphorus solution must be forced directly into the island's primary water supply so the creatures ingest it and die from the inside. He was violently eaten just as he penned the final word.

Tactical Environment: The Twilight Landing

The Weakness: The mutated spiders absolutely detest and flee from bright light, making the newly active signal tower the only safe haven on the island. This creates the perfect opportunity for a high-stakes Twilight Run. The GM should land the crew on the beach just as the sun dips below the horizon, forcing them to fight through the shadows to reach the bunker and the water supply controls before total darkness falls.

1. Chapter 3 Hook: The Reef-Spider Infestation

Mechanics of the Non-Giant Spider Poison

During Chapter 2, two crew members (or players) were bitten by small, aggressive reef-spiders. While not instantly fatal, the venom induces a slow, systemic degeneration of the nervous system.

  • 1E AD&D Saving Throw: Initial bite required a Save vs. Poison at +2. Failure inflicts the *Slow-Rot Fever*.
  • Degradation Effect: Every 3 days of travel, an infected crew member must roll 1d6. They lose 1 point from a corresponding ability score (1=STR, 2=DEX, 3=CON, 4=INT, 5=WIS, 6=CHA). If any score hits 0, the character falls into a permanent coma.
  • The Remedy: Standard Neutralize Poison spells are warped by the Great Barrier's background energies and only pause the degradation for 24 hours. A permanent cure requires a rare compound brewed from the *Ghost-Prickly Cactus* native only to Sundown Atoll.

2. Frontier Settlement: Sundown Atoll

Island Profile & Aesthetic

Sundown Atoll is a massive, self-sufficient desert island locked inside a dead ring of the barrier reef. It features a stark, sun-baked landscape with a sprawling, Western-frontier-style town built from white coral-brick, sun-cured timber, and sandstone blocks.

  • Architecture: Long wooden boardwalks, swinging saloon doors, high-front facades, a central dusty thoroughfare, and massive canvas-and-wood wind-pumps drawing brackish water from underground aquifers.
  • Self-Sufficiency: The island operates completely independently of the 13 Kingdoms. They survive on deep-well irrigation farming, rock-salt mining, and harvesting thick-hided sea reptiles. They have a functioning blacksmith, an apothecary (holding the poison remedy), and a heavily armed local militia.

3. The "Shinymen" Threat Profiles (Select One for Your Table)

Sundown Atoll is heavily plagued by a faction known to the locals as the Shinymen. Here are three 1E-compliant options explaining why they are a massive problem:

Option A: Acme's Sun-Shield Privateers (Bounty Hunters)

The Lore: These are high-tier mercenary enforcers hired covertly by Dr. Zephyr's Acme Corporation. To survive the blinding, white-hot glare of the Barrier reefs, they wear specialized, mirror-polished steel plate mail. They are a problem because they extort the town, block trade routes, and are hunting for the crew that stole the Lignum Vitae mask.

Option B: Crystalline Skinned Warlords (The Reef Mutants)

The Lore: Driven out into the island's salt-flats, these are miners who mutated due to exposing themselves to an unnatural, glowing mineral vein. Their flesh has partially bonded with glittering silver ore, making their bodies shine brilliantly under the sun. They regularly raid the town's food storehouses because they can no longer digest regular crops—they crave raw sulfur and iron.

Option C: Ancient Brass Automatons (The Clockwork Menace)

The Lore: Polished brass, clockwork constructs left behind by a civilization that predates the 300-year great lie. Because the power of the players' Lignum Vitae masthead recently reactivated regional signals, these machines have woken up from their desert vaults. They march into town on a fixed, unprogrammed path, dismantling buildings and slaughtering livestock to harvest resources for a master forge.

Chapter 3 Mission: The Canyon Contract

The Logistical Crisis

Will the players find the anti-poison? Yes, they will absolutely find the antidote, but it is not sitting on a shelf waiting for them. The apothecary is clean out of the raw material needed to brew it.

The Mission Beyond the Cure

The players discover that Sundown Atoll’s self-sufficiency is under direct threat. The Shinymen are actively choking the island out. They have established a fortified position at the Rust-Canyon Wellhead (the island's only pure water supply) and are intercepting the town’s deep-well irrigation pipelines.

The town marshal is facing a losing siege. He has the apothecary’s recipe for the anti-poison, but the final, vital ingredient—the sap of the Ghost-Prickly Cactus—only grows inside the deep, dry ravines currently occupied by the Shinymen.

What the Group Must Do to Trade for It

The Marshal doesn't want their gold; he needs their specialized tactical capabilities. To get the antidote ingredients and save their crew mates, the players must complete a two-part contract:

  • Objective 1: Defend the Warehouse District (Defense): The Shinymen are planning a night raid on the town’s central storage vaults to steal their rock-salt and dried rations. The players must use their +20% skill bonus and tactical experience to help the local militia set up an ambush and repel the attackers. Leveraging their shipmate skill attunement bonus (applicable when coordinating defense lines near the harbor docks/coastal infrastructure) is vital to fortify the sector and eliminate the raiding party.
  • Objective 2: Reclaim the Wellhead / The Rust-Canyon Run (Offense): Once the town is secure, the players must lead a counter-strike into the canyon desert badlands to break the Shinymen’s blockade on the water supply. They must push through the ravines and clear the outpost guarding the irrigation valves. While clearing out the camp, they can safely harvest the Ghost-Prickly Cactus sap required by the apothecary and return it to cure their two degenerating crew members.

Tactical Analysis: Player Advantages

The town militia has numbers, but they are terrified, pinned down, and lack critical capabilities that your two players and their ship possess. While the party consists of only two primary player characters and a crew of non-combat-proficient Nashua deckhands, they bring game-changing assets to Sundown Atoll that the local militia completely lacks:

  • 1. The Power of the Masthead (+20% Skill Attunement Aura): The local militia are commoners with standard, clumsy combat skills. However, because your players' sloop is tied up at the town docks, the Lignum Vitae Mermaid Mask is radiating its magical aura across the immediate coastal front. While within range of the ship (the Warehouse District right by the water), your players and any militia members fighting alongside them gain a +20% competence bonus to all non-combat skills (such as setting mechanical traps, barricading doors, spotting stealthy raiders, and engineering defensive lines). The players can use this unnatural coordination to flawlessly engineer barricades, rig traps, and transform a disorganized rabble of townspeople into a perfectly synchronized, hyper-efficient defense force.
  • 2. High-Grade Mobile Artillery (The Sloop's Cannons): The town's defenses are entirely static and can't cover every angle. Your players bring a highly historically accurate Reinforced Cargo Sloop with 2 Mounted Iron Cannons. The town warehouses are built right along the waterfront thoroughfare. Your players can pivot their sloop in the harbor channels/bays and use their cannons to provide devastating naval fire support directly into the flanks of the Shinymen raiders during the midnight warehouse assault. When the Shinymen launch their midnight raid on the seaside warehouses, the players can unleash grape-shot or solid cannonballs right into the enemy's flanks from the safety of the water. The town militia simply doesn't have that kind of mobile, heavy black-powder artillery.
  • 3. Pure Tactical Heroics (The PCs vs. Non-Combat Crew): The 4 Nashua deckhands (Thorn, Bramble, Pip, Grip) are excellent ship workers, but they are commoners who panic or lack high-tier combat proficiency. The town militia are just farmers and miners who line up and shoot bows blindly. Backed by the +1 mundane weapons and +1 armor scavenged from the Foundation outpost, your two main characters are the elite tactical pieces. They possess the precision tactical capabilities to slip behind enemy lines, execute surgical strikes against the Shinymen leadership, and deploy their advanced gear. They have the precision to win an asymmetric guerrilla war in the canyons where a clumsy, terrified militia would just get slaughtered in an open field.
