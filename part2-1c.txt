================================================================================
PART 2 — PLAYER SCENE: FIRST LIGHT
The Condition. The Answer. Departure.
================================================================================

The harbor is different before dawn.

The gulls are quieter. The chandler's row is dark. The fish market is
being set up rather than torn down — crates arriving, cobblestones being
hosed, the particular smell of a place being made ready for the day rather
than recovering from it. The pastry stand is not open yet. You note this
without sentiment and keep walking.

The dock entrance arch is staffed by a different officer than yesterday.
He looks at you, looks at your crew, looks at the papers you hold up
without being asked. He writes something in the logbook. You keep moving.

The far pier is quiet. The water between the planks is black and still.
The join between old stone and new stone is invisible in the dark — you
know it is there but you cannot see it. The thing beneath is down there
regardless.

Hound's Dream is lit by a single lamp at the stern. Fenn is on deck.
He was there before you arrived. You don't know how long he has been
standing there but you suspect it has been a while.

He looks at your crew. He looks at your papers. He looks at you.

FENN: "You came."

He says it the way you say something you were not entirely certain of.

You tell him you came with a condition.

He is quiet for a moment. The lamp moves slightly in the harbor air.

FENN: "Go ahead."

You tell him you want to know what kept him in Liege for three months.
Not a summary. The actual reason. Before you step aboard with your crew.

He looks at you for a long moment. That cataloguing look. Filing something.

Then he sits down on the rail — not defensively, just settling — and he
tells you.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FENN'S ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FENN: "Wendy Dale was supposed to be out of the fog margin by the end
of last autumn. She wasn't. I received a message in October — indirect,
three relays, the way Foundation communications work — that her extraction
window had closed. Sinochia naval activity in the outer margin. Two of
their patrol vessels had started running a new route that cut directly
across the approach corridor I had planned. Sinochia doesn't want her
leaving with what she knows — the fog is theirs and they intend to keep
it that way."

He looks at the water.

FENN: "I couldn't leave. If I sailed back to report and the window
reopened, there would be no one here to move. Hall and I agreed I would
hold position until the situation changed or until he sent me an asset
worth using."

He pauses.

FENN: "The Sinochia patrol route changed six weeks ago. Shifted north —
I don't know why, I don't have sources inside their naval command. But
the corridor opened back up. I sent word to Hall. You arrived eleven
days later. That is the three months."

He looks at you directly.

FENN: "You want to know if anyone else knew I was here. That is the
question underneath the question."

He doesn't wait for you to confirm it.

FENN: "The harbor authority logged me when I arrived. That is public
record — anyone with access to the naval registry can see that Hound's
Dream has been berthed on the far pier since September. I did not hide
my presence. Hiding draws more attention than staying still in a city
this size. What I did not do is tell anyone why I was here or how long
I intended to stay."

He stands back up.

FENN: "If Acme's people have been watching this pier, they have seen
me. I am aware of that. I have been aware of it for six weeks. It is
one of the reasons I wanted you moving before full light — the longer
we sit here the more time anyone watching has to make a decision about
whether to act."

He looks at your crew again. Then back at you.

FENN: "That is the actual reason. You can decide what it is worth."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE DECISION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You stand there for a moment.

The answer has texture. Specific months. A patrol route change with no
clean explanation. The acknowledgment that he is visible and has been
visible and knows it. He did not get defensive. He did not get smooth.
He told you the thing underneath the thing before you asked it.

That is either a man with nothing to hide or a man who has been
preparing this answer for three months.

You look at your crew. Thorn, Bramble, Pip, Grip. They are ready.
They have been ready since before you walked down here. They are
watching you the way crew watches a captain at a decision point —
not pushing, not pulling, just present.

You look at the lamp on Hound's Dream's stern.

You look at the harbor entrance arch, just visible at the end of the
pier. The officer at the booth is writing in his logbook. In a few
hours Harwick Dray will arrive at the middle pier with his clipboard
and work his way toward the far pier and find your vessel and begin
the secondary inspection process that will cost you time you do not
have.

You look at Fenn.

You step aboard.

FENN: "Good."

He says it quietly, without satisfaction, the way you say something
that needed to happen and now has. He moves to the bow without
ceremony. His hands are already on the lines.

FENN: "Get your crew to stations. We leave on the tide."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DEPARTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The harbor slides past in the dark.

You clear the entrance arch with twenty minutes before first light.
The officer in the booth watches you go. He writes something in the
logbook. You are already past caring what it says.

The city of Liege recedes behind you — pale stone, salt-stained,
the color of old teeth. The three flags on the customs house are
invisible in the pre-dawn grey. The fish market is beginning to
make noise. The pastry stand is not open yet.

Somewhere on the middle pier, Mira Solt is either watching you leave
or she is not. The Brothers Kael are either noting your departure or
they are not. Callo Vesh's carrier pigeon is either already in the
air or it will be shortly.

None of that is your problem right now.

Your problem right now is the water ahead, which is dark and wide
and moving north, and Fenn at the bow reading it the way a man reads
something he has read many times before, and the masthead above you
which is glowing very faintly in the pre-dawn dark — not enough for
anyone on shore to see, probably, but enough for you to notice.

The mask knows you are moving.

You are not sure yet whether that is good.

Fenn said he would give you Wendy Dale's position once you were
underway. You are underway.

You go forward to ask him.

END OF SCENE
